## Verdict: INCOMPLETE

The source proves the all-finite-dimensional alternating-vertex / nested-integral identity and the
required nonzero product witness for globally `C^r` functions. The ticket asks for functions that
are only `C^r` on an open neighborhood of the unit cube. The current theorem assumes global
`ContDiff ℝ r f`, which is stronger; it does not establish the full local-regularity scope. This is
an interface-strength mismatch, not a gap in the proved induction for its stated hypothesis.

## Formalized statement

In `RBM.Gauss`, `alternatingVertexSum` is defined recursively by the upper first-coordinate face
minus the lower first-coordinate face. This gives the coefficient
`(-1)^(r - number_of_upper_coordinates)` at each Boolean vertex. `iteratedUnitIntegral` is the
nested integral over `[0,1]^r`, integrating coordinate `0` first and then the remaining
coordinates. `cubeMixedDerivative` evaluates `iteratedFDeriv ℝ r` on
`fun i => Pi.single i 1`, so each of the `r` directions is a distinct coordinate unit vector.

The proved all-order theorem is:

```lean
alternatingVertexSum_eq_iteratedUnitIntegral
  (f : (Fin r → ℝ) → ℝ) (hf : ContDiff ℝ r f) :
  alternatingVertexSum r (fun v => f (fun i => if v i then 1 else 0)) =
    iteratedUnitIntegral r (cubeMixedDerivative f)
```

The proof inducts on `r`. It restricts to the two first-coordinate faces, applies the induction
hypothesis on each face, combines their nested integrals by linearity, and applies
`intervalIntegral.integral_deriv_eq_sub` in the first coordinate. The slice derivative is
identified with the ambient iterated derivative evaluated on the shifted coordinate unit vectors.
Dimension zero reduces to evaluation at the unique empty vertex with no integration variables;
dimension one is the single upper-minus-lower FTC step. The proof introduces no premise for the
r-fold identity.

The witness theorem `cubeFTC_coordinateProduct_witness` proves, for every `r`, that
`g(t) = ∏ i : Fin r, t i` has alternating vertex sum `1`, full mixed derivative identically `1`,
and nested integral `1`. This includes the empty product in dimension zero.

This result is only the deterministic finite-box calculus arrow. The prescribed manuscript formula
(4.12) is a weighted fluctuation-averaging estimate; this module makes no claim about that random
estimate or its probabilistic hypotheses.
