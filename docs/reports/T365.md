## MVT and strict transition

The private real-variable lemma `exists_strict_transition` applies the mean-value theorem to a C¹ function `f` with `f(0)=1`, `f(b)=0`, `b>0`, and `0≤f≤1` everywhere. It supplies `x∈(0,b)` satisfying

\[
 f'(x)=\frac{f(b)-f(0)}{b}=-\frac1b\ne0.
\]

To show `0<f(x)<1`, suppose `f(x)=0`. The global lower bound makes `x` a local minimum, so Fermat's theorem gives `f'(x)=0`, a contradiction. If `f(x)=1`, the global upper bound similarly makes it a local maximum. This proves the strict weight inequalities at the very same MVT point; it does not assume monotonicity of the ray or choose a separate transition point.

`transition_of_endpoints` applies this argument to the actual ray with `b=2/√h`. The global range bounds are T333's unconditional `weight_nonneg` and `weight_le_one`. Its conclusion includes all of

\[
 0<x<2/\sqrt h,\qquad 0<f(x)<1,\qquad
 f'(x)=-1/(2/\sqrt h),\qquad f'(x)\ne0.
\]

This derivative is the derivative along the actual scalar-sample ray. No covariance seminorm is substituted for it.
