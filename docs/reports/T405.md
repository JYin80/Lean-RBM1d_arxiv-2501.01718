# T405 - independent audit of the full mathematical A-prime implication

## Normalized claim and verdict

**Primary verdict: proved as written.** This verdict concerns the following
mathematical implication, including its original initial-data hypotheses. It is
not a claim that the corresponding Lean model witness has been implemented.

Fix an actual Gaussian model `Gauss.sample d`, a fixed bulk energy `E`, a fixed
`D >= 60`, and deterministic sequences `0 <= s_N <= t_N < 1`. Assume
`BoundsCore (sample d) E s`, `Cond272Reg (band d) E s t c` for some `c > 0`, and
the pre-A-prime Step-1 inputs on this window. Then one can choose an
order-independent prefix weight, a polynomial mesh, and a fixed measurable
high-probability modulus event so that the literal `WeightedMoment` holds for
`Step2Moment.jSnorm`, with `lev = Theta = 1`. Consequently the mathematical
fields of `APrimeSlot'` exist and

```text
jSnorm_N(u) = J_loop,N(u) / (eta_s / eta_u)^4  prec  1,
```

uniformly for all real `u in [s_N,t_N]`. The quantifiers are those of
`Step2Bootstrap.WeightedMoment`: for every sufficiently small fixed positive
`delta` and every fixed moment order, an eventual threshold exists **before**
the universal mesh-index quantifier.

The label “paper (2.71)” sometimes attached to this work is inaccurate. In the
authorized PDF, (2.71) is an expectation bound for two-loops. The first-pass
tail conclusion audited here is (2.76). This report does not infer (2.71) from
A-prime.

No additional stochastic estimate is assumed in this implication. The
deterministic-scale fluctuation average used below has an internal proof. The
stronger random-`Lmax` version of paper (4.5) is not used. In particular, the
proof does not need an unconditional sharp bound for `jG` on every late
window.

Two implementation/scope boundaries are substantive:

* At the accepted checkpoint, the actual model `hfamily` and complete general
  moving-window `APrimeSlot'` are not supplied by a compiled theorem. The
  existing `aprimeSlot_of_widened_family` additionally assumes one fixed
  `t0 < 1`; it cannot be invoked on arbitrary late moving windows unchanged.
* A-prime does not itself supply the next macro-cell's `BoundsCore`. Its
  output contains the factor `(eta_s/eta_t)^4`; Steps 3--5, or another proof of
  the original next-cell initial data, are needed for that handoff. This is
  distinct from the finite mesh induction **inside** one window, which closes
  here. The claim being audited explicitly assumes `BoundsCore` at the left
  endpoint of each window.

## 3. Scales, observables, and quantifiers

For a fixed `N` and arbitrary endpoint `v in [s,t]`, write

```text
m0 = Im m(E),  eta_u = m0(1-u),  A_u = W ell_u eta_u,
r_u = ell_u/ell_s,  x_u = eta_s/eta_u,  R = eta_s/eta_v,  A = A_v.
```

Monotonicity and the original hypotheses give, uniformly in `s <= u <= v <= t`,

```text
1 <= r_u <= sqrt(x_u),  1 <= x_u <= R,
N^c <= A_t <= A <= A_u <= N,  R^30 <= A,
WL <= N <= W^2,  eta_u >= N^(-1),  W <= N.
```

If needed replace `c` by `min(c,1/2)` before selecting tolerances; this only
uses a weaker consequence of the same hypothesis. No bound on `R` independent
of `N` is assumed. In particular `s_N` may approach zero, `t_N` may approach one,
and `t_N-s_N` may be shorter than one mesh interval.

`J_loop = 1 + max |L-K|/T` and `J_block = jG` are not identical.
`gsqBlk(x,y)` includes the neighbor maximum in the **first** block label and
both resolvent charges; its far denominator is the literal
`A_u^(-2) exp(-sqrt(d/ell_u)) + W^(-D)`. All uses of `J_block` below respect
that neighbor and floor. In particular, the diagonal behavior of `J_loop` at
`X=0` is not a counterexample to a bound for far `J_block`.

For one coordinate `a` the evolved variable is

```text
Y_u(a) = [U_(u,v)(L-K)_u]_a / [T_v(a) R^4].
```

Its endpoint denominator is fixed during the `u` differentiation. The drift
is `U_(u,v)(eGpm+quadGlue)` divided by this denominator. Its QV is the full
uncut Gaussian covariance of this very coordinate, divided by its square.
No near-only rate, indicator-weighted rate, or unevolved rate is substituted.

The order of choices can be made as follows:

1. Fix model, energy, `D`, window, and the original `c`.
2. Fix the modulus event, a polynomial mesh and the smoothing order.
3. Fix `delta`, the requested moment `p`, and the larger fixed moment `P` used

## 4. Independent check of deterministic averaging

Step 1 at length two and the weak-law removal of the indicator in (4.2)/(4.3)
give the entry control with the deterministic scale

```text
Phi_u = r_u/A_u + W^(-1),  Psi_u = sqrt(Phi_u).
```

Since `ell_s eta_u <= 1`,

```text
W^(-1) <= r_u/A_u,  W^(-1/2) <= Psi_u,
Phi_u <= 2 A_u^(-59/60) <= 2 N^(-59c/60).
```

Thus `Psi` is both polynomially bounded below and polynomially small. This
is not the random loop maximum appearing in the printed version of (4.12).

Here is the crucial cancellation check. Define `P_i` by integrating the
independent underlying coordinates incident to row/column `i`; let `Q_i=1-P_i`.
These projections commute, even though two rows share their connecting edge:
on a product probability space, repeated integration of the shared coordinate
is idempotent. For a finite set `U` not containing `i`, set

```text
Delta_U v_i = sum_(S subset U) (-1)^|S| (G_ii^S-m).
```

The rank-one minor identity and its discrete product rule imply, at any fixed
finite deletion depth,

```text
norm_(L^q)(Delta_U v_i) <= C N^epsilon Psi^(|U|+1).       (A1)
```

For one deletion the numerator has two off-diagonal Green entries. Each new
distinct deletion either splits an off-diagonal entry into two, gaining one
such factor, or differentiates an inverse diagonal, gaining two. Hence every
term has at least `|U|+1` off-diagonal factors. On the entry event all finitely
deleted minor diagonals stay separated from zero, since `|m|=1` and `Psi=o(1)`.
This establishes the favorable-event bound, including all finite deletion
sets, by deterministic successive minor identities.

For the complement, use the all-sample resolvent bound and

```text
|1/G_ii^S| <= |H_ii| + |z_u| + eta_t^(-1) sum_j |H_ij|^2.
```

Every fixed product has polynomial moments. Holder and an arbitrarily high
failure power establish (A1) in ordinary `L^q`. No event indicator is placed
inside the conditional cancellation.

Expand the `2P`-th moment of `sum_i t_i Q_i(G_ii-m)`, for deterministic weights
`max |t_i| <= W^(-1)` and `sum |t_i| <= 1`. In one equality partition let `r0`
be the number of distinct labels and `a` the number of singleton labels. For
each singleton `j`, insert `P_j+Q_j` on all other factors. The branch with only
`P_j` on those factors vanishes: the singleton factor still contains `Q_j`,
and all other factors are `P_j`-measurable. Commutation preserves this argument
after earlier expansions. Each surviving term therefore has at least `a`
additional `Q` letters, each on a factor with a different principal label.

For the extra set `U` on a factor,

```text
(product_(j in U) Q_j) v_i = (product_(j in U) Q_j) Delta_U v_i.
```

Any nonempty deleted term is annihilated by a `Q_j` from its deletion set.
Projection operator norms cost only a constant depending on fixed `P`. By
(A1), a joint moment is bounded by `C_P N^epsilon Psi^(2P+a)`. The coefficient
mass of the equality partition is at most `W^(-(2P-r0))`. Since
`a >= 2r0-2P` and `W^(-1) <= Psi^2`, the result is at most
`C_P N^epsilon Psi^(4P)`. There are finitely many partitions and projection
branches at fixed `P`.

This proves the needed centered average at scale `Phi`. It applies to the
finitely many deterministic `S` rows and block averaging weights. It is not an
assertion uniform over sample-dependent coefficients.

For the uncentered average put `v_i=G_ii-m`, `x_i=P_i v_i`, `z_i=Q_i v_i`.
Gaussian IBP gives exactly

```text
x_i = u m sum_k S_ik P_i[G_ii v_k].
```

The product of two centered diagonals costs `Phi`. For `k != i`, the minor
identity gives `P_i v_k-v_k = O_prec(Phi)`. For `k=i` that bound is not true in
the same form; the error is `-z_i=O_prec(Psi)`. The required sum nevertheless
has the diagonal factor `S_ii <= W^(-1)`, and
`W^(-1) Psi <= Phi`. Thus

```text
x - u m^2 S v = O_prec(Phi).
```

The centered estimate controls `S z`; the actual max-to-max bulk stability of
`I-u m^2 S` then gives `x=O_prec(Phi)`. Centered block averaging proves

```text
max_a |W^(-1) sum_(i in I_a) (G_ii-m)| prec Phi.          (A2)
```

Both charges follow by conjugation at the same Hermitian sample. This proves
the exact needed averaging statement internally; citing [40] outside its
literal domain is unnecessary.

For simultaneous time, the resolvent identity gives a polynomial
half-Holder modulus on the norm event. Conditional row integrations have the
same form of bound by integrating the resampled row's polynomial norm
envelope. `Phi` has a polynomial modulus and a polynomial positive floor.
Choose a polynomial time net first, the fixed moment afterward, and then its
entry losses and complement powers. A polynomial union and interpolation
produce the all-time event. At `u=0`, `G_0=mI` and the centered terms vanish.

## 5. Running support, moving endpoints, and a fixed slot event

The literal current smooth threshold is

```text
Theta_N = 8 e^2 N^(2 delta),  c_k = chi(S_k/Theta_N).
```

The prefix contains the actual normalized loops at **all** `j<k`, on the same
Gaussian sample. Positive active weight gives an upper bound
`16 e^2 N^(2 delta)` on each of their `jSnorm` values. On the norm/modulus
event this extends through the preceding mesh interval to every
`u in [s,u_k]`, including `u_k`. At the right endpoint use `j=k-1`; it is
strictly earlier. This argument does not presume the conclusion at `u_k`.

The general moving-window modulus is available without a fixed terminal
`t0<1`. For completeness, a coarse bound suffices. On `{norm X<=N}`,

```text
norm(G_u-G_w) <= 2 N^3 |u-w|^(1/2),
norm G_u <= N,  T_u >= N^(-D).
```

The actual two-loop numerator has a polynomial half-Holder modulus. The
primitive two-loop resolvent has a polynomial time derivative. Moreover
`ell_u=min(L,(1-u)^(-1/2))` has a polynomial Lipschitz constant, including its
saturation point; `A_u>=1`; and the derivative with respect to `ell` of
`exp(-sqrt(d/ell))` is uniformly bounded when `ell>=1`. For example these
elementary estimates yield, after absorbing fixed constants,

```text
|jSnorm(u)-jSnorm(w)| <= N^(2D+7) |u-w|^(1/2).           (M)
```

One may take `mesh_N=N^(4D+18)` eventually, with interpolation error at most
`N^(-2)`. Small `N` can be given a positive mesh arbitrarily. This is a fixed
mesh, independent of `delta` and the moment order. The existing first-cell
values `N^122` and `N^248` are sharper specializations, not premises of (M).

On the simultaneous (4.2) source, primitive decay and finite neighbor shifts
give `J_block <= C N^tau J_loop`; distant primitive contributions are compared
with the retained `W^(-D)` floor. Combining with the support bound gives

```text
J_loop(u) <= C N^(2 delta) x_u^4,
J_block(u) <= C N^(2 delta+tau) x_u^4.                  (S)
```

All sources can be intersected before selecting a sample. For the concrete
first-cell producers, select `tau'` once and obtain raw 3/4/6, the entry laws,
averaging and the comparison event at that same witness. Two independently
chosen existential `tau'` values are not interchangeable. For a general
window the stipulated Step-1 inputs already refer to that window; no
first-cell event is reused as a general-window theorem.

The probability of the cutoff support is not assumed. The source event and
the fixed norm event are high probability independently of the induction.
The favorable estimates are used on their intersection with positive
support, without declaring that intersection itself high probability.

## 6. Recomputed drift, QV, and all residuals

The near drift uses the product of the averaged one-loop `r/A` and the raw
three-loop `r^2/A^2`, with an internal sum of length `ell` up to logarithms.
This gives `eta_u^(-1) r_u^3 T_u`. In the actual `eGpm` formula the triple is
labeled `(a2,b,a1)`; its near/far internal split must therefore be made with
that orientation. The output-distance condition is symmetric.

For the internal far part of a near output, set
`F=ellStarStar-ellStar`. Its triple bound is
`eta_u^(-1) J_block T_u(F)`. Relative to the preceding near drift profile,
the error is bounded by

```text
C N^zeta [L J_block/(ell_u eta_u r_u^2)]
  A_u^2 exp((log W)^(3/4)) T_u(F).
```

Under (S), `J_block<=N` eventually, since `x^4<=N^(4/30)` and the tolerances
are small. Also `T_u(F)<=2W^(-D)` eventually for fixed `D`. The displayed
error is polynomially small for `D>=60` and `N<=W^2`; the looser bound
`C N^(5+zeta) W^(1-D)` used in T403 is sufficient. The near indicator must be
retained until this error has been compared with the near spatial tail.

For a far output, the close-to-an-endpoint internal region uses two far
Green legs and one entry mean `sqrt(r/A)`, giving a term linear in `J_block`.
The remaining region uses three far Green legs and the square-root-tail
convolution, giving `J_block^(3/2)`. The quadratic gluing uses `J_loop^2`.
Thus the complete raw drift profile is

```text
N^nu eta_u^(-1) T_u [r_u^3 + A_u^(-1) J_loop^2
  + r_u^(3/2) A_u^(-1/2) J_block
  + r_u A_u^(-1) J_block^(3/2)].                        (D)
```

For QV, the raw four-loop source enters through its square root; the raw
six-loop source supplies the near term. In the near-output/far-internal
regime put `F4=ellStarStar-4ellStar`. Its remainder coefficient is

```text
epsilon_Q = 2WL eta_u^(-2) J_block^2 T_u(F4)^2
              A_u^4 exp(4(log W)^(3/4)).
```

It is at most `C N^9 W^(1-2D)` and hence at most `W^(-1)` eventually. The
required logarithmic condition `(4D)^2<=log W` is eventual for each **fixed**
`D`; it is not asserted for all `D` at one `N`.

The full raw diagonal QV is consequently bounded by

```text
N^nu eta_u^(-1) T_u^2
  [r_u^5 + J_block^2 r_u^(3/2) A_u^(-1/2)
          + J_block^3 A_u^(-1)].                     (Q)
```

Both far powers are essential. The spatial leakage coefficient
`WL W^(-D)` is absorbed by `eta_u^(-1) A_u^(-1)`; indeed
`(WL)(eta_u A_u)<=N^2<=W^4`. The near-kernel leakage in `rootProfile` is
`256 e^3 W^(-D)` times the endpoint tail and kernel time factor. Its square
and its cross products are bounded using `(a+b)^2<=2a^2+2b^2`, so they are
not discarded. The `W^(-1)` repaired near coefficient is absorbed in the
near main rate since `r>=1` and `eta<=1`.

For evolved QV one first uses the covariance seminorm triangle,

```text
sqrt(Q_H(Uf)_a) <= sum_b |U_ab| sqrt(Q_H(f_b)),
sum_b |U_ab| T_u(b) <= N^zeta (R/x_u)^2 T_v(a).
```

Only then is the fixed endpoint denominator applied. This avoids an
unjustified replacement of off-diagonal QV terms by diagonal entries and
avoids an `L^4` loss. The exact accepted `qvAt_full_absorbed` has arbitrary
`s<=u<=v<1`; its first-cell specialization is not the definition of the
general rate.

## 8. Gradient, joint complement, and zero-time endpoint

For the regularized modulus,
`|D sqrt(|F|^2+epsilon^2)| <= |DF|`. The derivative coefficients of each
positive power mean have sum at most the cardinality to the reciprocal
power. The canonical smoothing order makes the product of the two
cardinality factors at most `e`. It follows that

```text
norm_cov(grad S_k) <= e max_(j<k,a)
  sqrt(u_j Q_H((L-K)_(u_j,a))) / [T_j(a) x_j^4].
```

This is a covariance norm on the common base Gaussian coordinates. Cross-time
covariances are retained. There is no factor proportional to the smoothing
order and no inverse regularization parameter.

For fixed `P>=1` write `phi=E[c_k^(2P)|Y_u|^(2P)]`. The three Holder inputs
are

```text
Adr = norm_(2P)(c_k driftAt),
V   = norm_P(c_k^2 qvAt),
Z   = 1_transition B_k sqrt(qvAt).
```

The weight derivative vanishes off its transition region, including the
endpoints of that region. Inside it `c_k>0`; hence all favorable estimates
need only the support caps (S). On a common source event `G`, with
`rho=P(G^c)`, global polynomial envelopes give

```text
Adr <= a + N^K1 rho^(1/(2P)),
V   <= q + N^K2 rho^(1/P),
norm_(2P)(Z) <= b sqrt(q) + N^K3 rho^(1/(2P)).
```

The last envelope controls the **joint** prefix-gradient/current-QV product.
Resolvent derivatives on Hermitian matrices, the positive polynomial
eta/tail floors, finite covariance sums, and the deterministic kernel bound
supply it. It would be insufficient to bound only current QV on the
complement. Good is never differentiated, and `G intersect support` is not
used as the exceptional event.

The cross integral has the factor `u^(-1/2)`. If `s>=1/2`, Cauchy--Schwarz
uses

```text
eta_s^(-1) integral_s^v du/u <= 2/m0.
```

If `s<1/2`, then `eta_s>=m0/2`. On `[s,min(v,1/2)]`, `x<=2` and the full
current rate is bounded by `C N^nu R^(-4)`; integrate `u^(-1/2)` directly.
On the rest, `integral_(1/2)^v du/u<=log 2` and Cauchy--Schwarz applies.
Both cases give the uniform cross budget

```text
C_P N^(nu-2delta) R^(-2).
```

This avoids `log(1/s)`, which would be invalid for arbitrarily small positive
`s_N`. At fixed `N`, the remaining coordinate, drift and covariance
envelopes are finite on `[0,v]`; the scaling derivative contributes only
the integrable `u^(-1/2)` times Gaussian variables with finite moments.
Dominated integration therefore extends the positive-time identity to zero.
For `s=v` all time integrals vanish. For `k=0` the prefix derivative vanishes
and the initial moment is used. For `P=0`, the moment is bounded by one and
none of these divisions by `P` is used.

## 9. Moment inequality, literal old weight, and first failure

The frozen-weight Gaussian identity and the actual normalized generator give

```text
phi' <= 2P(Adr+Ccross) phi^((2P-1)/(2P))
        + P(2P-1) V phi^((P-1)/P).
```

This uses `eGpm+quadGlue` as the drift of the very coordinate whose QV occurs
in `V`. Gaussian scaling is not called a martingale. Regularizing `phi` by a
positive constant and using a scalar supersolution gives

```text
norm_(2P)(c_k Y_v) <= norm_(2P)(c_k Y_s)
    + integral(Adr+Ccross) + sqrt((2P-1) integral V).
```

The initial term is bounded by `J_s xiK R^(-2)`. Initial `BoundsCore.decay`
gives `J_s prec 1`; its global polynomial envelope gives every fixed moment.
The deterministic `xiK` is subpolynomial uniformly in endpoint and label.
Consequently `norm_(2P)(c_k Y_v(a)) <= C_P N^nu` after choosing sufficiently
small source losses, uniformly in `k,a`.

The original `piecewiseW` is independent of moment order. Its positive
support, and also positive support of `widenedW` at any fixed positive
target order, imply
`S_k <= 5e^2 N^(2delta) < Theta_N`. Thus the new cutoff is **one** there.
This proves domination by `c_k^(2P)` for every fixed `P` on the same sample;
it is stronger than comparing weights only at identical moment orders.

At `v=u_k`, the actual endpoint identity is

```text
jSnorm(v) = R^(-4) + max_a |Y_v(a)| <= 1+max_a |Y_v(a)|.
```

The coordinate sum costs `L^2<=N^2`. Hence, under the single subprobability
measure `W_old dP`,

```text
(E[W_old jSnorm(v)^(2P)])^(1/(2P)) <= C_P N^(nu+1/P).
```

For a requested `p`, choose fixed `P>=p`, `1/P<=delta/8`, and
`nu<=delta/16`. Lyapunov **under this same measure** gives
`E[W_old jSnorm(v)^(2p)] <= C N^(delta p/2)`. The bounded `cutTrunc`
decreases the integrand. This is the literal `WeightedMoment`. The same
argument works with a fixed target-order widened weight if its `hfamily`
consumer is desired; one must not use Lyapunov between two different
order-dependent measures.

For the probability induction, consider the first grid index with
`jSnorm(u_k)>N^delta`. On its earlier narrow-prefix event, `W_old=1`, since
all preceding values are below `N^(2delta)`. Because truncation is at that
larger level, this failure implies the truncated variable is at least
`N^delta`. Markov gives the per-index bound

```text
C_p N^(-3 delta p/2).
```

Choose `p` after the polynomial mesh cardinality and requested failure
power, then add the first-failure probabilities. There is no division by a
prefix probability and no multiplication of earlier failure masses. Finally
add the fixed modulus-event complement once. If `t_N` is not a grid point,
the last successful grid point and (M) cover its final partial interval; an
active prefix indexed by `K+1` is unnecessary. If the net has only its
initial point, the same argument applies to the whole short window.

Taking `delta` smaller than the requested stochastic tolerance proves the
all-time conclusion. The successful common event is eventually nonempty
because its probability tends to one; nonemptiness of cutoff support was
not an input to the proof.

## 10. Initial handoff and noncircularity

Within a fixed window the initial estimate is used only at `s_N`. The same

loss is accumulated in the mesh induction.

Across the macro-cells of paper p.24, the correct dependency is


Replacing the middle three arrows by A-prime alone would be invalid.
`Flow/Step1Producer.lean:331` does not provide a shortcut: its later-cell

an unconditional proof of those very inputs would be circular. The
single-window proof above does not make that call.

## 11. Optional first-cell block estimate

The stronger T401 sublemma is mathematically valid but unnecessary for the
general A-prime implication. Its event must explicitly include the
probability-one band-support condition: off-band Gaussian coordinates have
zero variance, but are not identically zero at every point of the raw sample
space.

For distinct neighboring blocks, the complex entries have real/imaginary
variance `1/(6W)`. Two `1/4`-nets of the complex unit sphere have combined
cardinality at most `9^(4W)`. For fixed unit vectors the scalar Gaussian tail
at level four is at most `4 exp(-24W)`, so

```text
P(norm X_ab > 8) <= 4 exp(-(24-4 log 9)W).
```

A union over at most `2L` oriented neighboring pairs is superpolynomially
small. Different blocks need not be independent for this union.

On this event, conjugation by `exp(alpha d_L(a,y))`,
`alpha=log(65/64)`, changes each neighboring off-diagonal block by at most
`1/8`. The block row and column sums give an operator norm perturbation at
most `1/4`; diagonal blocks cancel exactly. At `E=0`, `u<=1/2`, the imaginary
gap is at least `1/2`. The conjugated resolvent therefore has norm at most
four, for either charge. The neighbor in `gsqBlk` costs `exp(2alpha)`, so


simultaneously in the whole first cell. Empty far sets give `jG=1` directly.
No corresponding constant late-window bound follows from this argument,
because its fixed imaginary gap is essential.
