## Smallest next theorem and exact calculation

For the paper's flow put `a=√u`, `b=1−u`, and

`g_u(x)=(a x−i b)⁻¹`.

The smallest useful analytic theorem has no new hypotheses beyond `0<u<1`:

```lean
theorem semicircleMeasure_flow_poisson (u : ℝ)
    (hu0 : 0 < u) (hu1 : u < 1) :
    (∫ x : ℝ, (1-u) / (u*x^2 + (1-u)^2) ∂semicircleMeasure) = 1
```

It immediately yields the required measure-integral statement for `0≤u≤1/2`, after a separate `u=0` branch:

```lean
theorem semicircleMeasure_flow_resolvent (u : ℝ)
    (hu : u ∈ Set.Icc (0:ℝ) (1/2)) :
    (∫ x : ℝ,
      ((((Real.sqrt u * x : ℝ) : ℂ) - Complex.I * (1-u))⁻¹)
        ∂semicircleMeasure) = Complex.I
```

Here is a direct proof of the scalar theorem. Define

`φ(t)=2t/sqrt(1+t²)`.

It is an increasing bijection `ℝ → (-2,2)`, with inverse `x ↦ x/sqrt(4−x²)` on `(-2,2)` and derivative

`φ'(t)=2/((1+t²)sqrt(1+t²))>0`.

Since `sqrt(4−φ(t)²)=2/sqrt(1+t²)`, the exact measure Jacobian is

`ρ_sc(φ(t)) φ'(t) = 2/[π(1+t²)²]`.

Set `c=1+u`. Then `b>0`, `c>b>0`, `b+c=2`, and `c²=b²+4u`. The change of variables gives

`∫ b/(u x²+b²) dρ_sc(x)
 = (2b/π) ∫_ℝ dt / [(1+t²)(b²+c²t²)]`.

For arbitrary `0<b<c`, the rational integral is

`∫_ℝ dt / [(1+t²)(b²+c²t²)] = π/[b(b+c)]`.

Indeed, with `d=c²−b²>0`, its integrand is

`d⁻¹ [c²/(b²+c²t²) − 1/(1+t²)]`.

Mathlib's Cauchy integral gives `∫(1+t²)⁻¹ dt=π`; scaling gives `∫(b²+c²t²)⁻¹ dt=π/(bc)`. Substitution and `b+c=2` now give `2b/π · π/[b(b+c)]=1`. This proves the needed density integral from the explicit density rather than from a postulated quadratic equation.

For the complex step, `u x²+b²>0` and

`g_u(x)=(a x+i b)/(u x²+b²)`.

The real part is odd. Reflection invariance of `semicircleMeasure` makes its integral zero, and the preceding scalar theorem makes the imaginary part exactly one. The denominator satisfies `‖a x−i b‖≥b>0` for every real `x`, so the integrand is bounded by `1/b` and is integrable against the probability measure. At `u=0`, `g_0(x)=(-i)⁻¹=i` pointwise; normalization gives its integral `i` without any division by `√u`. At `u=1/2`, `b=1/2` and the same proof applies, with denominator norm at least `1/2`, including `x=±2`. As a nondegenerate check, `g_(1/2)(0)=2i`, while its integral is `i`; the conclusion is an actual averaging identity, not pointwise constancy or an empty interval.

## Verified Mathlib interfaces and remaining proof work

A scratch `lake env lean` run against the installed Mathlib and accepted `.olean` files confirmed these exact names and signatures:

* `integral_withDensity_eq_integral_toReal_smul` converts a `withDensity` integral to the base-measure integral when the ENNReal density is measurable and finite a.e. Here `ENNReal.toReal_ofReal` reduces the scalar to `semicircleDensity`, using its proved nonnegativity.
* `MeasureTheory.integral_image_eq_integral_abs_deriv_smul` accepts a measurable set, `HasDerivWithinAt` on it, and `InjOn`; with `s=Set.univ` and `f=φ`, its left side is the integral over `φ '' univ = Ioo (-2) 2`. The endpoints have zero density/mass and the exterior has zero density. Its companion `integrableOn_image_iff_integrableOn_abs_deriv_smul` is available if the integrability bridge is needed.
* `integral_univ_inv_one_add_sq = π` and `integral_univ_inv_one_add_mul_sq (k) = π/|k|` are **root-level** constants from `Mathlib.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.ImproperIntegrals`; `integrable_inv_one_add_sq` and `integrable_inv_one_add_mul_sq` supply the hypotheses for splitting integrals. A scratch proof of `∫(b²+(ct)²)⁻¹dt=π/(bc)` for `b,c>0` compiled using the scaled theorem.
* `MeasureTheory.Integrable.of_bound`, `MeasureTheory.integral_map`, and `integral_complex_ofReal` supply bounded integrability, reflection, and real-to-complex transfer. The project theorem `semicircleMeasure_map_neg` supplies the map equality. A separate scratch check of the constant integral at `u=0` compiled.
