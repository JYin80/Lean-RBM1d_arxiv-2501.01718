## Exact claim and dependencies

The public increment theorem states, for **all** real `a,b`,

```lean
semicircleMeasure (Ioc a b) =
  ENNReal.ofReal (ProbabilityTheory.cdf semicircleMeasure b -
    ProbabilityTheory.cdf semicircleMeasure a)
```

The public strictness theorem is `StrictMonoOn (ProbabilityTheory.cdf semicircleMeasure) (Icc (-2) 2)`. Its only measure input is the accepted `semicircleMeasure` with its `IsProbabilityMeasure` instance and `semicircleMeasure_Ioo_pos` for `-2 ≤ a < b ≤ 2` (`SemicircleDensityMeasure.lean`). The new module imports `SemicircleDensitySupportFull.lean` and Mathlib's `Probability/CDF.lean`; it adds no hypothesis, axiom, or external input.

Mathlib's `ProbabilityTheory.cdf` is a `StieltjesFunction`; `measure_cdf` identifies its Stieltjes measure with `semicircleMeasure` under the probability-measure instance, and `StieltjesFunction.measure_Ioc` gives exactly the displayed `ENNReal.ofReal` difference. This also handles `a ≥ b`: the interval is empty and `ofReal` clamps a nonpositive difference to zero. The paper `paper/250520-YinJun-v2.pdf`, p. 7, defines the density `ρsc(x) = √((4−x²)₊)/(2π)`; it does not state this CDF theorem. The new result is a measure-theoretic consequence of the accepted density measure, with no paper-claim substitution.

## Boundary and nonvacuity audit

For any `a,b ∈ [-2,2]` with `a < b`, `(a,b) ⊆ (a,b]`. The accepted `semicircleMeasure_Ioo_pos` gives positive mass to `(a,b)` even when `a=-2` or `b=2`, hence positive mass to `(a,b]`. Mathlib's `monotone_cdf` makes `cdf b-cdf a ≥ 0`; `measure_ne_top` and `ENNReal.toReal_ofReal` convert positive interval mass into `cdf b-cdf a > 0`. This proves strictness throughout the **closed** support interval, including intervals adjacent to both endpoints; no endpoint atom is needed.

The module supplies a concrete interior witness `semicircleMeasure_Ioc_neg_one_one_pos`, and endpoint families `semicircleMeasure_Ioc_leftEndpoint_pos` and `semicircleMeasure_Ioc_rightEndpoint_pos`. In particular `b=0` in the first family and `a=0` in the second give nondegenerate endpoint intervals. All assumptions are realizable.
