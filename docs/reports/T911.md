# T911 — semicircle midpoint quantile reflection

## Result

The standalone producer proves, for every `W ≥ 1` and `j < W`, that the
midpoint quantile in the complementary cell is the negative of the original
midpoint quantile. It also proves that the central midpoint for odd `W` is
zero and provides a nondegenerate `W=2` witness with distinct representatives
and positive measure in both cells.

build, the scheduler recorded T909's final independent PASS and explicitly

## Source and dependencies

before (2.1), where the semicircle density is
`rho_sc(x) = (2*pi)^(-1) sqrt((4-x^2)_+)`. The accepted Lean density measure is
the same normalized even density. The new module imports only the accepted
CDF reflection and finite quantile-cell producers:

- `semicircleMeasure_cdf_reflection` gives `F(-x)=1-F(x)`.
- `semicircleLambda_cdf` gives the exact midpoint level `(j+1/2)/W`.
- `existsUnique_semicircleCDF_eq` gives uniqueness on `[-2,2]`.
- `semicircleQuantileCell_witness_two` and `semicircleQuantileCell_mass`
  supply the concrete cell-order and mass data for the witness.

No paper-delta is needed: these are exact consequences of the specified even
density and the accepted quantile construction. No new assumptions are
introduced.

## Declarations

In `RBM1D/Defs/SemicircleQuantileReflection.lean`:

- `semicircleLambda_reflection` proves the exact paired midpoint identity.
  With `k = W - 1 - j`, integer arithmetic gives `k+j+1=W`; CDF reflection
  identifies `F(-lambda_j)` with the level of `lambda_k`. Both points lie in
  `[-2,2]`, so uniqueness of the quantile inverse identifies them.
- `semicircleLambda_odd_center` specializes reflection to `W=2m+1`, where
  the complementary index is `m`, and concludes that the central midpoint is
  zero.
- `semicircleQuantileReflection_witness_two` proves
  `lambda_0 < lambda_1` at `W=2` and strictly positive measure for both
  consecutive quantile cells. Each mass is exactly `1/2` by the accepted
  finite-cell theorem.

The statements include the edge width `W=1` in the general reflection theorem;
the hypotheses are jointly satisfiable, with the existing W1/W2 quantile-cell
witnesses giving concrete checks.
