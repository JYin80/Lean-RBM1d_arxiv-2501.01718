## 3. Same-event minor calculus and the fixed proxy

Fix a finite deletion budget `M` first. For a sufficiently small fixed `b>0`, the hypotheses give an event `E_N(u,b)` on which

```text
max_ij |G_ij-m delta_ij| <= (N+2)^(-a/2),
max_ij |G_ij-m delta_ij| <= N^b sqrt(Gamma).
```

For every fixed `B>0`, its complement has probability at most `N^(-B)` eventually, uniformly in deterministic `u`. The event is defined by these thresholds, not by `B`. On it, eventually,

```text
1/(4W) <= Gamma <= (1+delta)^2/W + delta^2 <= N^(-a0),
delta=(N+2)^(-a/2),    a0=min(a,1/2+d.c)/2 > 0.
```

The lower bound uses the actual diagonal entries, not fluctuation averaging. All eventual dimension estimates are used only for `N>=1`.

For every finite deleted set `S`, take the actual principal-minor resolvent `G^S`, embedded by zero at deleted rows and columns. This is exactly the convention of `FlucIterHigh.gEnt`. Keep the original block membership and original `W`:

```text
Gamma_ab^S = W^(-2) ||1_a G^S 1_b||_HS^2,
q_N = 2 ceil(2 log(N+2)) + 8,
lambda^S = (W^(-q_N) + sum_ab (Gamma_ab^S)^q_N)^(1/q_N).
```

`q_N` is deterministic and **independent of the requested moment and failure probability**. Eventually `q_N>=log(L^2+1)` and, for each fixed `M`, `q_N>=4M`. Consequently

```text
max(W^(-1), max_ab Gamma_ab^S)
 <= lambda^S <= e max(W^(-1), max_ab Gamma_ab^S).                 (P0)
```

The proxy is measurable, strictly positive, and free of every row in `S`. Globally `1/lambda^S<=W`; by the Hermitian minor resolvent bound, `Gamma_ab^S<=eta_u^(-2)/W` and `lambda^S<=e eta_u^(-2)/W` eventually. Here `eta_u<=1`, and `eta_u>=eta_(t_N)>=N^(-K)`. These are polynomial envelopes, not comparisons to the realized Gamma.

### 3.1 Finite differences, including block boundary coordinates

Use `Delta_k F^S=F^S-F^(S union {k})` and distinct fresh labels. For `|S|+|T|<=M`, the identity (4.9), with the zero embedding, is

```text
Delta_k G^S = G^S_:k G^S_k: / G^S_kk.                         (R1)
```

It holds at a row/column coordinate equal to `k` too: the later minor is zero there. Coordinates already in `S` vanish on both sides. One must not discard these boundary terms or classify the endpoint vector's `k`-coordinate as off-diagonal.

Iteration on the original good sample keeps surviving minor diagonals bounded away from zero and centered entries/off-diagonal entries bounded by `C_M N^(C_M b) sqrt(Gamma)`. The existing pointwise scalar results in Section 2 cover this iteration and the scalar higher differences. In a block endpoint norm,

```text
W^(-1) ||1_a G^S_:k||_2^2 <= C_M N^(C_M b) Gamma,
W^(-1) ||G^S_k: 1_b||_2^2 <= C_M N^(C_M b) Gamma.
```

The possible diagonal coordinate costs `O(1/W)<=O(Gamma)`; the other coordinates use REL. Thus the block Hilbert-Schmidt norm in (R1), divided by `W`, costs `Gamma` with a small loss, with no residual factor of `W`.

For `r=|T|>=1`, the discrete product rule yields

```text
W^(-1)||1_a Delta_T G^S 1_b||_HS
  <= C_M N^(C_M b) Gamma^((r+1)/2),
|Delta_T (G^S_ii-m)|
  <= C_M N^(C_M b) Gamma^((r+1)/2),  i outside S union T.     (Rr)
```

For completeness, the induction invariant is two normalized endpoint vectors, internal off-diagonal entries between distinct deletion vertices, and inverse surviving diagonals. A fresh difference of an endpoint inserts one internal off-diagonal entry; a fresh difference of an internal entry replaces one edge by two. Each gains `sqrt(Gamma)` with the indicated loss. For an inverse diagonal the exact formula

```text
Delta_k (1/G^S_jj)
 = -G^S_jk G^S_kj / (G^S_jj G^(S+k)_jj G^S_kk)
```

gains `Gamma`, which is stronger. The new label is distinct from the internal vertices. Shifted factors satisfy the same estimates. This establishes the induction without a global bound on inverse diagonals. At order zero, the block norm divided by `W` is `O(sqrt(Gamma))`: start at the full resolvent and telescope finitely many (R1) terms. The centered diagonal has the same order-zero bound.

Applying the product rule to the squared Hilbert-Schmidt norm gives

```text
|Delta_T Gamma_ab^S|
 <= C_M N^(C_M b) Gamma^(1+r/2),  1<=r, |S|+r<=M.            (Lr)
```

In particular, all these minor loop maxima differ from Gamma by at most `C_M N^(C_M b) Gamma^(3/2)`. Taking `b` small enough makes this `o(Gamma)`. By (P0), all `lambda^S` with `|S|<=M` are comparable to Gamma by constants on this **same sample's event**.

### 3.2 Smooth-norm differences

For nonnegative vectors with the floor coordinate included, put `F(v)=||v||_q`. For `j<=M` and `q>=4M`, direct differentiation and Holder give

```text
|D^j F(v)[h_1,...,h_j]|
 <= C_j q^(C_j) ||v||_q^(1-j) product_l ||h_l||_q,
|D^j (1/F)(v)[h_1,...,h_j]|
 <= C_j q^(C_j) ||v||_q^(-1-j) product_l ||h_l||_q.           (D)
```

Indeed the `j`th derivative of `sum v_c^q` is a constant bounded by `q^j` times `sum v_c^(q-j) product h_l,c`; Holder bounds it by `||v||_q^(q-j) product ||h_l||_q`. The ordinary chain rule for its powers `1/q` and `-1/q` proves (D), with finitely many partitions at fixed `j`. Constants are independent of the number of blocks.

Interpolate the loop-vector cube indexed by `U subset T` multi-affinely. Mixed derivatives are convex combinations, up to sign, of the exact differences (Lr). Their q-norms acquire at most `(L^2+1)^(1/q)<=e`. Every interpolated vector has norm bounded below by `2^(-r)` times the norm of some vertex, since a vertex coefficient is at least `2^(-r)` and coordinates are nonnegative. Thus its norm stays comparable to Gamma. The mixed chain rule followed by the multivariable fundamental theorem of calculus proves

```text
|Delta_T lambda^S|
 <= C_M q_N^(C_M) N^(C_M b) Gamma^(1+r/2),
|Delta_T (1/lambda^S)|
 <= C_M q_N^(C_M) N^(C_M b) Gamma^(-1+r/2).                 (Pr)
```

This verifies the smooth-proxy step in V6. It cannot be replaced by higher differences of a hard maximum; switching maximizers need not preserve higher-order gains.

## 4. The precise common-minor product lemma

Define, for `i notin S`,

```text
Y_i^S = (G^S_ii-m)/lambda^(S union {i}),
X_i = Q_i Y_i^empty = Z_i/lambda^{i}.
```

For a total Lean family, set `Y_i^S=0` when `i in S`, matching `greenSetDiagCentered`. This convention is never used in a fresh-label estimate but permits the existing all-set annihilation lemma to apply directly. The denominator in every relevant term contains `i`, so the centering identity is exact.

Combining (Rr) and (Pr) by the discrete product rule gives, for `i outside S union T` and `|S union T union {i}|<=M`,

```text
|Delta_T Y_i^S|
 <= C_M q_N^(C_M) N^(C_M b) Gamma^((|T|-1)/2).              (Yr)
```

The order-zero, first-, and second-difference costs are respectively `Gamma^(-1/2)`, `1`, and `Gamma^(1/2)`. Globally, the raw `Y_i^S` is bounded by `W(eta_u^(-1)+1)<=2N^(K+1)`, and a finite difference by `2^M` times this. Rational expansions involving inverse diagonals are used only on the good event.

Here is a useful exact intermediate statement, sharper than an unspecified singleton-moment request. Fix `n>=1`, a tuple `k : {1,...,n}->I_N`, and its distinct-label set `R=image(k)`. Let each operator word `L_h` have distinct labels, all in `R \ {k_h}`. Let

```text
d_extra = sum_h numQ(L_h),
F_h = applyOps L_h (Q_(k_h) Y_(k_h)^empty).
```

For every `epsilon>0`, there is `C_(n,epsilon)>0` such that eventually, uniformly in `u`, `k`, and these words,

```text
E product_h |F_h|
 <= C_(n,epsilon) N^epsilon W^((n-min(d_extra,n))/2).        (SP)
```

The same statement allows conjugation of any slots. The deletion budget is `M=n`, including every denominator's own row label. Only finite differences of order at most `n-1` are needed for a slot. The sum of orders may reach `n(n-1)`; it is not the deletion budget.

### Rigorous resampling proof of (SP)

1. Let `T_h` be the Q-labels in `L_h`. By row-freeness and `applyOps_eq_applyOps_minorDiff`, replace `Y_(k_h)^empty` by `Delta_(T_h) Y_(k_h)^empty` under the extra Q operators. Commute the own-row center and other projections as necessary. Every nonempty deleted summand is annihilated by a Q belonging to that deleted set. This is an identity on the whole Gaussian space, before inserting an indicator.
2. Expand all Q operators as `1-P`. The remaining compositions of P operators integrate the union of their row-coordinate sets, by `condPred_condPred`. After applying triangle inequalities, conditional Jensen, and Fubini, the product is bounded by finitely many joint expectations of products of `|Delta_(T_h)Y_(k_h)^empty(omega_h)|`. Each `omega_h` is formed from a common base sample `omega` by resampling only rows in `R`, using auxiliary independent coordinate copies. Every `omega_h` has marginal law `P d`, by `measurePreserving_predSplit`. The number of terms/copies depends only on `n`.
3. **Exact shared denominator:** every coordinate with both endpoints outside `R` is unchanged. Therefore

   ```text
   G^R(omega_h) = G^R(omega),
   lambda^R(omega_h) = lambda^R(omega)     for every factor h.
   ```

   This is equality of the actual principal minors, not equality in distribution, an independent-copy comparison, or a claim that the undeleted Gamma is row-free. The common denominator is available because all word labels belong to `R`.
4. On the joint event that all transformed samples satisfy `E_N(u,b)`, compare each `Gamma(omega_h)` to this one `lambda^R(omega)`. Applying (Yr) and multiplying **before** using upper or lower envelopes gives

   ```text
   product_h |Delta_(T_h)Y_(k_h)^empty(omega_h)|
     <= C_n q_N^(C_n) N^(C_n b)
          (lambda^R(omega))^((d_extra-n)/2).
   ```

   On that event `W^(-1)<=lambda^R<=1` eventually. If `d_extra<=n`, use the lower bound; if `d_extra>=n`, use the upper bound. This gives exactly the bandwidth factor in (SP).
5. The joint bad-event probability is at most `C_n N^(-B)` by its marginals; the transformed samples need not be independent. The raw global product envelope is `C_n N^(n(K+1))`. For a desired remainder `N^(-A)`, take `B>n(K+1)+A+1` and absorb constants eventually. This estimates the exceptional contribution without disturbing the exact singleton cancellations.

This proves (SP), with the loss choices in Section 7. Separate bounds on individual negative and positive random powers would not prove it.

### Why the existing gain interface should not be forced

The natural bound is saturated once `d_extra>=n`. Choosing `B=sqrt(W)` and `rho=W^(-1/2)` in the old deterministic interface would demand `W^((n-d_extra)/2)` even for `d_extra>n`. The shared-minor proof only gives 1 in this regime; `lambda^R>=1/W` gives the wrong direction for a positive power. No such stronger bandwidth decay is needed by the moment argument.

Also, `OpsOk` alone does not restrict letters to `image(k)`. `OpsOkOut` does retain that support. Either keep that invariant in a new successor interface or explicitly bound the union of tuple and word labels. Silently applying the budget `M=n` to the old unrestricted-word premise loses the fully deleted minor's budget. These are concrete representation/interface issues, not failures of the manuscript's estimate.

## 5. Singleton pivot and the required fixed moments

Take `n=2p`, with fixed `p>=1`. In the mixed moment of `sum_i w_i X_i`, let a tuple have `a_single` singleton labels and `d0` distinct labels. Pivot its singleton slots using `prod_eq_sum_pivotFam`. The branch assigning P to every other slot has expectation zero by `integral_prod_pivotFam_empty`; the pivot's own conditional mean is zero, even after previous operators, by commutation. Every surviving branch puts at least one new Q on another slot. Distinct singleton labels are fresh, and all remain in the tuple's `R`. Thus

```text
d_extra >= a_single,     2 d0 <= n+a_single.
```

(SP) bounds every terminal branch by `C_(n,epsilon) N^epsilon W^((n-a_single)/2)`. There are at most `(2^(n-1)-1)^a_single` surviving choices, a constant for fixed `n`. Consequently

```text
|E product_h X_(k_h) or conjugate(X_(k_h))|
 <= C_(n,epsilon) N^epsilon W^((n-a_single)/2).              (S)
```

For a fixed equality partition with multiplicities `r_1,...,r_d0`, deterministic admissible weights have coefficient mass at most

```text
sum_(distinct labels j_1,...,j_d0) product_l |w_(j_l)|^r_l
 <= W^(-(n-d0)) (sum_i |w_i|)^d0
 <= W^(-(n-d0)).
```

Since `n-d0 >= (n-a_single)/2`, this cancels the bandwidth factor in (S). Summing over finitely many partitions proves the exact missing normalized moment:

```text
forall fixed p>=1, epsilon>0, exists C>0, eventually N,
  forall u in [s_N,t_N], forall deterministic admissible w,
    E |sum_i w_i Q_i Y_i^empty|^(2p) <= C N^epsilon.          (M)
```

Integrability follows from the global polynomial envelope for each fixed `N,u`. At `n=2`, the coincident-label case has product cost `W` and coefficient mass `W^(-1)`; the two distinct labels have two singleton gains and product cost 1. At `n=4`, the partition types `4`, `3+1`, `2+2`, `2+1+1`, and `1+1+1+1` leave respectively `W^(-1)`, `W^(-1/2)`, `1`, `1`, `1`. These checks include all collision regimes and confirm the exponent signs.

## 6. Recovery of the original fluctuation and removal of the floor

Let `F_w=sum_i w_i Z_i` and `R0=Gamma+1/W`. The auxiliary moments in (M) are not yet moments of `F_w/lambda^empty`, since each term has its own row-free denominator. This distinction must remain explicit.

Define

```text
A_i = Z_i/sqrt(lambda^{i}) = Q_i[v_i/sqrt(lambda^{i})],
B_i = sqrt(lambda^{i}) (1/lambda^empty - 1/lambda^{i}).
```

The first identity is exact by row-freeness. On the entry event, `|v_i/sqrt(lambda^{i})|<=C N^b`; its global bound is polynomial. Hence every fixed moment has arbitrarily small polynomial loss, and conditional Jensen gives the same for `A_i`. By the first-difference case of (Pr), `|B_i|<=C q_N^C N^(Cb)` on the entry event. Globally,

```text
|B_i| <= 2W sqrt(lambda^{i}) <= C sqrt(W) eta_u^(-1),
```

so its fixed moments have the same small-loss property after paying the complement. The exact identity

```text
F_w/lambda^empty = sum_i w_i X_i + sum_i w_i A_i B_i
```

and Holder at order `4p`, followed by the weight l1 bound, imply

```text
E (|F_w|/R0)^(2p) <= C_(p,epsilon) N^epsilon.                (RM)
```

Here `lambda^empty/R0<=e` globally eventually, by (P0). The recovery error only uses one deletion and fixed moments of `A_i,B_i`; it does not require a new growing deletion budget. (RM) is a sufficient intermediate estimate, not an additional hypothesis or a strengthening demanded of the paper's final conclusion.

On the WLL event, `1/W<=4Gamma`, hence `R0<=5Gamma`. Therefore

```text
P{|F_w|>N^tau Gamma}
 <= P{WLL event fails} + C_(p,epsilon) 5^(2p) N^(epsilon-2p tau).
```

Given `tau,D>0`, choose a fixed `p` large enough and then a positive moment loss `epsilon<2p tau-D`. Constants and the WLL complement are absorbed eventually. This proves the literal `Gamma=Lmax` tail estimate in Section 1. No inverse-Gamma moment on the entire space, nonzero fluctuation, or stronger witness is needed. At `u=0`, `Z_i=0`, so the conclusion holds directly.

## 7. Quantifiers, losses, and exceptional-event budget

The order is

```text
model d, bulk E, interval s,t, a,K, actual WLL/REL hypotheses;
one fixed function q_N independent of moments;
fixed p>=1 and desired moment loss epsilon>0;
n=M=2p, and constants from this finite calculus;
small entry tolerance b, then required exceptional exponent B;
eventual threshold N0;
all deterministic u, admissible w, tuple labels, and allowed words.
```

For an explicit budget convention, let `H_n>=1` dominate every fixed power of `N^b` and of `q_N` occurring in the calculus and n-factor products. Increase it to cover the finite minor comparisons as well. Choose

```text
0 < b < min(a0/(8 H_n), epsilon/(4 H_n)).
```

Then all needed comparison errors of the form `C q_N^H N^(H b) sqrt(Gamma)` vanish eventually. Also `q_N=O(log N)`, so `C_n q_N^(H_n)<=N^(epsilon/2)` eventually; the good product cost is at most `N^(3epsilon/4)` before harmless constants are absorbed. Choose an exceptional remainder `N^(-A)`, for example `A=1`, and take `B>n(K+1)+A+1` in the product step. For the separate `4p` recovery moments take the corresponding larger fixed exceptional exponent. The original entry-tail exponent may be enlarged by 3 to pay the at-most-`N^2` entry union. There is no union over all weights, and no `N^n` loss from tuples: their total absolute coefficient mass is at most 1.

The upper bound `q_N>=4M` is only eventual for each fixed `p`; neither `p` nor the deletion budget grows with `N`. The logarithmic q is a smoothing exponent, not the moment order. No bound involving `N^(-K)` or a growing support is imposed at `N=0`.
