## 4. Why the intersection does not follow

Let `C_N` be the common event and `T_N` the exact strict transition.  Existing results can at
best supply

```text
P(C_N^c) ≤ N^(-A) eventually for every fixed A,
T_N.Nonempty (after a target/canonical adapter),
possibly P(T_N) > 0 (after a finite-support theorem).
```

These statements do not imply `C_N ∩ T_N` is nonempty.  The whole transition can lie inside
`C_N^c`.  Even `P(T_N)>0` is insufficient unless one also proves, for example,

```text
P(C_N^c) < P(T_N),
```

or directly proves positive mass for `C_N ∩ T_N`.  High probability is asymptotic only for each
fixed failure exponent; it cannot be invoked with an `N`-dependent exponent chosen after seeing
the (possibly extremely small) transition mass.

Likewise, full support only turns a nonempty open transition into positive transition mass.  It
does not force an arbitrary high-probability measurable set to meet that open set.  The explicit
scalar transition point is not the nonconstructively chosen T579 resident, and a singleton has
Gaussian measure zero in its nondegenerate coordinates.

Therefore the implication

```text
HighProb commonEvent + Nonempty transition
  ⇒ Nonempty (commonEvent ∩ transition)
```

is unavailable and invalid in this generality.
