## Formal claim

`eventually_integral_actual_drift_full_cross_le_N1_slot` fixes the schedule parameters, `D ≥ 60`, and an integer `p ≥ 1` before choosing the eventual cutoff in `N`. For every active target-mesh cell `k`, subject also to `(k : ℝ) ≤ N^D`, and every output `a`, it proves

```text
2 * ∫_(s N)^v (g E D (deltaWeight δ) p N k a r
                 + positiveTimeCrossBudget E D (deltaWeight δ) s t N k p a r)
  ≤ driftTerm (mE E).im x R (slotXi' x) (slotA x R) (slotEps x R)
      (slotQ R) (slotBeta x R) (slotGamma x R) (slotJv x R) / R^4,
```

where `v = cutNetPt s (targetMesh D) N k`, `x = N^(δ/8)`, and
`R = ratR E s N v`. Both integral summands are the actual definitions from T615/T1201; the result is not a replacement profile.

## Mathematical preflight

I checked the accepted producer sources and the authorized PDF (5.21), (5.24), and (5.40)–(5.43). Those paper formulas motivate the imported time-net and quadratic-variation estimates. This consumer does not newly formalize the paper's stopped stochastic hierarchy, stochastic-integral estimate, or stopping-time claims.

1. **Actual drift on the early prefix.** T611 gives the all-sample estimate `|driftAt| ≤ N^(D+8)` on every active cell, running time, output, and sample. T615 defines `g` as the `p`-moment norm of that same drift with its actual smooth weight; the weight is between zero and one. Moment monotonicity therefore gives `g ≤ N^(D+8)`. For large `N`, `targetMesh D N = N^(4D+18)`. If `k ≤ N^D`, then
   `v - s_N = k / targetMesh ≤ N^(-3D-18)`, hence
   `∫ g ≤ N^(-3D-18) N^(D+8) = N^(-2D-10)`.

2. **Full positive-time cross budget.** The local pointwise estimate used in T1215 bounds the exact T1201 norm-good budget by `(15p/4) * crossProfile + (15p/8)N^(-1)/√r` for positive `r`; the zero-time case follows from the literal totalized definitions. T1245 then bounds `crossProfile` by
   `K * N^(13δ/640) * etaT(E,s_N)^(-1) * R^(-2) / √r`, with `K = crossProfilePointwiseConst`. T1207 transfers this norm-good integral to the full positive-time cross budget and adds `(15p/4)N^(-1)(√v−√s_N)`. Integrating the pointwise estimate gives the combined full-cross bound

   ```text
   (15p/2) K N^(13δ/640) etaT(E,s_N)^(-1) R^(-2) (√v−√s_N)
     + (15p/2) N^(-1) (√v−√s_N).
   ```

3. **Early-prefix powers.** Since `√v−√s_N ≤ √(v−s_N)`, the prefix length gives
   `√v−√s_N ≤ N^(-3D/2-9)`. The accepted scale estimate and monotonicity of `etaT` give `etaT(E,s_N)^(-1) ≤ N`; Cond272Reg's 30th-power bound gives `R ≥ 1`, so `R^(-2) ≤ 1`. The two displayed cross terms are thus bounded by fixed-in-`N` coefficients times
   `N^(13δ/640−3D/2−8)` and `N^(-3D/2−10)`, respectively. With `D ≥ 60` and `δ ≤ 1`, both exponents are strictly below `−1`. The drift integral exponent `−2D−10` is also below `−1`. Since `p` and `E` are fixed before the eventual cutoff, the three coefficients are absorbed to obtain a total bound of `3N^(-1)`; the factor `2` in the claim gives `6N^(-1)`.

4. **Actual slot comparison.** `APrimeInit.slotXi' x = x^(1/4)`, `slotQ R = R²/2`, and `x = N^(δ/8) ≥ 1`. Applying `APrimeSlotArith.driftTerm_ge_near` and dividing by `R⁴` gives the actual slot lower bound `1/(2 Im mE)`. The fixed-`E` eventual absorption `12 Im(mE) N^(-1) ≤ 1` completes the requested inequality.

The proof includes `k=0`: its target endpoint is `s_N`, both integrals are over a degenerate interval, and the slot is positive. No positive-cell assumption is used by the consumer theorem.
