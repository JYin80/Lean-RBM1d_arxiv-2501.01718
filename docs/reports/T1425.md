\[
f_u(H)=\operatorname{Im}\bigl(M^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(H-z_u)^{-1}\bigr),\qquad

All derivatives below act on the complex-valued inclusion of these real functions, with real Fréchet derivatives as in `Gauss.coordD1`, `coordD2`, and `wirtSecond`. Write

\[
C_{ab}=\texttt{centeredVarianceEntry}\ d\ N\ a\ b
=S_{ab}-M^{-1},\qquad W_{ab}f=\texttt{Gauss.wirtSecond}\ d\ N\ f\ H\ a\ b,
\]

and define the contraction

\[
\mathcal T_C F=\sum_{a\in J}\sum_{b\in J} C_{ab}W_{ab}F.
\]

Here a real coefficient means real scalar multiplication, equivalently multiplication by its complex inclusion. `C` is real and symmetric; it need not be nonnegative. Both sums are over **all ordered matrix-index pairs**, including the diagonal.

The deterministic target, with precisely the paper's weights, is

\[
\boxed{
\|\mathcal T_C F\|
\leq
\sum_{u\in s} L_1(z_u)\prod_{i\in s\setminus\{u\}}f_i

The equalities used to derive (D) require only `Im z_u ≠ 0`. The unmodified weights in (D) require `Im z_u > 0`, as in (2.22). Under that condition every `f_u ≥ 0`. No bulk-energy restriction, microscopic scale, growth condition, stochastic event, or loss exponent is needed for (D).

## 3. Explicit contraction of the product Hessian

Write

\[
w_u=\prod_{i\in s\setminus\{u\}}f_i,
\qquad
w_{uv}=\prod_{i\in s\setminus\{u,v\}}f_i.
\]

For `a ≠ b`, let `x_u` and `y_u` be the real first derivatives of `f_u` along `Bmat a b true` and `Bmat a b false`. The cross contribution of the existing real-coordinate product expansion to `W_ab F`, for one ordered pair `(u,v)`, is

\[
Q_{ab}(u,v)=\tfrac14(x_u x_v+y_u y_v).
\tag{1}
\]

For `a=b`, it is the product of the two derivatives along `E_aa`, without the factor `1/4` or an imaginary coordinate. Set

\[
D^u_{ab}=\texttt{stieltjesImWirtingerFirst}\ H\ z_u\ a\ b.
\]

The coordinate swap lemmas give, off the diagonal,

\[
D^u_{ab}=\tfrac12(x_u-i y_u),\qquad
D^u_{ba}=\tfrac12(x_u+i y_u).
\]

Consequently the uniform identity, valid also when `a=b`, is

\[
Q_{ab}(u,v)=\tfrac12\left(D^u_{ab}D^v_{ba}+D^u_{ba}D^v_{ab}\right).
\tag{2}
\]

This identity follows by multiplication of the two binomials; on the diagonal both summands coincide. In particular, one must **not** replace `Q_ab` by `D^u_ab D^v_ba` entrywise. That replacement becomes valid after the following contraction:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}D^u_{ba}D^v_{ab}
&=\sum_{a,b}C_{ba}D^u_{ab}D^v_{ba}
&&\text{(swap the two full finite sums)}\\
&=\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}D^u_{ab}D^v_{ba}
&&\text{(symmetry of }C\text{)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Thus define

\[
A_u=\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}W_{ab}f_u,\qquad
B_{uv}=\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}D^u_{ab}D^v_{ba}.
\]

Expanding the existing `stieltjesImProductLineSecond`, interchanging only finite sums, and using (2)–(3), yields the exact identity

\[
\boxed{\mathcal T_C F=
\sum_{u\in s}w_u A_u+
\sum_{u\in s}\sum_{v\in s\setminus\{u\}}w_{uv}B_{uv}.}
\tag{4}
\]

There is no further factor `2` on the second sum: it already runs over ordered distinct factor labels. `u≠v` does not mean `z_u≠z_v`; repeated spectral parameters are allowed and are useful for the witness in §6.

## 4. The exact signed-resolvent identities

Put

\[
G_u^+=(H-z_u)^{-1},\qquad
G_u^-=(H-\bar z_u)^{-1}=(G_u^+)^*,\qquad
R_u^\sigma=(G_u^\sigma)^2.
\]

The adjoint equality uses Hermiticity. It is the same identity used in the proof at `OUComparisonHessian.lean:896`; it is not the false entrywise identification of `G*` with `conj G` without a transpose. Define only for this report

\[
K_1^{\sigma\tau}(z)=M^{-1}\sum_{a,b}(R_z^\sigma)_{aa}C_{ab}(G_z^\tau)_{bb},
\]

\[
K_2^{\sigma\tau}(z_u,z_v)=M^{-2}\sum_{a,b}(R_u^\sigma)_{ab}C_{ab}(R_v^\tau)_{ba}.
\]

Then the existing definitions are exactly

\[
L_j=\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in\{+,-\}}|K_j^{\sigma\tau}|.
\tag{5}
\]

Each absolute value encloses the **entire centered `a,b` contraction**. There is no absolute value on `C_ab` and no sum of entrywise absolute values inside a kernel.

### Single-factor contribution

The existing single-factor theorem says

\[
W_{ab}f_u=\operatorname{Im}\left[
M^{-1}\bigl((R_u^+)_{aa}(G_u^+)_{bb}
+(R_u^+)_{bb}(G_u^+)_{aa}\bigr)\right].
\]

The second summand equals the first **after contraction**, by swapping `a,b` and using `C_ab=C_ba`. Real coefficients commute with imaginary parts. Therefore

\[
A_u=2\operatorname{Im}K_1^{++}(z_u)
=\frac{K_1^{++}(z_u)-K_1^{--}(z_u)}{i},
\tag{6}
\]

where real values are included in `ℂ` and `K_1^{--}=\overline{K_1^{++}}`. In particular,

\[
|A_u|\leq |K_1^{++}|+|K_1^{--}|\leq L_1(z_u).
\tag{7}
\]

The mixed `+-` and `-+` choices are nonnegative additional terms in the paper's majorant. They need not appear with nonzero coefficients in the exact single-factor identity. Demanding an equality with all four absolute contractions would be a wrong target.

### Cross-factor contribution

The existing first-derivative theorem gives

\[
D^u_{ab}=\frac{i}{2M}\bigl((R_u^+)_{ba}-(R_u^-)_{ba}\bigr).
\]

Substitution into `B_uv`, followed by one **simultaneous** `a,b` swap, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_{uv}
&=-\frac1{4M^2}\sum_{a,b} C_{ab}
 \bigl((R_u^+)_{ba}-(R_u^-)_{ba}\bigr)
 \bigl((R_v^+)_{ab}-(R_v^-)_{ab}\bigr)\\
&=-\frac1{4M^2}\sum_{a,b}
 \bigl((R_u^+)_{ab}-(R_u^-)_{ab}\bigr)C_{ab}
 \bigl((R_v^+)_{ba}-(R_v^-)_{ba}\bigr)\\
&=-\tfrac14\left(K_2^{++}-K_2^{+-}-K_2^{-+}+K_2^{--}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

This is the missing four-sign identity. The coefficient is `-1/4`, from `(i/(2M))²`; the two powers of `M⁻¹` are precisely those in `paperL2Kernel`. Hence

\[
|B_{uv}|\leq\tfrac14 L_2(z_u,z_v).
\tag{9}
\]

Do not transpose one resolvent alone, replace an entry by its reverse, or take an absolute value before summing `a,b`. None of those operations is justified by Hermiticity of `H` at nonreal `z`.

For `Im z_u>0`, the spectral formula gives `f_u≥0`, hence `w_u,w_uv≥0`. Apply the triangle inequality to (4), then (7) and (9), and finally `L_2/4≤L_2`. This proves the mathematical statement (D) with an absolute constant `1`. The intermediate `1/4` is normalization bookkeeping, not a request for a stronger final theorem than the paper needs.
