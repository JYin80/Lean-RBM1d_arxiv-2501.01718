## 1. Gaussian IBP: the weighted diagonal exception matters

Step 1, (4.2)/(4.3), and removal of the weak-law indicator give

`max_ij |G_ij-m δ_ij| ≺ sqrt(b_u)`.

Since `ell_s η_u≤ell_s η_s≤1`, `W^-1≤r_u/A_u`; hence `b_u≤2r_u/A_u`. This remains true when ell saturates at L. All these scales are deterministic and polynomially bounded above/below on the prescribed window.

Conditional expectation preserves stochastic domination at such a deterministic scale when the variable has all polynomial moments: Jensen bounds conditional high moments and Markov yields arbitrary failure powers. Apply this to products of entries; the global resolvent bound and Gaussian norm moments control exceptional samples. Thus

`E_i[δ_i δ_k]=O_prec(b_u)` uniformly in i,k.

The exact complex Gaussian row IBP identity in the paper and `Gauss.IBP.condExpDiag_eq_sum_Sblk` is

`x_i:=E_iδ_i = u m(E) sum_k S_ik E_i[G_ii δ_k]`.

For k≠i the rank-one identity gives `G_kk-G_kk^(i)=G_ki G_ik/G_ii`. On the weak-law event the denominator is bounded away from zero, so this difference, and its conditional expectation, are `O_prec(b_u)`. Exceptional samples are controlled by polynomial resolvent/minor/norm moments. Because `G_kk^(i)` is row-i measurable,

`E_iδ_k-δ_k=O_prec(b_u), k≠i`.

For k=i that assertion is not available: only `E_iδ_i-δ_i=O_prec(sqrt(b_u))` follows. Keep this term in the S-weighted sum. Since `S_ii≤W^-1` and `b_u≥W^-1`,

`S_ii sqrt(b_u)≤b_u`.

Consequently the correct aggregate statement is

`x_i=u m(E)^2 sum_k S_ik δ_k + O_prec(b_u)`.

The named fluctuation-averaging input applied to the row weights replaces the sum of δ_k by the sum of x_k with error `O_prec(b_u)`. The bulk stability bound for `(I-u m(E)^2S)^-1` then gives `max_i|x_i|≺b_u`. Applying fluctuation averaging to the block weights gives

`max_a |<(G_u-m(E)) E_a>| ≺ b_u ≍ r_u/A_u`.

**Interface distinction:** `Gauss.ibpRem(i,k)` is literally `E_i[G_ii δ_k]-m δ_k`. The all-pairs convenience premise in `condExpDiag_stochDom_of_ibpRem` is stronger than this proof supplies at k=i. The weighted sum is what the exact row equation needs. One should formalize that sum, treating k=i separately, rather than claim the pairwise remainder is `O_prec(b_u)`. No assumption `W b_u≺1` is needed.

## Addendum — independent audit of the adapted [40] input

**Updated mathematical verdict: proved by adaptation of the known source argument, for the deterministic control needed by A′.** The conditional input in the original verdict is discharged at the mathematical level by T328 §§A–E with the details below. Primary source checked directly on 2026-09-22: Erdős–Knowles–Yau–Yin, *The Local Semicircle Law for a General Class of Random Matrices*, author PDF dated May 24, 2013, https://www.unige.ch/~knowles/SC.pdf, 62 PDF pages. Exact locators: Theorem 4.6, second estimate (4.11); Appendix B Lemmas B.1/B.2, (B.9)–(B.18), Remark B.3, and the direct-diagonal modification immediately following that remark (printed pp. 50–56). This is a primary-source audit using the literature-check skill, not a citation inferred from T328. No source cache or other project file was modified.

The source theorem is not applicable verbatim: its spectral domain requires η≥M^-1, and its original variance matrix is stochastic. The needed extension is valid because the direct-diagonal proof requires independent underlying entries, minor identities, deterministic entry control with a polynomially small upper bound and lower bound W^-1/2, and polynomial moment envelopes. The resolvent expansion and its cancellation do not require the variance row sum to equal one. The source itself explicitly describes the direct-diagonal replacement of its centered inverse-diagonal base estimate.

Here is the actual adaptation, reconstructed independently. Fix an even moment order 2p first, and work with minors deleting at most O(p) labels. An event on which every full-matrix entry differs from mδ_ij by at most N^εΨ, with ε smaller than the fixed upper-decay exponent, gives the same minor bounds by repeated rank-one deletion. Each surviving off-diagonal correction is O(N^(2ε)Ψ²), while each diagonal remains within o(1) of |m|=1. A finite-minor union costs only a polynomial factor at fixed p. Thus inverse minor diagonals are bounded on this event.

On its complement, every minor resolvent is bounded by η^-1≤N^K. Schur's complement gives

`|1/G_ii^T|≤|H_ii|+|z|+η^-1 sum_(j outside T∪{i}) |H_ij|²`.

Gaussian moments imply a polynomial bound for every fixed moment of this expression, uniformly for u∈[0,1]. Every monomial of the fixed-p expansion therefore has polynomial moments. A larger failure exponent pays the exceptional set without altering its favorable Ψ degree. Conditional Jensen and high-moment Markov prove the same assertion after any of the finitely many conditional projections. This replaces the source's η≥M^-1 usage completely; no hidden η^-1 remains in the favorable bound.

For explicit degree accounting, expand each centered diagonal using deletion identities until all singleton labels are deleted wherever they are not lower indices, or a fixed cap of 2p+1 off-diagonal factors is reached. The initial centered diagonal costs Ψ. Its first correction has two off-diagonal factors; each further correction increases their number by at least one. A surviving singleton label must appear as a new lower index in another factor: otherwise that other product is measurable outside the singleton row, and its expectation against the centered factor is exactly zero. Assign each singleton to such a partner. Each introduced distinct lower index needs a separate correction, so a factor receiving b partners costs at least Ψ^(1+b). A cap-terminated factor already pays Ψ^(2p+1), sufficient for every remaining singleton. This proves the joint product cost Ψ^(2p+a) for a singleton labels.

For a multiplicity partition with r distinct labels, its absolute coefficient sum is at most `W^(-(2p-r))`. Since `a≥2r-2p` and `W^-1≤Ψ²`, its contribution is bounded by

`W^(-(2p-r)) Ψ^(2p+a) ≤ Ψ^(6p-2r+a) ≤ Ψ^(4p)`.

The last inequality uses Ψ≤1. There are only finitely many partitions and expansion leaves at fixed p. High moments therefore give the required centered weighted average at scale Ψ²; source tolerances can be made arbitrarily small after p is selected.

Shared edges do not defeat that cancellation. On the product space of independent upper-triangular real/imaginary coordinates, let R_i be the set of coordinates incident to row i. P_i integrates exactly R_i. Then `P_iP_j=P_jP_i=P_(R_i union R_j)`, including their shared edge, because integrating a coordinate twice is idempotent. If a minor expression omits row i, applying P_j or Q_j preserves that measurability. Hence the singleton cancellation is exact before any exceptional-event split. One must not multiply by a good-event indicator inside that cancellation.

Scaling H_u=sqrt(u)X changes neither these coordinate sets nor the deletion identities. The diagonal base estimate is `Q_i(G_ii^T-m)=O_prec(Ψ)`, obtained directly from deterministic entry control and conditional moments, so it has no division by u and no variance-row normalization. At u=0 the centered quantity is identically zero. The deterministic center need only be bounded and bounded away from zero for this argument; m(E), of modulus one, meets that requirement even though it is not the unit-variance semicircle transform at z_u.

Finally, uniformity in deterministic u is not silently promoted to a simultaneous event. On a Gaussian norm event of arbitrary polynomial probability, the resolvent identity gives a polynomial Hölder-1/2 modulus in u, also for row-conditional averages by integrating the resampled row. All needed weights are time independent. The deterministic Φ=Ψ² satisfies Φ≥N^-1 and has polynomial modulus. Choose the time mesh fine enough to make interpolation errors at most N^-3, then choose fixed p for the required mesh-plus-index failure exponent, and only then choose source tolerances and exceptional exponents. This proves the simultaneous-time assertion. T328's order of choices is consistent.
