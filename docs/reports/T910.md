## Symmetry implication

For `k := W - 1 - j`, natural arithmetic gives `k < W` and `k+j+1=W`. Put
`x := semicircleLambda W hW j hj`. The midpoint CDF equation and reflection
give

```text
F(-x) = 1 - (j+1/2)/W = (k+1/2)/W.
```

The last equality is ordinary real arithmetic after casting `k+j+1=W` and
using the positive denominator `W`. The quantile range theorem puts `x` in
`[-2,2]`, hence so is `-x`; the reflected level is in `[0,1]` because
`k<W` and `W>0`. The actual midpoint `lambda_k` also lies in `[-2,2]` and
has that same CDF value by `semicircleLambda_cdf`. Applying
`existsUnique_semicircleCDF_eq` to the two subtype points proves

```lean
semicircleLambda W hW (W - 1 - j) (by omega) =
  -semicircleLambda W hW j hj
```

with exactly the required `W ≥ 1` and `j < W` hypotheses. No endpoint is
excluded: `W=1,j=0` is included, and all divisions are justified by `hW`.

For `W=2m+1`, the complementary index simplifies to
`(2*m+1)-1-m=m`. The symmetry equation identifies the central midpoint with
its negative, so linear arithmetic proves
`semicircleLambda (2*m+1) (by omega) m (by omega) = 0`.

## Nondegenerate finite-width checks

- `W=1`: `semicircleQuantileCell_witness_one` supplies endpoints `-2,2`, a
  midpoint strictly inside the cell, and cell mass `1`. The odd-center result
  specializes to its midpoint being zero.
- `W=2`: reflection pairs index `0` with `1`. The existing
  `semicircleQuantileCell_witness_two` gives strict placement of both
  midpoints in their consecutive cells and mass `ENNReal.ofReal (1/2)` for
  each. Thus both cells have strictly positive measure. The scratch check also
  derives `lambda_0 < lambda_1` from the strict placement inequalities, so
  the two representatives are genuinely nonconstant.

These witnesses verify the finite-index and level hypotheses are satisfiable;
the uniqueness argument is not made true by empty cells or impossible
conditions.
