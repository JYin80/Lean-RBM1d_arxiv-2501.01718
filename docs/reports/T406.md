Shared Hermitian edges do not invalidate the conditional projections; collisions have the correct weight cost; the diagonal IBP exception is harmless after retaining its variance weight; and a polynomial time net gives one measurable HighProb event for all actual row/block labels and every time in the window. ## 1. Exact claim card

Let `L_N>=3`, `W_N>=1`, and let the matrix dimension `n_N = L_N W_N` be bounded by a fixed power of `N`. Work with the actual product Gaussian model:

* `X_ij`, `i<j`, are independent circular complex Gaussians, with real and imaginary variances `S_ij/2`; `X_ii` are independent real Gaussians of variance `S_ii`.
* `X_ji = conjugate(X_ij)`. The underlying independent variables are the real coordinates for unordered edges, not independent oriented rows.
* `S = Sblk`, so `S_ij >= 0`, `sum_j S_ij = 1`, and `S_ij <= W^(-1)`. For `H_u = sqrt(u) X`, the variance row sum is `u <= 1`.
* Fix `0 < kappa <= 1` and `|E| <= 2-kappa`; write `m=m(E)`, `|m|=1`, `z_u=E+(1-u)m`, `eta_u=Im(z_u)`. Then `m(um+z_u)=-1`.
* The deterministic window is `I_N=[s_N,t_N] subset [0,1)`, with `inf_(u in I_N) eta_u >= N^(-K_eta)` eventually.

For a deletion set `T` and indices outside `T`, let `G^T(u)=(H_u^T-z_u)^(-1)`. Let `P_i` integrate all independent coordinates incident to `i`, holding the other coordinates fixed, and let `Q_i=1-P_i`. Put

```text
v_i(u) = G_ii(u)-m,
Z_w(u) = sum_i w_i Q_i v_i(u),
Phi_N(u) = Psi_N(u)^2.
```

The required local-law input is the following uniform deterministic-time statement. For every `tau>0` and `D>0`, there is `N_0(tau,D)` such that, for every `N>=N_0` and every deterministic `u in I_N`,

```text
P(max_(i,j) |G_ij(u)-m delta_ij| > N^tau Psi_N(u)) <= N^(-D).       (LL)
```

The threshold is independent of `u`. The scales are deterministic and, eventually throughout `I_N`,

```text
N^(-K_Psi) <= Psi_N(u) <= 1,
W^(-1) <= Psi_N(u)^2.                                           (SC)
```

The first conclusion is: for each fixed integer `p>=1` and each `epsilon>0`,

```text
E |Z_w(u)|^(2p) <= C_(p,epsilon) N^epsilon Phi_N(u)^(2p),         (FA-M)
```

eventually, uniformly in deterministic `u` and in every deterministic complex coefficient vector satisfying

```text
max_i |w_i| <= W^(-1),       sum_i |w_i| <= 1.                    (W)
```

This gives a fixed-time domination bound uniform in the choice of deterministic `w`. It does **not** put the supremum over the entire class (W) inside the probability. For the joint event, select the actual polynomial-size family of `S` rows and block weights `W^(-1)1_(i in I_a)`. These weights are independent of time and sample. More general time-dependent deterministic weights require an additional uniform modulus in their total absolute mass; (W) alone does not provide one.

For the continuum conclusion assume `Phi` has a polynomial modulus, for example

```text
|Phi_N(u)-Phi_N(v)| <= C N^B_Phi |u-v|^beta,
beta>0,
```

uniformly in the window. This is verified directly for the actual A′ scale in §9. Then for every `epsilon>0` there is a single measurable event `Good_N(epsilon)`, independent of the requested failure exponent, which is HighProb and on which

```text
sup_(u in I_N) max_(actual row/block w) |Z_w(u)| / Phi_N(u)
  <= N^epsilon
```

eventually. On the same type of event, weighted IBP and the fixed-bulk `Sblk` stability give

```text
sup_(u in I_N) max_a
  |W^(-1) sum_(i in I_a) (G_ii(u)-m)| / Phi_N(u) <= N^epsilon.   (AVG)
```

The event and eventual threshold may depend on `epsilon`; the HighProb definition then quantifies over every fixed failure exponent. Neither the moment order nor a probability-loss exponent is allowed to grow with `N` in the fixed-moment argument.

## 2. Conditional algebra, including the shared edge

Write `R_i` for the set of underlying independent real coordinates incident to row/column `i`. Integration over coordinate sets satisfies

```text
P_i P_j = P_(R_i union R_j) = P_j P_i,
P_i^2=P_i,       P_i Q_i=0,
||P_i f||_q <= ||f||_q,       ||Q_i f||_q <= 2||f||_q  (q>=1).  (PQ)
```

The overlap `R_i intersect R_j` contains the real and imaginary coordinates of their shared edge. Integrating such a coordinate twice has exactly the same effect as integrating it once. Fubini on the product coordinate law proves (PQ); independence of row random variables is neither asserted nor needed. Degenerate zero-variance coordinates cause no problem.

All resolvent functions at a fixed positive `eta` are bounded and measurable. Thus every finite product and projection word used below is integrable. The projections commute with complex conjugation. At a fixed upper-half-plane spectral parameter one must not replace `G_ji` by `conjugate(G_ij)`; the proof below estimates the two oriented entries separately. Conjugated slots are handled by conjugating the whole identity.

If `j in T`, then `G^T` reads no coordinate in `R_j`; consequently `P_j G^T=G^T` and `Q_j G^T=0`. Applying other `P/Q` letters preserves the required invariance or centering, by commutation.

These are the exact properties implemented in `Gauss/CondRow.lean` and `Gauss/FlucIter.lean:279–433`, particularly `condPred_condPred`, `condRow_condRow_comm`, and `condRow_applyOps_qRow`. The product space has auxiliary unused coordinates, so the identification with conditioning on the minor is needed only for functions of the actual finite matrix. The proof does not identify the entire infinite-product outside-row sigma algebra with the sigma algebra of one minor.

## 3. Finite deletion differences and complement moments

First work where `Psi<=N^(-a)` for a fixed `a>0`. For a fixed deletion budget `M`, choose the entry tolerance `tau<a`, and set `delta=N^tau Psi`. On the event in (LL), every minor with at most `M` deleted labels satisfies

```text
|G_ab^T-m delta_ab| <= C_M delta,
|G_aa^T| >= 1/2,       |1/G_aa^T| <= 2,                        (MIN)
```

eventually, simultaneously over its surviving indices. Here is the deterministic induction: if the entry error at a deletion level is `d<=1/2`, the exact rank-one identity gives the next error at most `d+2d^2`. Starting from `delta -> 0`, any fixed number of steps remains `O_M(delta)`. This proves the finite-minor estimate from the full-matrix event; no independent minor local law is assumed and no all-sample smallness hypothesis is introduced.

For distinct other labels `U`, disjoint from `{i}`, define

```text
Delta_U v_i = sum_(T subset U) (-1)^|T| (G_ii^T-m).
```

The claimed gain is

```text
||Delta_U v_i||_q <= C_(r,q,epsilon) N^epsilon Psi^(r+1),
r=|U|,                                                     (D_r)
```

for each fixed integer `r>=0` and fixed `q>=1`, uniformly in the labels and deterministic time. For `r=0`, this is (LL) converted to a moment estimate. For `r>=1`, the first difference is

```text
G_ii^T-G_ii^(T+j) = G_ij^T G_ji^T / G_jj^T.                 (D1)
```

For an additional new deletion label `k`, apply the exact discrete product rule

```text
Delta_k(f_1 ... f_l)
  = sum_(h=1)^l (product_(b<h) f_b^(+k))
                  (Delta_k f_h) (product_(b>h) f_b).
```

The two atom rules are

```text
Delta_k G_ab^T = G_ak^T G_kb^T / G_kk^T,
Delta_k (1/G_aa^T)
  = -G_ak^T G_ka^T / (G_aa^T G_aa^(T+k) G_kk^T).            (AT)
```

Their index conditions are material: the new `k` differs from `i` and every earlier deletion label, hence differs from every endpoint already present in the monomial. Thus replacing an off-diagonal atom adds one off-diagonal factor, and replacing an inverse-diagonal atom adds two. A shifted atom preserves its off-diagonal status and is evaluated in an admissible minor. Each monomial in an `r`-fold difference has at least `r+1` off-diagonal factors and bounded inverse-diagonal factors. There are finitely many monomials, with number and degrees depending only on `r`; for `r>=1`, the number of off-diagonal factors is at most `2r`.

For example, writing an upper deletion label as a superscript, the second difference is exactly

```text
Delta_{j,k} G_ii
 = G_ik G_kj G_ji / (G_kk G_jj)
   + G_ij^(k) G_jk G_ki / (G_kk G_jj)
   - G_ij^(k) G_ji^(k) G_jk G_kj
       / (G_jj G_jj^(k) G_kk).
```

The first two terms have three off-diagonal factors; the last has four. No coincident-label term has been discarded. Coincidences are handled later by equality partitions, rather than by applying (AT) with inadmissible labels.

On the entry event, (MIN) and (AT) therefore give

```text
|Delta_U v_i| <= C_r N^(c_r tau) Psi^(r+1),
```

where `c_r` is fixed. The tolerance `tau` can be made arbitrarily small after fixing `r,q`.

There is a particularly direct way to pay the complement. The original difference itself, before its rational expansion, satisfies

```text
|Delta_U v_i| <= 2^r (eta^(-1)+1) <= C_r N^B
```

on every sample, because all principal-minor resolvents have the same spectral envelope. Consequently its bad-event contribution to the `L^q` norm is at most

```text
C_r N^(B-D_0/q).
```

Choose `D_0/q > B+K_Psi(r+1)+1` after fixing `r,q`; (SC) makes this smaller than the desired scale. This proves (D_r). It also avoids any need to bound the rational monomials individually off the good event.

T403's alternative use of inverse-diagonal moments is valid: Schur complement gives

```text
|1/G_ii^T| <= |H_ii|+|z|+eta^(-1) sum_(j outside T+i) |H_ij|^2,
```

whose fixed moments are polynomial under the Gaussian row-variance bound. That alternative is unnecessary for this proof. No cancellation is ever performed after inserting the entry-event indicator into the product.

The repository's `MinorDiffGain.norm_minorDiff_greenSetDiagCentered_le` at line 1070 has precisely the distinct-label and finite-budget side conditions and the exponent `r+1`. The argument above establishes the mathematical estimate directly, without treating a `MinorGood` or `FlucGain` structure as an unexplained input.

## 4. The complete `2p` moment expansion

Fix `p>=1`, and expand

```text
E |sum_i w_i Q_i v_i|^(2p)
 = sum_(i_1,...,i_(2p))
      w_(i_1)...w_(i_p) conjugate(w_(i_(p+1))...w_(i_(2p)))
      E[product_(h<=p) Q_(i_h)v_(i_h)
         product_(h>p) conjugate(Q_(i_h)v_(i_h))].              (ME)
```

For a tuple, let `r_0` be the number of distinct labels, and `a` the number of labels appearing exactly once. The combinatorial inequality is

```text
2p >= a+2(r_0-a),       so a>=2r_0-2p.                        (C1)
```

Process the singleton labels in any fixed order. For singleton `j`, leave its unique slot unchanged and insert `P_j+Q_j` into every other factor. In a branch where all those factors carry `P_j`, their product is measurable outside `R_j`. The unique `j` slot still has zero `P_j` expectation: its original `Q_j` is preserved through every earlier projection word by (PQ). The expectation of that branch is zero.

Every surviving branch therefore adds a `Q_j` to at least one different slot. Each singleton is processed exactly once, so the extra `Q` labels on a given factor form a **set** `U_h` of distinct labels, none equal to that factor's original label. There are at least `a` such additions in total:

```text
sum_(h=1)^(2p) |U_h| >= a.                                   (C2)
```

A later projection preserves both earlier extra `Q` letters and the centering needed for the next singleton. An identically zero word causes no problem. The proof does not count repeated applications of the same `Q_j` as additional gains.

For a factor with original label `i`,

```text
Q_U v_i = Q_U Delta_U v_i.
```

Indeed, every term in the alternating sum with a nonempty deletion set is killed by one of its deleted-label `Q` operators. Commute the remaining `P/Q` letters and the original `Q_i` outside this identity. Their `L^(2p)` norm cost is a constant depending only on `p`, by (PQ). The maximal required deletion budget is `2p-1`, and all labels within such a budget are distinct.

Apply (D_r) with `q=2p`, allocating its arbitrarily small losses among the `2p` slots. Hölder and (C2) give, for every surviving branch,

```text
|E(product of its factors)|
  <= C_p N^epsilon Psi^(2p+sum_h |U_h|)
  <= C_p N^epsilon Psi^(2p+a).                                (C3)
```

The conjugated slots obey exactly the same estimate. The number of projection branches is bounded in terms of `p` alone, for example by `2^(2p(2p-1))` before discarding zero branches.

It remains essential to sum the coefficients by equality partition, not by the total number `n_N^(2p)` of tuples. If the class sizes of a fixed equality partition are `b_1,...,b_(r_0)`, then

```text
sum_(distinct labels j_1,...,j_(r_0)) product_l |w_(j_l)|^(b_l)
 <= (max_i |w_i|)^(2p-r_0) (sum_i |w_i|)^(r_0)
 <= W^(-(2p-r_0)).                                           (C4)
```

The first inequality uses `|w|^b <= (max|w|)^(b-1)|w|` and then drops the distinctness restriction in a nonnegative sum. It is valid for signed or complex weights and for zero coefficients. There is no omitted mass factor and no requirement that every admissible weight have support at least `2p`.

Combining (C1), (C3), (C4), `W^(-1)<=Psi^2`, and `Psi<=1`,

```text
W^(-(2p-r_0)) Psi^(2p+a)
 <= Psi^(6p-2r_0+a)
 <= Psi^(4p).                                                (C5)
```

The direction of the last inequality is correct: the exponent is at least `4p`. There are only `Bell(2p)` equality partitions. This proves (FA-M).

Small checks expose the two complementary mechanisms. At `p=1`, a repeated index costs `W^(-1)` and supplies `Psi^2`, while two distinct indices force two additional conditional gains and supply `Psi^4`. At general `p`, the all-paired partition has `r_0=p,a=0` and is paid entirely by collision weights; the all-distinct partition has `r_0=a=2p` and is paid entirely by singleton gains. `p=0` is the separate identity `E|Z|^0=1`, and never enters the gain argument.

## 5. Why the upper assumption can be only `Psi<=1`

Polynomial smallness is needed to derive (MIN), not for the final conclusion. Fix the desired moment loss `epsilon>0` and `p`. Choose fixed positive `a,tau` so small that

```text
2p(a+tau)<epsilon,
```

and take further smaller tolerances when using §3. Split the deterministic `(N,u)` parameter set into two parts.

* If `Psi_N(u)<=N^(-a)`, use §§3–4, with the eventual threshold uniform on this part.
* If `Psi_N(u)>N^(-a)`, the ordinary entry moment bound, projection contraction and the total absolute mass bound give

```text
||Z_w(u)||_(2p) <= 2 max_i ||v_i(u)||_(2p)
               <= C N^tau Psi_N(u)
               <= C N^(tau+a) Psi_N(u)^2.
```

Raising to the power `2p` proves (FA-M) on the second part. The basic entry moment bound follows from (LL), the full resolvent envelope, and the polynomial lower bound exactly as in the complement calculation of §3.

This split depends only on the deterministic scale, never on the sample, so it does not interfere with conditional cancellations. No estimate for high deletion differences is claimed on the second part. In the actual A′ application the whole eventual window lies in the first part.

## 6. Gaussian IBP and the `k=i` exception

For the actual circular complex model, conditional Gaussian IBP gives the exact identity

```text
x_i := P_i v_i = u m sum_k S_ik P_i[G_ii v_k].                (IBP)
```

The complex covariance normalization matters. For `i!=k`, the real and imaginary coordinates each have variance `u S_ik/2`; their derivative terms combine to `-u S_ik P_i[G_ii G_kk]`. For `i=k`, the one real diagonal coordinate has variance `u S_ii`, and its derivative is `-G_ii^2`. There is no extra factor of two. Combining this with `G-m=m(-H-um)G` and `sum_k S_ik=1` proves (IBP). A generic Gaussian Hermitian model with nonzero off-diagonal pseudovariance would require an additional term; the actual model has the required circular covariance.

Put `z_i=Q_i v_i` and define the pair remainder

```text
R_ik = P_i[v_i v_k] + m(P_i v_k-v_k).
```

Then the aggregated remainder is exactly

```text
e_i := x_i-u m^2 (S v)_i = u m sum_k S_ik R_ik.              (REM)
```

For `k!=i`, the minor `v_k^(i)` is `P_i`-invariant, hence

```text
P_i v_k-v_k = (P_i-1)(v_k-v_k^(i)) = -Q_i Delta_i v_k.
```

The one-deletion estimate and projection contraction make this `O_prec(Psi^2)`, uniformly in the pair. Also `P_i[v_i v_k]=O_prec(Psi^2)` by `L^q` contraction and Hölder. These bounds use global moments, not a claim that a smallness event is preserved under resampling.

At `k=i`, the corresponding equation is

```text
P_i v_i-v_i=-z_i,
```

which has only scale `Psi`. It must not be bounded by `Psi^2` without its coefficient. In (REM), however,

```text
S_ii (Psi+Psi^2) <= W^(-1)(Psi+Psi^2) <= 2 Psi^2,
```

using `W^(-1)<=Psi^2` and `Psi<=1`. In fact the leading diagonal scale is at most `Psi^3`. Summing the off-diagonal terms costs only the row mass `sum_k S_ik=1`. Therefore

```text
max_i |e_i| = O_prec(Phi).                                  (E)
```

The same estimates give every fixed moment at an arbitrarily small polynomial loss. If only `Psi<=1` is assumed, the deterministic large-scale branch of §5 applies directly to `e_i=x_i-u m^2(Sv)_i`, whose basic `L^q` bound is `O(N^tau Psi)`; no minor inverse estimate is needed there.

This checks `IBP.condExpDiag_eq_sum_Sblk` and `ibpRem_eq_add` against the exact covariance convention. The actual coefficient-retaining consumer is `CondDom.norm_condExpDiag_sub_le_offdiag` / `DetIBPWeighted.norm_condExpDiag_sub_le_two_phi`. There is no `W Phi= N^o(1)` assumption and no use of the false all-pair remainder bound.

## 7. Dyson conversion and transformed weights

The two required centered averages are `S z` and the block average of `z`. From (REM),

```text
(I-u m^2 S)x = e+u m^2 S z.                                 (DY)
```

Thus a uniform maximum-to-maximum bound for `I-u m^2 S` gives `max_i|x_i|=O_prec(Phi)`, and

```text
|sum_i b_i v_i| <= |sum_i b_i z_i| + (sum_i |b_i|) max_i |x_i|
                = O_prec(Phi)
```

for each actual block weight. The same events can be used throughout.

The needed stability is an internal theorem, `Green/EntryBound.stable_Sblk_short_edge:1254`; its constant `Kstab(kappa)` is independent of `u,N,L,W`. An elementary direct check also suffices and avoids leaving a kernel estimate as an unexamined mathematical leaf. Let `B` average a vector inside each block, and `J` lift a block vector to a block-constant vector. Then

```text
S=J C B,       C=(I+R+R^(-1))/3,
||B||_(infinity->infinity)=||J||_(infinity->infinity)=1,
```

where `R` is cyclic block shift. If `(I-xi S)x=f`, `xi=u m^2`, then `y=Bx` satisfies

```text
(3-xi)y - xi(R+R^(-1))y = 3Bf.
```

Consequently

```text
(|3-xi|-2u) ||y||_infinity <= 3||f||_infinity.                (ST)
```

For the fixed bulk condition,

```text
1-Re(m^2) = 2-E^2/2 >= 2kappa-kappa^2/2 >= 3kappa/2.
```

For `u<=1/2`, `|3-xi|-2u>=3-3u>=3/2`. For `u>=1/2`, use

```text
|3-xi|-2u
 = [3(1-u)(3+u)+6u(1-Re(m^2))] / [|3-xi|+2u]
 >= 3kappa/4.
```

Thus `||y||_infinity<=4 kappa^(-1)||f||_infinity`. Since `x=f+xi JCy`,

```text
||x||_infinity <= (1+4/kappa)||f||_infinity.
```

This weaker constant than the repository's is already sufficient for stochastic domination. It verifies the actual norm and profile, not merely spectral invertibility. A general stochastic variance matrix need not have this uniform bound; for example, an eigenvalue near `-1` can be unstable when `m^2=-1` and `u` approaches one. The fixed `Sblk` and bulk conditions must therefore be retained.

No transformed averaging weights are used in (DY). In particular, the proof never asserts without verification that a row of `(I-u m^2 S)^(-1)`, or a block weight multiplied by that inverse, still has total absolute mass at most one and maximal coefficient at most `W^(-1)`. Maximum-to-maximum stability is applied only after the row-weighted centered estimate has already been proved. This is the exact structure of `norm_condExp_le` and `norm_sum_coef_green_sub_le` in `Green/EntryBound.lean:817–866`.

## 8. The continuum-time event and quantifier order

The same matrix `X` drives every time; no independence between times is used. On a polynomial norm event `B_N={||X||<=N^B_X}`, the resolvent identity and `|sqrt(u)-sqrt(v)|<=|u-v|^(1/2)` give

```text
||G(u)-G(v)|| <= N^(2K_eta) (||X||+1)|u-v|^(1/2).            (H1)
```

Such a fixed polynomial norm event is HighProb. Indeed the Gaussian row-variance bound gives `|| ||X||_F ||_(2q) <= C_q sqrt(n_N)` for every fixed `q`; take `B_X` larger than half the dimension-growth exponent, then choose `q` after the requested failure exponent. The norm threshold itself does not depend on that exponent.

One must not assume that row resampling preserves `B_N`. Instead integrate the resolvent increment before estimating it. The checked repository bound is

```text
P_i[||X(resampled)||](omega)
  <= ||X(omega)|| + 2||X(omega)||^2 + 5/2.
```

It is `CondExpMod.integral_norm_Xmat_rowSplit_le:276`. Together with (H1), it gives a polynomial half-Hölder modulus for `P_i v_i` and hence `Q_i v_i`, uniformly in `i` on `B_N`. For all actual weights, summing costs at most their total absolute mass. It also gives a polynomial half-Hölder modulus for `e_i=x_i-u m^2(Sv)_i`; the increment of the scalar `u` costs only the polynomial full-resolvent envelope. Thus there is a fixed exponent `B_F` such that, on `B_N`, every observable needed in the row/block/IBP family has increment at most

```text
C N^B_F |u-v|^(1/2).                                       (H2)
```

Let the total number of labels be at most `N^B_labels`. Choose a deterministic net of mesh at most `N^(-b)` containing both endpoints, with `O(N^b)` points. Choose `b` once so that

```text
B_F-b/2 < -2K_Psi-1,
B_Phi-b beta < -2K_Psi-1.                                  (NET)
```

On nearest net points `v`, (SC), (NET) imply that interpolation costs at most `O(N^(-1)Phi(u))`, and `Phi(v)<=(1+O(N^(-1)))Phi(u)`. For any fixed `epsilon>0`, define `Good_N(epsilon)` to be `B_N` intersected with the finitely many net inequalities at threshold `N^(epsilon/3)Phi(v)` for centered row/block averages and aggregated IBP remainders. This is a measurable event defined independently of `p` or a failure exponent.

For each desired `D`, choose a fixed moment order large enough to pay `N^(b+B_labels)` and `N^(-D)`. For example, applying (FA-M) with moment loss `p epsilon/3` at a threshold `N^(epsilon/3)Phi` gives a tail exponent `p epsilon/3`, which can exceed `D+b+B_labels+2`. Apply the corresponding moment bound to `e_i`, then use a finite union bound. The constants at this fixed `p` are absorbed by enlarging the eventual `N` threshold. Therefore this very same `Good_N(epsilon)` is HighProb.

Interpolation makes its inequalities valid at every real time; the deterministic stability estimate in §7 then gives (AVG), after absorbing the fixed stability constant and interpolation constants into the remaining `N`-power slack. If both resolvent charges are required, conjugation supplies the other charge, or it can be added as a factor-two finite family.

The legitimate order is

```text
fixed model/window/exponents and source local law;
epsilon>0;
fixed mesh exponent b and the event definition;
D>0;
fixed p large enough for D and the net size;
entry tolerances and exceptional-event exponents for that p;
N_0;
all N>=N_0, all net labels, then all continuum times.
```

In particular, one cannot take separate nonuniform fixed-time thresholds, claim an uncountable union bound, take all `p` before the eventual `N` threshold, or choose a different event depending on `D` and call it the one HighProb event. None of those invalid steps is needed here. The distinction agrees with `UnifDomIcc` in `Eq45FlowInputs.lean:125` and the existential-index bad event in `Defs/StochDom.lean:82–95`.

## 9. Actual A′ scale, endpoint, and nondegeneracy

For the intended window,

```text
Phi_u = (ell_u/ell_s)/A_u + W^(-1),       A_u=W ell_u eta_u.
```

The time dependence simplifies exactly:

```text
Phi_u = 1/(W ell_s eta_u) + 1/W.                            (SCALE)
```

Thus a polynomial lower bound for `eta_u`, fixed bulk energy, and `ell_s>=1` give a polynomial Lipschitz modulus for `Phi`, with

