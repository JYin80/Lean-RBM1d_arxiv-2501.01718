## Actual scalar resolvent and primitive formulas

The model's loop is the ordinary trace of the resolvent/block-projector product (`Gauss.loopObs`, `Loop.GLoop.gloop`), with `E_a=P_a/W`. At E=0, `m=i`, `z_h=i(1−h)`, `eta_h=1−h`, and the (+,−) primitive is

`K_(h,a,b)=W^(−1) [(I−h S_B)^(−1)]_(a,b)`.

This is the literal `Kgen_two`/`kTwo` formula, since `m_+m_−=1`. Write `P_h=(I−hS_B)^(−1)`. For L≥3, S_B has nonnegative entries and row sum one. The convergent Neumann series gives

`P_h=sum_(n≥0)h^n S_B^n`, `P_h(a,a)≥1`,

`0≤(P_h−I)(a,b)≤h/(1−h)` for every a,b.

For a real scalar ray X=λI, H_h=sqrt(h)λI, and the actual two-loop is exactly

`L_(h,a,b)(λI)=1_(a=b)/[W(hλ²+(1−h)²)]`.

Indeed G is the scalar `(sqrt(h)λ−i(1−h))^(−1)I`, G† contributes its conjugate, and `Tr(E_a E_b)=1_(a=b)/W`. This calculation checks the factor W: it is 1/W, not 1/(WL), W or 1/W². Hermitian projection in `loopObs` fixes both sample matrices.

The actual prefix normalization is time-specific:

`R_h=eta_0/eta_h=(1−h)^(−1)`,

`jSnorm(h)=(1+max_(a,b)|L_(h,a,b)−K_(h,a,b)|/T_h(dist(a,b)))/R_h⁴`.

The fixed endpoint normalization of the evolved moment family at u_2 is **not** the denominator in this prefix. Each inner term uses its own preceding time u_j. Confusing these two normalizations is unnecessary and would invalidate the advertised exact formula.

## Plateau at the zero sample

At h=0 both L and K equal `1_(a=b)/W`, hence the unregularized error is zero and J_0=1. For 0<h≤1/2, at X=0,

`L_(h,a,b)=1_(a=b)/[W(1−h)²]`.

Subtract I/W first. The scalar resolvent term obeys

`|(1−h)^(−2)−1|=(2h−h²)/(1−h)²≤8h`.

The Neumann remainder is ≤h/(1−h)≤2h entrywise. Therefore for every a,b,

`|L_(h,a,b)(0)−K_(h,a,b)|≤10h/W`.

The tail floor is exactly `T_h(d)≥W^(−D)`, so

`max_(a,b)|L−K|/T_h≤10h W^(D−1)≤1`.

Since R_h≥1, J_h≤2. This uses a deterministic explicit sample and does not duplicate the norm-event common-support argument.

Now evaluate the actual inner smoothing. For j=0,1, define

`z_(j,a,b)=sqrt(|L_(u_j,a,b)−K_(u_j,a,b)|²+epsilon²)/T_(u_j)(dist(a,b))`,

`H_j=(1+(sum_(a,b) z_(j,a,b)^q)^(1/q))/R_(u_j)^4`.

These H_j are precisely T314/T333 `smoothJS`, including the deterministic +1 term. Since epsilon/T≤N^(−10), the already verified elementary smoothing sandwich gives

`J_j≤H_j≤(L²)^(1/q)(J_j+N^(−10))`.

The actual outer norm is `S_2=(H_0^q+H_1^q)^(1/q)`. Thus, at ω0,

`S_2≤(2L²)^(1/q)(max(J_0,J_h)+N^(−10))≤e(2+N^(−10))≤3e`.

As N≥2 and δ>0, `3e<8e²N^(2δ)=Theta`. Therefore the actual cutoff `cutChi(S_2/Theta)` is exactly one; its power is one for every p. Both smoothing levels and their total cardinal factor have been included. This proof does not pretend that the regularized inner norm vanishes at u=0.

## Zero cutoff at an explicit finite scalar sample

Set λ=2/sqrt(h), a positive finite number. Then H_h=2I and

`L_(h,a,a)=1/[W(4+(1−h)²)]≤1/(4W)`.

On the other hand `K_(h,a,a)=P_h(a,a)/W≥1/W`. Hence

`|L_(h,a,a)−K_(h,a,a)|≥3/(4W)`.

At 0≤h≤1/2 and L≥3, the actual diffusion length is

`ell_h=min((1−h)^(−1/2),L)=(1−h)^(−1/2)`.

Consequently the exact diagonal tail and its prefix-normalized denominator are

`T_h(0)=W^(−2)(1−h)^(−1)+W^(−D)`,

`T_h(0)R_h⁴=W^(−2)(1−h)^(−5)+W^(−D)(1−h)^(−4)≤48W^(−2)`.

The last inequality uses D≥2, W≥1, `(1−h)^(−5)≤32`, and `(1−h)^(−4)≤16`. Thus

`|L_(h,a,a)−K_(h,a,a)|/[T_h(0)R_h⁴]≥W/64`.

The regularization only increases the modulus, the inner power norm dominates its diagonal coordinate, and the outer power norm dominates its h-term. Keeping the positive +1 can only help. Therefore

`S_2(ω1)≥H_1(ω1)≥W/64>16e²N^(2δ)=2Theta`.

The actual `cutChi` is zero on [2,∞), so its value is zero here. For p≥1, `0^(2p)=0`. No limiting argument or intermediate-value assumption is needed to produce the second sample.

## Realization in the actual Gaussian sample space

The sample space is the product of real coordinate spaces; Xmat reads the real diagonal coordinate and the real/imaginary upper-triangular coordinates. Define ωλ by assigning λ to every used real diagonal coordinate at this N and zero to every other coordinate (including coordinates belonging to other sizes). The literal `Xentry` definition gives Xmat_N(ωλ)=λI. Take ω0=ω_(0) and ω1=ω_(2/sqrt(h)). These are actual members of the given Gaussian sample space, not a replacement toy space.

The covariance support has positive variance in every used diagonal direction: S_ii>0 (the block variance has the self-loop 1/(3W)). Off-band coordinates of zero variance are fixed at zero in both samples. For the finite set of coordinates on which S_2 depends, the Gaussian product measure has full support on its nondegenerate coordinate subspace. A cylinder neighborhood around either sample, restricted to that subspace, therefore has strictly positive probability. Continuity of S_2 and the strict inequalities S_2<Theta and S_2>2Theta gives neighborhoods on which the respective weights remain exactly one and zero. Such cylinders are measurable.

The scalar curve λ↦weight(ωλ) is C1 by T333. It changes from 1 to 0 on the finite interval `[0,2/sqrt(h)]`. The mean-value theorem supplies λ* in its interior with nonzero derivative. Since χ′ vanishes outside its transition, necessarily

`1<S_2(ω_(λ*))/Theta<2`, `0<weight(ω_(λ*))<1`.

A nonzero directional derivative along this finite sum of positive-variance diagonal directions implies a nonzero covariance-gradient seminorm at that point. This certifies a genuinely active derivative, beyond merely showing the function takes two values. It is not a claim that the transition point belongs to the high-probability Good event; the transition/nonconstancy consumer does not need that stronger statement.

A sufficient general asymptotic formulation is: fix D≥2 and α>0; assume eventually `L_N≥3`, `N^α≤W_N≤N`, `L_N≤N`. Fix `0<δ<α/2`, a deterministic N0, and an integer mesh exponent B>D−1. Set `mesh_N=(max(1,N))^B`, `h_N=mesh_N^(−1)`, and `m_N=max(1,N)`.

For each such N the same two samples work for every p≥1; the cutoff base is independent of p. Thus the order is fixed D,δ,B,N0 and dimension family, then eventual N, then all p≥1. No growth condition in p is required for this witness.
