For a fixed size suppress `N`. In the matrix calculations below, `X` denotes

\[
 S_{(a,p),(b,q)}=\frac1{3W}
       \mathbf1_{a-b\in\{0,1,-1\}}.

On `ZMod L`, with `L >= 3`, this support is exactly distance at most one.

## The single measurable event and its probability

Define

\[
\begin{split}
 \mathcal S_N&=\{X_{ab}=0\text{ for every }\operatorname{dist}(a,b)>1\},\\
 \mathcal B_N&=\{\|X_{ab}\|_{2\to2}\le8
      \text{ for every }a\ne b,\ \operatorname{dist}(a,b)\le1\},\\
 \mathcal E_N&=\mathcal S_N\cap\mathcal B_N.
\end{split}
\]

Every defining map is a continuous function of finitely many coordinate
evaluations. Thus all three sets are measurable. Off-band coordinates read
by `Xentry` have `gvar=0`, including when the entry uses the reversed pair.
Their law is exactly the point mass at zero. Therefore `P(S_N)=1`.
An arbitrary element of `Omega d` need not have those coordinates zero, so
retaining this event factor is essential for the pointwise conclusion.

For fixed distinct adjacent blocks, the real and imaginary coordinates of
all `W^2` entries are distinct independent coordinates. Indeed, an equality
between the unordered pairs `{(a,p),(b,q)}` and `{(a,p'),(b,q')}` forces
`p=p'` and `q=q'`, since `a != b`. The ordering by `idxKey` can only exchange
the two entries of a pair and change the sign of its imaginary coordinate.
Both component variances equal `S_ij/2=1/(6W)`. The reverse block is the
adjoint; there is no independence claim between the two orientations.

For unit vectors `v,w` put `c_pq=conj(v_p) w_q`. Then
`sum |c_pq|^2=1`. Writing each entry as `R_pq+i I_pq`, the coefficients of
`Re sum c_pq X_pq` are `Re c_pq` and `-Im c_pq`. Independence gives

\[
 \mathbb E e^{t\operatorname{Re}\langle v,X_{ab}w\rangle}
 =\exp\!\left(\frac{t^2}{12W}\sum_{p,q}|c_{pq}|^2\right)
 =e^{t^2/(12W)}.
\]

The imaginary part has the same formula. This checks both variance
normalization and circular symmetry directly. Markov's inequality at
`t=6Ws` gives each one-sided real or imaginary tail at `s` at most
`exp(-3Ws^2)`. Since `|Z|>r` implies that a real or imaginary part has
absolute value greater than `r/sqrt(2)`,

\[
 P\{|\langle v,X_{ab}w\rangle|>r\}
       \le4e^{-(3/2)Wr^2}.                                      \tag{1}
\]

A maximal `1/4`-separated subset of the unit sphere in real dimension `2W`
is a `1/4`-net. The disjoint open balls of radius `1/8` around its points
lie inside the ball of radius `9/8`, so its cardinality is at most `9^(2W)`.
For unit `v,w` and approximants `v_0,w_0`, the exact decomposition

\[
 v^*Mw-v_0^*Mw_0=(v-v_0)^*Mw+v_0^*M(w-w_0)
\]

has total error at most `||M||/2`. Taking the supremum yields
`||M|| <= 2 max_net |v_0^* M w_0|`. Applying (1) with `r=4` to both net
indices gives

\[
 P\{\|X_{ab}\|>8\}\le4\cdot9^{4W}e^{-24W}
       =4e^{-c_0W},\qquad c_0=24-4\log9>15.                    \tag{2}
\]

There are `2L` oriented distinct neighboring pairs for `L >= 3`.
A union bound, with no inter-block independence, gives

\[
 P(\mathcal E_N^c)\le8L_Ne^{-c_0W_N}.                         \tag{3}
\]

For `exampleGrow`, the accepted dimension and bandwidth fields give
`L_N <= N` and `W_N >= N^(5/8)` eventually. For each fixed `M>0`,
`c_0 N^(5/8) >= (M+1) log N + log 8` eventually, so (3) is at most `N^-M`.
This proves the exact `HighProb` quantifiers, with the same events for all
failure powers.

The Gaussian conventions used here were additionally checked against the
local Mathlib source
`Mathlib/Probability/Distributions/Gaussian/Real.lean:221,229,492,499`:
its second Gaussian parameter is variance, its zero-variance law is Dirac,
and its MGF has exponent `mu*t+v*t^2/2`.

## Weighted conjugation, all rows and columns, and resolvent constants

Fix `omega in E_N`, `u in [0,1/2]`, and a target block `b`. Let

\[
 \alpha=\log(65/64),\quad f(a)=\operatorname{dist}(a,b),\quad
 D_b=\bigoplus_a e^{\alpha f(a)}I_W,\quad K=D_bH_uD_b^{-1}-H_u.
\]

The cyclic metric triangle inequality gives `|f(a)-f(a')| <= 1` on every
neighboring pair. This holds across the cyclic boundary and at both sides
of an antipode; no linear ordering of the cycle is used. Thus

\[
 |e^{\alpha(f(a)-f(a'))}-1|\le e^\alpha-1=1/64.
\]

An entire diagonal block of `K` is zero since `D_b` acts there by one
scalar. Off-band blocks are zero on `S_N`. Every remaining block has norm
at most `(1/64) sqrt(u) 8 <= 1/8`. Large diagonal blocks of `X` therefore
do not enter this estimate.

For an arbitrary vector `v=(v_a)_a`, put `x_a=||v_a||`. Every row satisfies

\[
 \|(Kv)_a\|\le\tfrac18(x_{a-1}+x_{a+1}).
\]

Squaring and summing checks the column multiplicity as well:

\[
 \|Kv\|^2\le\tfrac1{64}\sum_a2(x_{a-1}^2+x_{a+1}^2)
             =\tfrac1{16}\sum_a x_a^2.
\]

Both shifts permute `ZMod L`, including `L=3`. Hence `||K|| <= 1/4` with
no `L` or `W` loss. This is the norm of the generally non-Hermitian
perturbation; Hermiticity of `K` was not assumed.

For either charge let `A=H_u-z_sigma I` and `G=A^-1`. Hermiticity gives

\[
 \|Av\|\,\|v\|\ge|\operatorname{Im}\langle v,Av\rangle|
      =|\operatorname{Im}z_\sigma|\|v\|^2
      \ge\tfrac12\|v\|^2.
\]

Finite dimension implies invertibility and `||G|| <= 2`. This also agrees
with `norm_Gsig_le` in `RBM1D/Loop/Split.lean:534`. Since `||KG|| <= 1/2`,
the Neumann series for `I+KG` converges and has norm at most `2`. The
factor order is

\[
 A+K=(I+KG)A,\qquad (A+K)^{-1}=G(I+KG)^{-1},
\]

so `||(A+K)^-1|| <= 4`. Similarity, with the same sign of `K`, gives

\[
 D_bGD_b^{-1}=(D_bAD_b^{-1})^{-1}=(A+K)^{-1}.
\]

Compressing its `(a,b)` block costs no norm factor and uses `f(b)=0`:

\[
 \|G_u^\sigma[a,b]\|_{2\to2}
     \le4e^{-\alpha\operatorname{dist}(a,b)}.                  \tag{4}
\]

Each scalar entry is bounded by this block norm. Both charges satisfy
(4), so the actual `gmBlk` does too. All choices of target block `b` are
deterministic consequences of the same event; no additional probability
union is needed.

## From the exact neighbor maximum to the exact far ratio

Let `r=dist(a,b)`. For every term selected by `SB(a,a') != 0`,

\[
 \operatorname{dist}(b,a')\ge r-1.
\]

Applying (4) separately to both orientations and taking the maximum gives

\[
 \operatorname{gsqBlk}_u(a,b)\le16e^{-2\alpha(r-1)}.           \tag{5}
\]

The term `a'=a` is included. A possible diagonal term `a'=b` also satisfies
the bound when `r <= 1`; it has not been silently dropped. On the eventual
far range one has `r>1` anyway.

The literal tail is positive and satisfies `T_{u,60}(r) >= W^-60`.
If `r >= ellStar/2`, then `2r >= (log W)^(3/2) ell_u` and `ell_u >= 1`.
For `W >= 1` this yields

\[
 \frac{\operatorname{gsqBlk}_u(a,b)}{T_{u,60}(r)}
 \le16e^{2\alpha}
       \exp\!\big(60\log W-\alpha(\log W)^{3/2}\big).         \tag{6}
\]

It suffices that

\[
 \log W\ge(61/\alpha)^2,\qquad W\ge16e^{2\alpha}.
\]

Then the right side of (6) is at most `16 exp(2 alpha)/W <= 1`.
These conditions hold eventually since `W_N -> infinity`. Every non-far
term in `jG` is exactly zero. Thus every term in its finite maximum is at
most one, proving the normalized claim. The cutoff has the correct factor
`1/2`; replacing it by `ellStar` would have concealed a factor of two in
this calculation.

The same calculation gives the safe stronger statement: for each fixed
`D,K >= 0`, the same event family satisfies

\[
 \sup_{0\le u\le1/2}(jG_{u,D}-1)\le W^{-K}
\]

eventually, with a threshold depending on `D,K`. Use `D+K+1` in place of
`61`. This is not simultaneous over unbounded `D,K` at one fixed size.

## Uniform time, boundaries, and nondegeneracy

The event concerns only `X_N`. For every sample in it the argument uses
only the uniform inequalities `sqrt(u) <= 1`, `eta_u >= 1/2`, and
`ell_u >= 1`. Consequently its conclusion quantifies over every real time
on the interval. There is no uncountable intersection used to define the
event, no time net, and no differentiability assumption at zero.

At `u=0`, `H=0`; once `ellStar/2>1`, a far pair and each selected neighbor
are in different blocks, giving `gsqBlk=0` and exactly `jG=1`. The proof
allows `u=1/2` since the imaginary part stays nonzero. If the far range is
empty, the finite maximum in `jG` is zero and again `jG=1`.

The eventual qualification is necessary for this particular event. At
`N=0`, `exampleGrow` has `W=1,L=3`. The zero sample lies in `E_0`. At
`u=1/2`, `ellStar=0`, so a diagonal pair is far, `gsqBlk(a,a)=4`, and
`tailT(0)=2+1=3`. Hence `jG >= 1+4/3=7/3>2`. This does not contradict any
eventual assertion and verifies that no all-size conclusion was smuggled
into the argument. It also avoids relying on a convention for `N^-M` at
`N=0`.

The event has positive probability eventually by (3). An adjacent real
coordinate has positive variance `1/(6W)` and is nonzero almost surely;
intersecting with that condition does not reduce this positive probability.
Choose such a sample in `E_N` and `u=1/4`. Then `H_u` is nonzero,
`A_u=W sqrt(3)/2>0`, and the time interval has positive length. Together
with `L_N,W_N -> infinity`, this supplies nondegenerate satisfiability
without using the zero sample as the witness.

For any fixed `tau>0`, the actual definitions give
`firstCellS tau N=0` and
`firstCellT tau N=min(1-W_N^(-tau),1/2)`. The latter is eventually `1/2`.
The cap on `[0,1/2]` therefore supplies the whole first cell and is
independent of the choice of source parameter `tau`.

If `G_N` is another measurable HighProb family, then `G_N intersect E_N`
is measurable and HighProb: asking each family for failure power `M+1`
gives total failure at most `2N^(-M-1) <= N^-M` for `N>=2`.
This certifies the event-intersection step in T401 Section 5. The identities
and estimates that make up its separately named source package and its
near-remainder formula require their own audits.
