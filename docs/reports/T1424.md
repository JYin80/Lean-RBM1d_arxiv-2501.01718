## 1. Exact claim and authoritative source

Write

\[
 F_q(x)=\left(\sum_{i\in I}x_i^q\right)^{1/q},\qquad
 H_q(x)=F_q(x)^{-1},\qquad d=|I|.
\]

On nonnegative vectors this is precisely `Cutoff.smoothMax (q : ℝ) x`
(`CutoffBounds.lean:108`). The proposed conclusion is, for each fixed deletion
order \(r\le M\),

\[
 |\Delta^r F_q(a)|\lesssim_M q^{C_M}N^{C_M b}\Gamma^{1+r/2},
 \qquad
 |\Delta^r H_q(a)|\lesssim_M q^{C_M}N^{C_M b}\Gamma^{-1+r/2}.
\]

The original specification supplies coordinatewise differences of the vectors,
nonnegativity, a positive floor coordinate, \(\Gamma>0\), and even
\(q\ge4M\), but neither quantitative comparison with \(\Gamma\) nor dimension
control. Both omissions matter independently.

The prescribed manuscript formula (4.12) is

\[
 \sum_k t_k(1-\mathbb E_k)(G_{kk}-m)\prec
 \Psi^2=\max_{a,b}\mathcal L_{t,(+,-),(a,b)},
 \qquad |t_k|\le W^{-1},\quad \sum_k|t_k|\le1.
\]

It has a **random, unsmoothed** right-hand scale. On the same displayed source
page, before (4.12), the paper states on \(\Omega(t,c)\)

\[
 cW^{-1}\le\max_{a,b}\mathcal L_{t,(+,-),(a,b)}
 \le CW^{-1}+W^{-c}.
\]

The page was extracted and visually inspected. Formula (4.12) cites [40]; that
citation is not an authorized external proof input in this project. Neither
smoothing nor higher proxy differences occur among the paper's assumptions.
The present review does not promote them to paper hypotheses.

## 2. Two decisive counterexamples, including a fixed floor

### 2.1 A strictly positive floor does not control the reciprocal scale

Fix an even \(q\ge4\), \(M=1\), \(\Gamma=N=1\), and \(0<\varepsilon\le1\).
Take two coordinates, the first a fixed floor, and the one-dimensional cube

\[
 a^\varnothing=(\varepsilon,0),\qquad
 a^{\{1\}}=(\varepsilon,\varepsilon).
\]

Every zeroth coordinate is at most one and every first coordinate difference
is at most one. The floor is identical and strictly positive at both vertices;
also \(q\ge\log2\). Nevertheless

\[
 F_q(a^\varnothing)=\varepsilon,\quad
 F_q(a^{\{1\}})=2^{1/q}\varepsilon,\quad
 |\Delta H_q|=(1-2^{-1/q})\varepsilon^{-1}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

Thus positivity, even with dimension control, is insufficient. If order zero
is included, \(H_q(a^\varnothing)=\varepsilon^{-1}\) already fails. A lower
comparison with \(\Gamma\), with its constant/loss explicitly tracked, is
needed for the stated uniform class. Requiring **the floor itself** to be
comparable to \(\Gamma\) would be unnecessarily strong; comparison of the
whole norm suffices.

### 2.2 Fixed \(q\) does not remove coordinate cardinality

The T1422 report's \(a^0_i=1,a^1_i=2\) example gives exactly
\(|\Delta F_q|=d^{1/q}\). It remains valid even if the floor must be unchanged:
take

\[
 a^0=(1;1,\ldots,1),\qquad a^1=(1;2,\ldots,2)
\]

with \(d\) non-floor coordinates, \(\Gamma=N=1\), and fixed even \(q\ge4M\).
All coordinate differences are at most one, zeroth coordinates at most two,
and both norms are at least \(\Gamma\). But

\[
 |\Delta F_q|
 =(1+2^q d)^{1/q}-(1+d)^{1/q}\sim d^{1/q}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

No constant polynomial in a fixed \(q\) controls this. A quantitative lower
norm bound repairs the first counterexample and does not repair this one.

Strictly speaking, \(\log d\le q\) is a convenient sufficient condition, not
the uniquely weakest one: the coordinate-to-norm factor is exactly
\(\kappa=d^{1/q}\). A fixed bound \(\kappa\le K\), or a permitted explicitly
budgeted bound polynomial in \(q\), suffices. Direct norm-level difference
inputs remove the need for a cardinality assumption entirely.

## 3. A precise corrected theorem and its proof

### 3.1 Minimal usable interface

Fix \(M\), an even natural \(q\ge\max(2,4M)\), a finite nonempty \(I\), and
\(0\le r\le M\). Consider one cube \(a:\{0,1\}^r\to\mathbb R_+^I\).
Fix \(\Gamma>0\) and \(c>0\). Assume:

1. **Vertex lower comparison:** \(F_q(a^v)\ge c\Gamma\) at every vertex.
2. **All shifted faces:** for every nonempty \(J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}\),
   and every fixed choice of its complementary bits,
   \[
   \|\Delta_J a\|_{\ell_q}\le B_{|J|}\Gamma^{1+|J|/2},\qquad B_j\ge0.
   \]
3. Only if the norm conclusion includes order zero, also assume
   \(F_q(a^v)\le B_0\Gamma\).

This norm-level statement needs no dimension condition and no distinguished
floor. For the original coordinatewise interface, instead assume

\[
 |(\Delta_J a)_i|\le A_{|J|}\Gamma^{1+|J|/2},\qquad d^{1/q}\le K,
\]

and put \(B_j=K A_j\). Coordinatewise zeroth bounds give \(B_0=K A_0\).
Use \(K=e\) through the already proved `rpow_card_le_exp_one` when
\(\log d\le q\). A positive fixed floor may be retained for the application,
but cannot replace hypothesis 1.

The lower-comparison constant must not be hidden in a constant alleged to
depend only on \(M\). Either expose its dependence as below, or fix, for
example, \(c=1/2\) in the application-facing corollary.

The phrase “all shifted faces” is substantive: differences solely at the
empty base are not this interface. For deletion sets the condition is imposed
for every relevant disjoint pair \((S,T)\), with the total deletion budget
covering \(|S|+|T|\). No extra assumption \(\Gamma\le1\) is needed here.

### 3.2 Explicit derivatives, including zero coordinates

For \(s\in\{1,-1\}\), put \(F_{q,s}=F_q^s\). At every nonzero vector, use
the even polynomial \(P_q(x)=\sum_i x_i^q\). It is positive off the origin,
so \(P_q^{s/q}\) is smooth there, including when individual coordinates vanish.

Let \(\Pi(k)\) denote set partitions of \(\{1,\ldots,k\}\), and set

\[
 h_k=\sum_{\sigma\in\Pi(k)}|\sigma|!.
\]

For \(1\le k\le M\), ordinary differentiation, Hölder, and the partition
chain rule give the dimension-free multilinear estimate

\[
 |D^kF_{q,s}(x)[v_1,\ldots,v_k]|
 \le h_k q^{k-1}F_q(x)^{s-k}\prod_{j=1}^k\|v_j\|_{\ell_q}. \tag{A}
\]

Here is the coefficient check, so (A) is not a new derivative assumption.
For \(1\le j\le k\le q\),

\[
 |D^jP_q(x)[v_1,\ldots,v_j]|
 \le(q)_j F_q(x)^{q-j}\prod_\nu\|v_\nu\|_{\ell_q}
 \le q^j F_q(x)^{q-j}\prod_\nu\|v_\nu\|_{\ell_q}.
\]

Hölder uses exponents \(q/(q-j),q,\ldots,q\); when \(j=q\), omit the first
factor. For a partition with \(p\) blocks the outer derivative coefficient
satisfies \(|(s/q)_p|\le p!/q\). Powers of \(F_q\) combine as
\((s-pq)+(pq-k)=s-k\), and powers of \(q\) cost at most \(q^{k-1}\).
Summing partitions proves (A).

In fact even \(q\ge2\) suffices for arbitrary fixed \(M\): derivatives
\(D^jP_q\) vanish for \(j>q\), and the remaining terms obey the same argument.
Keeping \(q\ge\max(2,4M)\) is a harmless restriction of the existing ticket,
not a mathematically necessary growth condition. It also treats \(M=0\)
without permitting the undefined intended “zero norm exponent”.

### 3.3 Vertex lower bounds suffice on the whole interpolating cube

Let \(A(t)\) be the multiaffine interpolation of the vertices. It is a convex
combination of nonnegative vectors. One of its \(2^r\) weights is at least
\(2^{-r}\); coordinatewise monotonicity and homogeneity therefore give

\[
 F_q(A(t))\ge2^{-r}c\Gamma\ge\rho\Gamma,
 \qquad \rho=2^{-M}\min(c,1)>0. \tag{B}
\]

There is no common maximizing coordinate requirement, no loss depending on
\(|I|\), and no small-increment assumption in (B). For each nonempty \(J\),
\(\partial_J A(t)\) is a convex combination of the corresponding shifted face
differences, hence

\[
 \|\partial_J A(t)\|_{\ell_q}
 \le B_{|J|}\Gamma^{1+|J|/2}. \tag{C}
\]

The mixed chain rule, (A)–(C), and repeated fundamental theorem of calculus
on the unit cube now prove, for \(1\le r\le M\),

\[
 |\Delta_{[r]}F_{q,s}(a)|
 \le \Gamma^{s+r/2}
 \sum_{\pi\in\Pi(r)}
 h_{|\pi|}\,q^{|\pi|-1}\,\rho^{s-|\pi|}
 \prod_{B\in\pi}B_{|B|}. \tag{D}
\]

Indeed a term with \(k\) blocks has exactly the scale
\(\Gamma^{s-k}\Gamma^{k+r/2}=\Gamma^{s+r/2}\). The norm exponent is
\(1+r/2\); the reciprocal exponent is \(-1+r/2\), as requested. For order
zero use the separate upper/lower assumptions, not the derivative formula.

This proves the missing mathematical implication, with constants depending
only on the fixed order and the displayed comparison parameters. It does not
claim that this argument has been formalized in Lean.

### 3.4 Exact loss accounting

For coordinatewise inputs \(A_j\le A N^{\alpha b}\), with \(A\ge1\),
\(N\ge1\), \(b,\alpha\ge0\), \(K\) fixed, and \(c\) fixed, (D) implies

\[
 |\Delta^rF_q(a)|\le C_{M,c,K} A^r q^{r-1}N^{r\alpha b}
                    \Gamma^{1+r/2},
\]
\[
 |\Delta^rH_q(a)|\le C_{M,c,K} A^r q^{r-1}N^{r\alpha b}
                    \Gamma^{-1+r/2},\qquad1\le r\le M. \tag{E}
\]

The polynomial degree \(r-1\) in \(q\) is sufficient. The input loss exponent
\(\alpha\) generally becomes \(r\alpha\); it must not silently remain the
same named constant. Different order-dependent input losses are handled
exactly by the product over blocks in (D).

An explicitly weaker lower bound \(F_q(a^v)\ge cN^{-\lambda b}\Gamma\)
also works: add at most \((r-1)\lambda b\) to the norm loss and
\((r+1)\lambda b\) to the reciprocal loss. Likewise a bound
\(d^{1/q}\le Kq^\theta\) adds at most \(\theta r\) to the \(q\)-degree.
Unspecified positivity or an uncontrolled dimension factor is not such a
budgeted hypothesis.

## 4. Exact Gaussian specialization: what works and what is still missing

### 4.1 Source definitions and exponent

The unaccepted T1414 source defines

\[
 q_N=2\lceil2\log(N+2)\rceil+8,\qquad
 a^S=\bigl(W_N^{-1};(L^S_{ab})_{a,b\in\mathbb Z_{L_N}}\bigr),
\]

where \(L^S_{ab}\) is the nonnegative zero-embedded principal-minor squared
Hilbert–Schmidt loop with the original \(W_N^{-2}\) normalization
(`RandomLmaxMinorProxy.lean:28–51`). Its proxy is \(F_{q_N}(a^S)\), after the
routine natural-power/real-power and finite-sum identification. The coordinate
type is a single floor plus ordered block pairs, of cardinality \(1+L_N^2\).

The definition gives

\[
 4\log(N+2)+8\le q_N\le4\log(N+2)+10.
\]

Thus \(q_N\) is positive and even for every \(N\), and for **each fixed**
\(M\), eventually \(q_N\ge4M\). Under the eventual geometric fact
\(L_N\le N\),

\[
 \log(1+L_N^2)\le2\log(N+2)\le q_N.
\]

The inspected partial source already writes this eventual count argument at
lines 490–540, using `d.dim` and `d.W_pos`. It is mathematically sound as an
argument, but remains unaccepted source. The accepted abstract cardinality
lemma is in `CutoffBounds.lean:145`.

For fixed \(r\) and every \(\eta>0\), \(q_N^{r-1}\le N^\eta\) eventually.
Hence the logarithmic smoothing factor can be paid from an arbitrary final
positive loss budget. This does not allow the moment/deletion order to grow
with \(N\) without a new estimate.

### 4.2 The floor and the full scale must not be confused

Define at the **same** sample and time

\[
 R=\max_{a,b}L^\varnothing_{ab},\qquad
 \Lambda=\max(W^{-1},R).
\]

The deterministic floor proves global positivity and an inverse envelope
\(F_{q_N}(a^S)^{-1}\le W\). It does **not** prove
\(F_{q_N}(a^S)\ge c\Lambda\) for an arbitrary minor. In particular, it is
wrong to infer \(W^{-1}\ge cR\) from the paper's bound
\(R\le CW^{-1}+W^{-c}\). This would impose a stronger scale than (4.12).

On the paper's event, \(cW^{-1}\le R\), so
\(R\le\Lambda\le\max(1,c^{-1})R\). This is the required comparison back
to the random right side of (4.12). A theorem with right side \(\Lambda\)
alone is insufficient until that same-event comparison is supplied.

### 4.3 A noncircular first-order route to the missing lower bound

Fix a total deletion budget \(B\). Suppose that on one event \(E_N\), for
every relevant shifted first-order edge and every block pair, an actual loop
producer supplies

\[
 |L^{S\cup\{j\}}_{ab}-L^S_{ab}|
 \le C_B N^{\alpha_B\beta}\Lambda^{3/2},\qquad |S|<B. \tag{F}
\]

The scale in (F) is the full-matrix \(\Lambda\) at that very sample. If an
existing producer instead uses a local minor scale, the conversion must be
proved; it cannot be silently read as (F).

If

\[
 BC_B N^{\alpha_B\beta}\sqrt\Lambda\le\tfrac12, \tag{G}
\]

telescoping gives \(|a_i^S-a_i^\varnothing|\le\Lambda/2\). Choose a
maximizing coordinate of \(a^\varnothing\), whose value is \(\Lambda\).
For every \(|S|\le B\), that coordinate remains at least \(\Lambda/2\), and
all coordinates are at most \(3\Lambda/2\). Consequently

\[
 \tfrac12\Lambda\le F_{q_N}(a^S)\le\tfrac32 e\Lambda. \tag{H}
\]

This uses first-order differences only; neither Pr, reciprocal differences,
nor any fluctuation-averaging conclusion is an input. Requiring an all-order
Pr theorem to establish (H) would introduce a circular dependency.

For example, if the same event supplies \(\Lambda\le C N^{-\delta}\) with
fixed \(\delta>0\), choose \(\alpha_B\beta<\delta/2\), and (G) follows
eventually. The paper's displayed loop upper bound supplies such smallness
when the intended bandwidth grows at a positive polynomial rate. Neither a
bandwidth lower-growth assertion nor the actual loop producer (F) was proved
by the files in this review's permitted evidence set. They must remain
explicit inputs at the present acceptance boundary.

Once the actual all-face loop differences through the required finite order
and (H) are available on the same event, (D) applies with \(\Gamma=\Lambda\)
and \(c=1/2\). This is a valid possible specialization of the corrected
theorem. It is not evidence that the specialization is currently accepted.

### 4.4 Quantifiers, time, zero, and a simultaneous witness

The safe order is: fix the total deletion/moment budget, final tolerance, and
failure exponent; choose a smaller positive input tolerance \(\beta\) paying
both (G) and the loss in (E); then take eventual \(N\) satisfying all these
requirements, \(q_N\ge4M\), cardinality control, and \(N\ge2\). For a final
loss \(N^\varepsilon\), one may allocate \(\varepsilon/2\) to
\(M\alpha\beta\) and \(\varepsilon/2\) to the fixed power of \(q_N\).
All vertex/face assertions refer to one event, one full-matrix scale, and one
sample. The event may depend on these fixed parameters.
An entry witness for one tolerance does not by itself prove availability of
the whole event family for arbitrarily small input tolerances, and no single
sample is required to work for every tolerance simultaneously.

No assertion \(\forall N\,\forall M,\ q_N\ge4M\) is intended. At \(N=0\),
the formula gives \(q_0=12\), not arbitrarily large order. The positive floor
defines a positive proxy there (using \(W_0>0\)), but eventual dimension
control \(L_N\le N\) must not be specialized to zero. The analytic theorem
(D) contains no \(N\); its loss corollary uses \(N\ge1\). Using \(N+2\)
instead gives an alternative everywhere-positive bookkeeping scale.

An exact nondegenerate abstract witness is a singleton coordinate type with
\(a^v=\Gamma>0\) at every vertex. Then the norm and reciprocal are
\(\Gamma\) and \(\Gamma^{-1}\), all nonempty differences vanish, and all
hypotheses hold simultaneously with \(c=A_0=1\). Nonzero differences are
not needed for an upper bound. If a varying example is desired, for \(M\ge1\),
\(0<\Gamma\le1\), use two coordinates
\(a^v=(\Gamma,\Gamma+(4M)^{-1}\Gamma^{3/2}|v|)\): it has a fixed positive
floor, nonzero first differences, zero higher vector differences, and
\(F_q(a^v)\ge\Gamma\), with all coordinates at most \(5\Gamma/4\).

For the **actual Gaussian** carrier, the permitted T1419 reconciliation
records the accepted single-\(\tau'\), \(K=1\), first-cell Step-1/T1412
sample, eventually \(N\ge2\), at a deterministic positive interior time.
Intersect its entry event and flow event before choosing the sample. If the
new loop assertions are deterministic consequences on that same intersection,
the same sample witnesses (H) and every finite-cube hypothesis; no new sample
for each minor is allowed. If new high-probability events are used, first
prove their intersection has positive probability, then choose the sample.

**Evidence limit:** the prescribed files do not yet establish (F) and all
higher loop-face estimates on that actual event. Thus this review verifies
an abstract simultaneous witness and the noncircular witness-transfer route,
not a fully verified actual-model witness for the corrected theorem. The
first-cell witness in the reconciliation is not a later-cell entry producer.
No new time-uniform estimate or extension beyond the reported first-cell
window follows from a fixed-time analytic lemma.
