the T615 weight `W = weight … p N k`, and the same-endpoint flow observable
`Y_r = flowY … v r`. The rate terms are T615's `momNormW W p driftAt`, T1225's
piecewise cross budget, and T993's `rateNormW W p qvAt`.

The proof dependency chain is:

1. **Pointwise generator:** T1225's accepted
   `eventually_actual_closedCell_momFlowDeriv_le` supplies the literal
   derivative inequality for each `r ∈ [s_N,v]`, with the same `p,N,k,a`,
   weight, endpoint, and output. Its coefficients are exactly `2p` and
   `p(2p−1)`.
2. **Rate integrability:** T615's actual weighted-drift rate is
   interval-integrable on the target window; T1073's all-order actual drift
   integral producer uses this same integrability component. T993's
   `intervalIntegrable_g` gives interval integrability of the actual weighted
   QV norm for every fixed `p ≥ 1`. The proof uses these rate-integrability
   declarations without importing the additional `BoundsCore` and scheduled
   loss premises required for T1073/T993 profile bounds.
3. **Cross integrability and the endpoint:** T1123 gives eventual
   interval-integrability of the actual positive-time cross budget for each
   fixed `p ≥ 1`, including `k = 0`. The T1225 closed-cell budget equals that
   producer at every time: at `r = 0`, both are exactly zero (the producer's
   `1/√r` coefficient evaluates to zero under Lean's real division-by-zero
   convention); at `r ≠ 0`, this is the defining positive-time branch.
4. **Minkowski closure:** `MomentDuhamel.weightedMinkowski_of_deriv_le`
   requires only the nonnegative actual weight, continuity of its weighted
   `2p` moment, the derivative on the open interval, derivative and rate
   integrability, and prefix integrability of
   `momNormW · (driftNorm + closedCellCrossBudget)`. The existing Gaussian
   continuity and derivative-integrability producers supply the closed-cell
   regularity, including a left endpoint at zero. Its conclusion has exactly
   the requested factor `2` and square-root coefficient `2p−1`.

The `k = 0` case is included in the same theorem: the target endpoint equals
`s_N`, so all interval integrals are zero and the closed-cell argument still
applies. No derivative at `r = 0` is assumed; the generic lemma asks for the
derivative only on the open interval.
