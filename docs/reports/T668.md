## 3. Zero mode: equal-mass quadrature passes

Let

```text
rho_sc(d lambda) = (1/(2 pi)) sqrt(4-lambda^2) 1[-2,2](lambda) d lambda,
F(x)             = rho_sc((-∞,x]),
gamma_j          = F^(-1)(j/W),
lambda_j         = F^(-1)((j+1/2)/W).
```

The semicircle CDF is continuous and strictly increasing on `[-2,2]`.  Hence the cells
`[gamma_j,gamma_(j+1)]` have mass `1/W`, every representative lies in its cell and is strictly
inside `(-2,2)`, and symmetry gives

```text
lambda_(W-1-j) = -lambda_j.
```

For odd `W`, the unique middle representative is `0`; for even `W`, every representative is in
an opposite pair.  The cell endpoints themselves may be `-2` and `2`, but no resolvent singularity
occurs because the imaginary gap is at least `1/2`.

For `u>0`, put `z_u=I(1-u)/sqrt(u)`.  The semicircle Stieltjes transform at `z_u` is
`I sqrt(u)`, since it is the upper-half-plane solution of `m(m+z_u)=-1`.  After the factor
`1/sqrt(u)`, and with `u=0` handled directly, this gives

```text
integral g_u(lambda) rho_sc(d lambda) = I,
0 <= u <= 1/2.
```

Moreover

```text
|partial_lambda g_u(lambda)|
  <= sqrt(u)/(1-u)^2
  <= 2 sqrt(2).
```

The total variation on `[-2,2]` is therefore at most `8 sqrt(2)`.  On each equal-mass cell,
integrate the oscillation from its chosen representative and then sum the cell variations:

```text
|W^(-1) sum_j g_u(lambda_j) - I|
  <= W^(-1) sum_j osc_cell_j(g_u)
  <= Var_[ -2,2 ](g_u)/W
  <= 8 sqrt(2)/W.                                      (Q0)
```

This is uniform on the full closed time interval.  It does **not** use Euclidean widths of the
endpoint quantile cells, so the `W^(-2/3)` width near `+/-2` is harmless.  A naive global
Lipschitz estimate multiplied by the largest cell width would give the wrong `W^(-2/3)` rate;
the variation sum is the required argument.

## 4. Nonzero modes: exact probabilistic-method ledger

For a nonzero internal Fourier mode `q in ZMod W`, define

```text
F_(q,u)(pi) = W^(-1) sum_{j=0}^{W-1}
  g_u(lambda_(pi j)) exp(2 pi I q j/W).
```

For a uniform permutation `pi`, the expectation is zero because each image is uniform and the
nonzero character sums to zero.  Also `|g_u(lambda)|<=2`.  Swapping two images of `pi` changes
the complex sum by at most

```text
W^(-1) * |g_u(lambda_a)-g_u(lambda_b)|
         * |exp(2 pi I q j/W)-exp(2 pi I q k/W)|
<= 8/W.                                                 (P1)
```

Expose `pi(0),...,pi(W-1)` sequentially.  Coupling two conditional completions by the
corresponding transposition shows that every real or imaginary Doob increment is bounded by
`8/W`.  Azuma-Hoeffding, proved internally for this finite exposure, then yields

```text
P(|F_(q,u)| >= r) <= 4 exp(-W r^2/256).                 (P2)
```

The factor `4` is the union over real and imaginary parts, using
`|z|>=r => |Re z|>=r/sqrt(2) or |Im z|>=r/sqrt(2)`.

For time interpolation, the same spectral gap gives, for `u,v in [0,1/2]`,

```text
|g_u(lambda)-g_v(lambda)|
 <= 4(2|sqrt(u)-sqrt(v)|+|u-v|)
 <= 12 sqrt(|u-v|).                                    (P3)
```

Take

```text
h_N = log N/W,
T_N = ceil(1/(2h_N)) + 1,
r_N = 64 sqrt(log N/W).
```

Eventually `0<h_N<1/2`, and the clipped grid of `T_N` points covers `[0,1/2]` within distance
`h_N`.  There are exactly

```text
(W-1) T_N
```

mode/time constraints for one permutation.  If one independently chooses a permutation for each
physical block, the exact cardinality is

```text
K_N = L (W-1) T_N <= 2 N^2
```

eventually, because `LW<=N`, `W<=N`, and `T_N<=2W`.  Inserting `r_N` in (P2) gives a fixed-triple
tail `4N^-16`, so the full independent-block union bound is at most

```text
4 K_N N^-16 <= 8 N^-14 < 1.                            (P4)
```

Thus a deterministic family of block permutations exists.  Interpolation by (P3) adds
`12 sqrt(h_N)`, giving

```text
sup_{b,q!=0,u in [0,1/2]} |F_(q,u)(pi_b)|
  <= 76 sqrt(log N/W).                                 (Q1)
```

There is also a cleaner construction that removes the block factor from the random union bound.
Choose one good `pi`, and for a block representative `b` use a cyclic domain shift
`pi_b(j)=pi(j+r_b)`.  The Fourier sum only acquires the unit scalar
`exp(-2 pi I q r_b/W)`, so its norm is unchanged.  Taking shifts `r_(2k)=0` and
`r_(2k+1)=1` gives distinct permutations on every pair once `W>=2`.  Since the quantile values
are distinct, the paired circulants are then genuinely different.  The independent-block ledger
(P4) remains valid and is the conservative cardinality requested by the audit.

The first uncertain implication is precisely the proof of (P2).  Its derivation is elementary and
the constants above are sufficient, but it is not a current theorem.  Neither a numerical experiment
nor an appeal to an external concentration theorem may replace it.

## 5. Assembly of `A_b`, `M_N`, and the literal predicates

Let `F_W` be the unitary discrete Fourier matrix and set

```text
A_b = F_W^* diag(lambda_(pi_b j)) F_W.
```

Each `A_b` is Hermitian, has spectrum `{lambda_j}`, and has norm `<2`.  Functional calculus gives

```text
g_u(A_b) = F_W^* diag(g_u(lambda_(pi_b j))) F_W.
```

Its internal diagonal entries equal the common zero mode, while every internal off-diagonal entry
is one of the nonzero Fourier coefficients in (Q1).

Pair physical representatives `(0,1),(2,3),...`.  For `c=b+1`, conjugation by

```text
U = [[1/sqrt(2), 1/sqrt(2)],[-1/sqrt(2),1/sqrt(2)]] tensor I_W
```

gives the exact blocks

```text
M_bb = M_cc = (A_b+A_c)/2,
M_bc = M_cb = (A_c-A_b)/2.
```

When `L` is odd, the last representative `L-1` is left unpaired with diagonal block `A_(L-1)`.
The pairing is made on representatives before coercion to `ZMod L`; in particular, no accidental
pair `(L-1,0)` is created.  Reverse entries have physical difference `-1`, so every nonzero entry
is on the literal same/adjacent support.

The resolvent is conjugated by the same pair rotation.  Consequently:

- within a paired diagonal block, the error is the average of the two Fourier errors;
- in a paired off-diagonal block, it is half their difference;
- the cross-block zero mode vanishes exactly because every block has the same eigenvalue multiset;
- an unpaired block has the original Fourier bounds;
- all other physical blocks are zero.

Hence (Q0)-(Q1) give the entrywise estimate in Section 2.  In `Dims.exampleGrow`,

```text
L = N^(1/4+o(1)),  W = N^(3/4+o(1)),
sqrt(log N/W) / W^(-1/6)
  = sqrt(log N) W^(-1/3)
  = N^(-1/4+o(1)) sqrt(log N) -> 0.
```

This proves the strict `goodSetFlow` comparison.  Since the diagonal block trace is unchanged by
the pair rotation and equals the common zero-mode average, (Q0) gives the plus-centered row with

```text
(8 sqrt(2)/W) / (2 N^zetaCtr/W) = 4 sqrt(2) N^(-zetaCtr) -> 0.
```

The minus row follows only now, by same-sample conjugation.  The norm row is also strict eventually:
`norm M_N<2<N`.

## 6. Concrete boundary and scaling checks

The checks below are exact, not numerical evidence.

1. **Left endpoint `u=0`.**  `g_0(lambda)=I` for every `lambda`.  The zero-mode error and every
   nonzero Fourier mode are exactly zero.  Thus the square-root time singularity is only a modulus
   issue and causes no endpoint omission.
2. **Right endpoint `u=1/2`.**  The denominator gap is exactly at least `1/2`; the spatial
   Lipschitz constant is at most `2 sqrt(2)`; `ell_(1/2)=sqrt(2)` and the literal good-event
   threshold is exactly `2^(1/12)W^(-1/6)`.
3. **A concrete dimension value.**  At `N=256`, the actual definitions give `L=4` and `W=64`,
   so there are two representative pairs and no odd leftover.  This is a boundary sanity check,
   not evidence for the eventual inequalities.  For general large `N`, `LW<=N<=2LW` and
   `W>=N^(5/8)` are already proved by `Dims.exampleGrow`; the sharper descriptive powers
   `L=N^(1/4+o(1))`, `W=N^(3/4+o(1))` give the ratios above.
4. **Parity.**  Odd `W` has the central representative `0`; even `W` has only opposite pairs.
   Odd `L` has one unpaired last physical block; even `L` has none.  None of these cases changes
   a Fourier or predicate bound.

No finite numerical experiment was used.  In particular, there is no finite tested range being
presented as proof.
