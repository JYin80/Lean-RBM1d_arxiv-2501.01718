## Claim and dependency audit

`RBM.Gauss.norm_permutationFourierSum_prefixFiber_mean_sub_le` in `RBM1D/Gauss/PermutationFiberMeanRange.lean:20–89` quantifies over every `W : ℕ` with `2 ≤ W`, `k : Fin W`, arbitrary prefix table `p`, distinct `a,b : Fin W` unused among `p i` for `i < k`, and arbitrary complex tables `x,χ` with `‖x i‖ ≤ 2` and `‖χ i‖ ≤ 1` for every `i`. It explicitly requires `0 < card(permutationPrefixFiber W k p a)` **before** either cardinal denominator appears in the conclusion. The conclusion is the norm of the difference of the two complex normalized fiber sums bounded by `8/(W:ℝ)`. T703's cardinality equality transfers positivity to the `b` fiber, so the proof never relies on `0/0`.

The import/proof chain is acyclic:

`PermutationFiberMeanRangeSharpTwo → PermutationFiberMeanRange → PermutationFiberCount → PermutationFiberSwap → PermutationFourierSwap → Mathlib`.

- T676 defines the actual Fourier sum `W⁻¹ ∑ᵢ χᵢ x_{πᵢ}` and proves the exact domain-swap identity. Its two factors are bounded by `2` and `4`, giving `8/W` for `W ≥ 2`.
- T694 defines the actual prefix fibers and the codomain swap `π.trans (Equiv.swap a b)`. The unused-value hypotheses preserve every revealed prefix value; conjugating this image swap to a domain swap gives the T676 pointwise bound.
- T703 transports sums across the exact fiber equivalence and proves equal fiber cardinalities. It does not assert that arbitrary fibers are nonempty.
- T706 rewrites the difference of the two numerators as a sum of pointwise differences, applies the triangle inequality and T694's bound, then divides by the strictly positive common cardinality. The sign reversal is harmless because it uses `norm_sub_rev`.
- T715 imports T706 and proves uniqueness of the `W=2,k=0,p=id` fibers from a permutation's value at zero. They are respectively the identity and transposition singletons, both of card `1`.

## Boundary and sharpness checks

For T715's literal tables `x=(2,-2)` and `χ=(1,-1)`, all table bounds hold and both tables are nonconstant. The identity fiber's Fourier sum and normalized mean are `2`; the transposition fiber's are `-2`. Thus their complex mean difference has norm `4`, and the theorem separately proves `4=8/(2:ℝ)`. Its conclusion states both card equalities and the exact normalized-mean equality, not merely pointwise swap equality or nonempty support. These data instantiate every hypothesis of the T706 bound: at `k=0` both unused-value conditions are vacuous, `0≠1`, and the first fiber has card `1`.

`W=1` admits no distinct candidate pair, so there is no omitted distinct-pair case. An arbitrary inconsistent prefix may make both fibers empty (e.g. `W=3,k=2,p 0=p 1=0,a=1,b=2`); T706 excludes that case through `hpos`, rather than making an unconditional division claim. The explicit `W=2` equality witness establishes nonvacuity and sharpness of the general constant.
