## Exact consumer arithmetic

Using T995's slots

```text
x = N^(δ/8),       R = ratR(E,s,N,v),       Ξ = x^(1/4),
A = 2*cWt^2*x^33*R^10,             ε = 1/(2*cWt^2*x^33*R^10),
q = R^2/2,       β = 1/(2*cWt*x^16*R^2),
γ = 1/(2*cWt^(3/2)*x^24*R^4),      Jv = cWt*x^16*R^4/2,
```

direct substitution into the literal definition of `APrimeOneStep.driftTerm`
gives

```text
driftTerm(m,x,R,Ξ,A,ε,q,β,γ,Jv) / R^4
  = ((3 + 1/√2)/(4*m)) * x^(5/4)
    + exp(1) * (18/m + 1/2) * x^(-3/4) * R^(-4).
```

The leading coefficient is approximately `0.9268/m`, and
`x^(5/4) = N^(5δ/32)`. The second summand tends to zero relative to that
scale when `R` stays near one. Thus the exact consumer has a fixed leading
coefficient at the same power as T1219's estimate.

## Why the checked producers do not close the fit

T1073 bounds the actual `p=1` drift integral by
`(11 / Im(mE) + 3) N^(5δ/32) R^(-2)`. T1217 bounds the full literal
positive-time cross integral by `Ccross N^(5δ/32) R^(-2)`, with `Ccross > 0`.
T1219 combines them into the same-scale upper bound with

```text
C = 2 * (|11 / Im(mE) + 3| + Ccross + 1).
```

At the nondegenerate T995 model witness `E=0`, `Im(mE)=1`. The combined
coefficient is then greater than `30`, while the consumer's leading
coefficient at `m=1` is less than `1`. When `R` is close to one, the factor
`R^(-2)` does not bridge this coefficient gap, and the consumer's vanishing
`x^(-3/4) R^(-4)` remainder cannot absorb it. T995's first positive cell has
`s=0` and endpoint tending to zero, hence `R=1/(1-v) → 1`; an all-cell proof
cannot assume a large endpoint ratio. For large `R`, T1219's bound would fit
the leading term once `R^2 ≥ m C / ((3+1/√2)/4)`, but that only covers part of
the endpoint range. Cond272Reg supplies an upper bound on `R`, not the lower
bound needed here.

The T995 schedule does leave strict exponent margins in several component
proofs (for example, the near-tail row is already bounded at `N^(δ/8)`, and
the deterministic cross-profile calculation uses exponent
`zetaSrc + 2*(δ/100)`). Those observations suggest that a sharper complete
literal drift-plus-cross theorem may be possible. The accepted public
conclusions T1073, T1217, and T1219 have already absorbed those margins into
`N^(5δ/32)` and retain only fixed coefficients. Recovering a lower-power
bound for every full-profile row and for the full positive-time cross budget
would require a new proof; it does not follow from their stated inequalities.
I did not infer the exact consumer fit from the exponent slack alone.
