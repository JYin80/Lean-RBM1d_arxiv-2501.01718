## Decisive gap

For measurable `C_N=commonEvent_N` and `T_N=transition_N`, a mass argument
would follow from

```text
P(T_N) > P(C_Nᶜ),
```

since then `P(T_N ∩ C_N)>0`. T579 provides, for each **fixed** `A>0`, an
eventual `P(C_Nᶜ)≤N^(−A)`. T1065 provides only `P(T_N)>0`, with no radius or
mass lower bound for its open cylinder. Neither statement permits choosing
`A` as a function of `N`. The abstract values
`P(C_Nᶜ)=exp(−√N)` and `P(T_N)=exp(−N)>0`, with `T_N⊆C_Nᶜ`, satisfy these two
types of assertions and have empty intersection. This demonstrates why the
two exported probability facts alone cannot prove the claim; it is not a
counterexample to the actual Gaussian events.

The alternative direct-support route needs a positive-mass measurable
cylinder `U_N⊆rawCarrier_N∩transition_N` at the **same** parameters. Then
`FiniteSupportCore.direct_toMeasurable_retention` would give
`P(U_N∩commonEvent_N)=P(U_N)>0`. No reviewed theorem certifies all raw
carrier inequalities and strict transition with stable margins at one
zero-variance-compatible center. T1065 certifies only the transition margin.
The cheapest next check is to derive either a quantitative lower bound for
the exact T1065 cylinder beating the T579 complement, or a strict carrier
and transition cylinder at one center. The former requires actual
quantitative continuity/radius estimates; qualitative openness does not
suffice.
