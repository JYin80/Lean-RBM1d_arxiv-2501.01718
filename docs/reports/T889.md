## Exact cellwise argument

Fix `W:ℕ`, `1≤W`, and real `u` with `0≤u≤1/2`. Write `b=1-u`, `a=√u`, `g_u(x)=(a x-i b)⁻¹`, `γ_j=F⁻¹(j/W)` for `0≤j≤W`, `λ_j=F⁻¹((j+1/2)/W)` for `j<W`, and `C_j=(γ_j,γ_(j+1)]`. T883 must expose, with the positive `W` hypothesis visible, `γ_0=-2`, `γ_W=2`, `γ_j<λ_j<γ_(j+1)`, and `μ(C_j)=ENNReal.ofReal (1/W)`. Its real mass is then `μ.real(C_j)=1/W`. In particular the first and last cells are positive-mass cells, even though their outer endpoints are density zeros. The cells are pairwise disjoint and their finite union is `(-2,2]`; the omitted point `{-2}` and the exterior of `[-2,2]` are μ-null. This proves that the full integral equals the sum of integrals over **all** cells. Avoid a claim that the half-open union alone equals `[-2,2]` as sets.

The denominator satisfies `‖a x-i b‖≥b≥1/2` for *every* real `x`; there is no support or endpoint exception. The algebraic resolvent identity gives

`g_u(s)-g_u(x) = a(x-s) / ((a s-i b)(a x-i b))`, hence

`‖g_u(s)-g_u(x)‖ ≤ (a/b²)|s-x| ≤ 2√2 |s-x|`.

For the last inequality, `a≤1/√2` and `b≥1/2`. It remains valid at `u=0`, where `a=0` and `g_0(x)=i` pointwise; no division by `√u` is used. At `u=1/2`, `b=1/2`, so the denominator gap remains positive even at `x=±2`.

On each `C_j`, both `x` and `λ_j` lie in `[γ_j,γ_(j+1)]`. Therefore

`‖∫_{C_j}(g_u(λ_j)-g_u(x)) dμ(x)‖ ≤ 2√2 (γ_(j+1)-γ_j) μ.real(C_j)`

`= (2√2/W)(γ_(j+1)-γ_j)`.

The first equality uses the constant set integral and the exact **real** cell mass; the inequality uses a pointwise norm bound under the restricted integral. Summing the complex cell errors and applying the finite triangle inequality gives

`‖(1/W)∑_(j<W) g_u(λ_j) - ∫ g_u dμ‖`

`≤ (2√2/W)∑_(j<W)(γ_(j+1)-γ_j) = (2√2/W)(γ_W-γ_0) = 8√2/W`.

This is a telescoping total-variation estimate: the **sum** of all cell widths is exactly `4`. It does not use the largest cell width, whose endpoint behavior would spoil the desired order. Substituting the accepted `semicircleFlowResolvent_integral` yields the candidate closed-flow bound against `i` for every `W≥1` and `0≤u≤1/2`.

## Endpoint and nonvacuity checks

- At `u=0`, `g_0(x)=i` for every `x`; the quadrature error is exactly zero for all positive `W`. This branch checks the sign and precludes any argument that divides by `√u`.
- At `W=1`, the midpoint level is `1/2`, and accepted CDF reflection gives `F(0)=1/2`; inverse uniqueness yields `λ_0=0`. At `u=1/2`, `g(0)=2i` by the public T873 theorem, so the one-cell quadrature is `2i` and the error against the accepted integral `i` is exactly `1`. The bound `8√2` is conservative and nonvacuous.
- At `W=2`, strict inverse order gives two distinct interior midpoint levels `1/4` and `3/4` and cells of mass `1/2`. Reflection plus inverse uniqueness (if T883 exports or a Q0 module proves it) gives representatives `-q,q` with `0<q<2`. Their average is `i b/(u q²+b²)`. For `u>0`, this equals `i` **iff** `q²=1-u`. Thus the W2 error cannot vanish at both `u=1/4` and `u=1/2`: those would require `q²=3/4` and `q²=1/2` simultaneously. This gives a rigorous W2 nonzero-error witness at one of two explicit closed-flow times without an exact quarter-quantile formula. It is an optional proof obligation if the later Q0 theorem is required to carry a W2 nonvacuity witness; it is not yet a Lean theorem. In particular, nonconstant pointwise values alone would not prove nonzero quadrature error.
