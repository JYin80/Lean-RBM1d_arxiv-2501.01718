# T901 — centered finite Fourier complex concentration

## Verdict

**PASS — standalone module and root integration build.** For `W ≥ 2`, tables
`x, χ : Fin W → ℂ` with `‖x i‖ ≤ 2` and `‖χ i‖ ≤ 1`, and `r ≥ 0`, the new
module proves

```text
(uniformPerm W).real
  {π | r ≤ ‖permutationFourierSum W x χ π - finiteComplexMean W x χ‖}
  ≤ 4 * exp (-(W:ℝ) * r² / 256),
```

where `finiteComplexMean` is the exact complex counting average over all
permutations. Under the additional character cancellation hypothesis
`∑ i, χ i = 0`, it proves the same estimate for the uncentered Fourier sum.

## Proof

The private real and imaginary mean identities show that the components of the
complex finite average are exactly the corresponding real counting averages.
The four accepted T892 scalar tails then apply at `s = r / √2` to the upper and
lower tails of both coordinates of the centered complex observable. The
accepted T896 bridge covers its norm event by these four signed coordinate
events and bounds their union by four times the common scalar estimate. Since
`2(r/√2)² = r²`, the exponent is exactly `-W r²/256`.

The zero-character result uses the accepted finite permutation mean-zero
theorem, which makes the complex center zero. The W0/W1 boundary theorem
establishes that the permutation space is a singleton and the centered complex
observable vanishes pointwise; for each positive threshold its tail measure is
zero. No `1/W` estimate is used at those widths.

The concrete W2 witness takes `x=(2,-2)` and `χ=(1,-1)`. It satisfies the
stated bounds and `∑χ=0`, gives Fourier values `2` at identity and `-2` at swap,
and has complex finite mean zero. Each permutation singleton has uniform mass
`1/2`, so the zero-sum model is nonconstant and has positive mass at both
values.

This is a finite uniform-permutation theorem. It makes no claim about Gaussian
Green functions, Theorem 2.3 formulas (2.3)–(2.4), or the Gaussian local law.
No new hypothesis beyond the bounded tables is used by the centered theorem;
character cancellation is required only for the uncentered specialization.
