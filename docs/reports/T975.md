# T975 — finite-grid simultaneous Fourier probability

## Mathematical preflight and result

**Mathematical preflight PASS; standalone proof PASS.** For `W ≥ 2`,
`T ≥ 1`, a table `x : Fin T → Fin W → ℂ` with `‖x t j‖ ≤ 2`, and `r > 0`,
the module proves

`1 - 4 T (W-1) exp(-W r²/256) ≤ uniformPerm W (goodSet)`

where `goodSet` requires the same permutation to satisfy the strict bound
`‖permutationFourierSum W (x t) χ_q π‖ < r` for every `t : Fin T` and every
nonzero canonical mode `q`. This lower bound is meaningful even when its
right-hand side is negative; when the tail sum is below one it also gives
positive probability and hence recovers the existence mechanism of T914.

## Probability and event audit

The weak bad event for pair `(t,q)` is exactly
`{π | r ≤ ‖permutationFourierSum W (x t) χ_q π‖}`. The module proves the
strict good event is the complement of the union of these weak bad events,
so equality at `r` is assigned to the bad event. The index set is the exact
product `Fin T × (Fin W \ {0})`, with cardinality `T*(W-1)`. Each event and
the finite union are measurable because `PermΩ W` carries the discrete
measurable space. T901's accepted one-mode tail bounds each event by
`4 exp(-W r²/256)`; the finite measure union bound and
`probReal_compl_eq_one_sub` give the asserted lower bound under the actual
`uniformPerm W` probability measure. No independence assumption is used.

The W2 witness uses the nonconstant table `x=(2,-2)`. At the identity and
the unique nonzero mode, the normalized Fourier sum is `2`; thus the identity
belongs to the strict-good event at threshold `3` and to the weak-bad event at
threshold `2`. Its uniform singleton mass is `1/2`, so both event forms have
a concrete positive-mass instance (at their stated thresholds). No
nondegeneracy hypotheses are added to the probability theorem.

## Paper scope and limits

I inspected the prescribed `paper/250520-YinJun-v2.pdf` at formulas
(2.3)–(2.4). These are the high-probability Gaussian entrywise Green-function
local law and partial block-trace law, with `N,W,ℓ,η` and a Gaussian matrix.
T975 is only a finite uniform-permutation probability statement. It proves
neither paper formula, a Gaussian event probability, nor an asymptotic
bandwidth or grid-size estimate. It uses the accepted finite-permutation
tail and union results T901 and T914; T928's exponent calculation is not
needed for this generic bound.
