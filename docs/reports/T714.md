## Scalar identity, including endpoints

For real `λ∈[-2,2]` and `0≤u≤1/2`, the denominator has squared norm

`u λ²+(1-u)² ≥ (1-u)² ≥ 1/4`.

Hence `|g_u(λ)|≤2`; the gap is `1-u≥1/2`, including `u=1/2` and `λ=±2`. At `u=0`, `g_0(λ)=(-i)⁻¹=i` pointwise. This branch must precede every division by `sqrt u`.

For `0<u≤1/2`, let `y=(1-u)/sqrt u>0`. The paper's integral convention gives

`∫g_u(λ) ρ_sc(dλ) = (sqrt u)⁻¹ m_sc(iy)`.

The upper-half-plane solution of `m(m+iy)=-1` is `m_sc(iy)=i(sqrt(y²+4)-y)/2`. Since `y²+4=(1+u)²/u`, this becomes `i sqrt u`; thus the integral is `i`. The other quadratic root has negative imaginary part. The identity therefore holds at every time in the **closed** interval. This calculation checks the sign: the resolvent uses `(λ-z)⁻¹`, not `(z-λ)⁻¹`.

## Equal-mass midpoint quadrature

Define `ρ_sc(dλ)=(2π)⁻¹ sqrt((4-λ²)_+) dλ`, with CDF `F(x)=ρ_sc((−∞,x])`. Normalization, continuity, strict positivity of the density on `(-2,2)`, and symmetry give `F(-2)=0`, `F(2)=1`, a unique inverse on `[0,1]`, and `F(-x)=1-F(x)`. For every **positive** natural `W`, set

`γ_j=F⁻¹(j/W)` (`0≤j≤W`), `λ_j=F⁻¹((j+1/2)/W)` (`0≤j<W`).

Use disjoint cells `(γ_j,γ_{j+1}]`, with the leftmost endpoint added to the first cell. Endpoints have zero mass, so each cell has mass `1/W`; `λ_j` is in its closed cell and in `(-2,2)`. No singularity occurs at `γ_0=-2` or `γ_W=2`. Symmetry gives `λ_{W-1-j}=-λ_j`: if `W` is odd the middle point is `0`, while if `W` is even all points form opposite pairs. For `W=1`, `λ_0=0` and the sole cell has mass one. For `W=2`, `λ_0<0<λ_1=-λ_0`, and both cells have mass `1/2`; these are genuine nonempty cases, not an eventual or vacuous assertion. At `u=0`, every `W` gives zero quadrature error.

For `a,b∈[-2,2]`, the resolvent identity and the denominator gap give the direct complex Lipschitz bound

`|g_u(a)-g_u(b)| ≤ [sqrt u/(1-u)²] |a-b| ≤ 2 sqrt 2 |a-b|`.

This also holds at `u=0`, where the left side is zero. On cell `j`, integrate `g_u(λ_j)-g_u(λ)` against its mass `1/W`; its norm is at most `2 sqrt 2 (γ_{j+1}-γ_j)/W`. Summing **all cell widths**, including both endpoint cells, telescopes to `(γ_W-γ_0)=4`. Therefore

`| W⁻¹ ∑_{j<W} g_u(λ_j) - i | ≤ 8 sqrt 2 / W`, for every `W≥1` and `u∈[0,1/2]`. **(Q0)**

Equivalently, this is a total-variation bound: `Var_[−2,2](g_u)≤8 sqrt 2`, and the sum of cell variations, rather than the largest cell width, is divided by `W`. The widest endpoint cells can be of order `W⁻²ᐟ³`; multiplying that width by a global Lipschitz constant would lose the required `W⁻¹` rate. The displayed direct telescoping argument is Lean-friendlier than introducing an abstract complex total-variation API. The constant is conservative and T671's identity and Q0 constant need no correction.
