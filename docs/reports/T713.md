## Mathematical and Lean inspection

I read the actual T676/T694/T703/T706 producers and reports T694/T698/T703/T704/T708/T706. In `PermutationFiberMeanRange.lean:20–33`, the theorem quantifies over every `W≥2`, `k:Fin W`, prefix table `p`, distinct `a,b` unused before `k`, and complex tables satisfying `∀i, ‖x i‖≤2` and `∀i, ‖χ i‖≤1`. The explicit premise `0 < card(A)` occurs before either normalized sum in the signature. T694's equivalence and T703's cardinality equality then give `card(B)=card(A)>0`; there is no hidden assumption that an arbitrary prefix is consistent and no use of a `0/0` mean.

The proof uses T703 in the correct direction: its sum-transport identity changes the sum of `F(codomainSwap π)` over `A` into the sum of `F(ρ)` over `B` (`:41–56`). It subtracts pointwise values in the reverse order of T694's sensitivity theorem, correctly handled by `norm_sub_rev` (`:57–62`). `norm_sum_le` and the uniform `8/W` pointwise bound give a numerator at most `card(A)·8/W` (`:63–71`). Cardinality equality first makes the denominators identical, and `card(A)>0` justifies dividing the real norm inequality (`:72–89`). T694 itself reduces its codomain swap to T676's domain swap, where the factors `‖χ_j−χ_k‖≤2` and `‖x_{πk}−x_{πj}‖≤4` yield exactly `8/W`. No Fourier zero-sum premise is needed.

The declared `permutationPrefixFiber_meanRange_two_witness` (`:94–112`) fixes `W=2`, `k=0`, `p=id`, `a=0`, `b=1`, proves both cards positive, and returns `x=(2,-2)`, `χ=(1,-1)` with their norm bounds. It has no clause about either mean or their norm difference. Mathematically the two fibers are singletons (identity and transposition), with means `2` and `-2`, hence difference `4`; the source proves neither singleton fact nor this equality. T676's pointwise sharpness theorem does not itself state the normalized fiber-mean equality. A repair should add a theorem for these very tables and fibers whose conclusion is the normalized mean difference `= 8/(2:ℝ)` (or `=4`), with the positive cardinality witnesses retained.

At `W=1` no distinct `a,b:Fin 1` exist, so the comparison cannot be instantiated. At `W=3`, `k=2`, and `p 0=p 1=0`, candidates `a=1,b=2` are distinct and unused but both fibers are empty; the explicit positivity premise excludes this inconsistent prefix. Thus the main theorem is not vacuous, and the displayed `W=2` data do satisfy every premise.

I checked the paper's (2.3)–(2.4) in `paper/250520-YinJun-v2.pdf`: they are high-probability Green-entry and partial-trace local laws with `W`, `ℓ`, `η`, and `N` scales. This finite permutation-fiber theorem asserts neither one. It contains no `N`/time power, first-cell or common-event statement, conditional expectation, Doob increment, Azuma estimate, P2 tail, or paper closure.
