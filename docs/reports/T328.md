# T328 — deterministic fluctuation averaging sufficient for A′

## Final verdict after primary-source audit

**The averaging input needed for mathematical A′ has a noncircular proof from the pre-A′ Step1 estimates, by adapting the diagonal fluctuation-averaging proof of [40] with a deterministic control.** The sufficient conclusion is

`max_a |W⁻¹ Σ_(i∈I_a)(G_u,ii−m)| ≺ Φ_u ≤ 2 (ell_u/ell_s)/A_u`,

where `A_u=W ell_u eta_u` and `Φ_u=(ell_u/ell_s)/A_u+W⁻¹`. The probability event can contain the supremum over the whole window. This does not prove the stronger random-two-loop version of paper (4.5). The earlier random-normalization argument below is retained as an **unaudited research appendix**, is deferred, and is not used in this verdict. This report closes the averaging subproblem mathematically; it makes no claim that all other A′ ingredients or their Lean implementations are complete.

### A. Exact external source and literal mismatches

Reference [40] is László Erdős, Antti Knowles, Horng-Tzer Yau, Jun Yin, *The local semicircle law for a general class of random matrices*, Electronic Journal of Probability 18 (2013), 1–58. [Author PDF, May 24, 2013](https://www.unige.ch/~knowles/SC.pdf).

Theorem 4.6, second estimate (4.11), assumes deterministic `M⁻¹/²≤Ψ≤M⁻ᶜ`, entry error `Λ≺Ψ`, and deterministic weights with maximum `M⁻¹` and absolute sum at most one. It gives the centered diagonal average `O_≺(Ψ²)`. No commutation with S is required for this estimate. Definition 2.2 restricts eta to at least `M⁻¹`. Definition 2.1 uses a supremum of failure probabilities, not a simultaneous event. Appendix B, Lemma B.2, (B.18), Remark B.3, and the proof of Theorem 4.6 provide the proof needed here. PDF printed pages 13–14 and 50–56; searchable extraction lines 926–975, 4910–5054, 5790–5847. In particular the paragraph before (4.8) explicitly requires deterministic control. Therefore the original paper's direct substitution `Ψ²=max L₂` is not justified by the cited theorem statement alone.

The following calculations are our adaptation/application, not additional conclusions quoted from [40].

### B. Precise deterministic theorem used here

Let the actual Gaussian band model have `n=WL≤N`, `W≥N^w` for a fixed w>0, bulk energy `|E|≤2−κ`, `L≥3`, and `0≤s≤u≤t<1`. Assume `eta_t≥N⁻ᴷ` for fixed K. Take a deterministic control Ψ_u satisfying

`W⁻¹/²≤Ψ_u≤N⁻ᵃ` for a fixed a>0,

and suppose `max_ij |G_u,ij−m delta_ij|≺Ψ_u`, with fixed-time failure bounds uniform in u. Then, uniformly over u and any polynomial-size deterministic family of weights `|t_i|≤W⁻¹`, `Σ|t_i|≤1`,

`Σ_i t_i Q_iG_u,ii ≺ Ψ_u²`.                                      (DFA)

The assertion is for each requested ε,D>0, with a sufficiently large fixed moment order p chosen before N. There is no all-p assertion at fixed N, and no supremum over sample-dependent coefficients. All rows of S and all block averages form an allowed family. Constants in the weight maximum are absorbed by dividing the coefficients by that constant.

**Why the source proof applies beyond its printed eta range.** For fixed p, resolvent deletion gives, uniformly in minors of at most O(p) rows, diagonal entries bounded away from zero and the same entry control: each deletion changes a surviving entry by a product of two off-diagonal entries divided by a bounded diagonal. The deterministic center has |m|=1. Globally,

`|G_ij^T|≤eta_t⁻¹≤Nᴷ`,

and Schur's formula gives

`|1/G_ii^T|≤|H_ii|+|z_u|+eta_t⁻¹ ||H_i,outside(T∪i)||₂²`.

Gaussian moments bound the latter expression polynomially at every fixed order, uniformly in u≤1. This supplies the moment envelopes used to pay exceptional sets in Appendix B. Replacing its envelope `|G|≤M` by `Nᴷ` changes only the exceptional-probability exponent selected after p, not any power of Ψ in the main terms. Conditional Jensen preserves stochastic bounds at deterministic Ψ using these envelopes.

For clarity, the retained degree counting is as follows. In a 2p-th moment expansion, a tuple with r distinct indices and a singleton labels has coefficient mass at most `C_p W^{−(2p−r)}`. Centering each singleton forces an extra resolvent difference on another factor. Deletion gains one additional off-diagonal factor of size Ψ, so the product bound is `N^ε Ψ^{2p+a}`. Since `a≥2r−2p` and `W⁻¹≤Ψ²`, the whole contribution is at most `C_p N^ε Ψ^{4p}`. Finite-minor identities, commuting conditional projections on the independent Gaussian coordinates, and the fixed-p expansion are unchanged. Shared row edges do not invalidate commutation: the projections integrate subsets of underlying independent coordinates. The base bound for the diagonal version is simply `Q_i(G_ii^T−m)≺Ψ`; no variance-row normalization is used in this argument. Thus `H_u=√u X` is admissible without rescaling by u, and u=0 is trivial. There is no singular small-u parameter.

No good-event indicator is inserted before a conditional cancellation. Bounds on a good event are converted to moments after the exact cancellation, paying the complement by the polynomial envelopes. Markov's inequality with fixed p then proves (DFA). This addresses both literal mismatches: variance row sums u rather than one, and eta below the source's domain. It is an adaptation of its proof, not a direct application of its theorem statement.

### C. The deterministic control is supplied before A′

Use the initial `BoundsCore` at s and `Cond272Reg`, then `Gauss.step1Hyp_gauss_of_scale''`, `Step1.weakLaw`, and `Step1.apriori` with n=2. `Gauss.lemma41Flow_gauss` supplies the entry improvement. As established in T320/T323, the weak law removes the indicator in the entry estimate on a common high-probability event; this is not an event-restricted estimate promoted without justification. Consequently

`max_ij |G_u,ij−m delta_ij|² ≺ Φ_u`,
`Φ_u = 1/(W eta_u ell_s)+1/W`.

Set `Ψ_u=√Φ_u` (fixed constants are harmless in stochastic domination). Let `r_u=ell_u/ell_s` and `R=eta_s/eta_t`. Scale monotonicity and (2.72) give

`1≤r_u≤R^{1/2}≤A_t^{1/60}≤A_u^{1/60}`,
`N^c≤A_t≤A_u≤W≤N`.

Therefore

`W⁻¹≤Φ_u≤2A_u^{−59/60}≤2N^{−59c/60}`,
`Φ_u≤2r_u/A_u`.

For sufficiently large N, Ψ_u has the required fixed polynomial upper bound, for example `Ψ_u≤N^{−c/3}`. The source's M is comparable to W; for our profile it is 3W, and constant weight rescaling handles block weights 1/W. Dimension n and N are polynomially comparable because n≥W≥N^w. Also `A_t≤W sqrt(eta_t)` implies `eta_t≥(A_t/W)²≥N⁻²`, so the needed polynomial envelope is automatic in this regime. Nothing requires `WΦ_u≺1`; that stronger condition can fail on later windows.

### D. Conditional Gaussian IBP, with the diagonal exception handled

Write `v_i=G_ii−m`, `a_i=P_i v_i`, `Z_i=Q_i v_i`. The identities `G−m=m(−H−um)G` and complex Gaussian integration by parts in row i give exactly

`a_i = u m Σ_k S_ik P_i[v_k G_ii]`
`    = u m² Σ_k S_ik P_i v_k + u m Σ_k S_ik P_i[v_k v_i]`.

The second term is `O_≺(Φ_u)`: both centered diagonals are `O_≺(√Φ_u)`, and conditional Jensen with the preceding global envelopes preserves their product bound at the deterministic Φ_u. Since the row absolute sum is one, there is no cardinality loss.

For k≠i, the exact minor identity is

`G_kk−G_kk^(i)=G_ki G_ik/G_ii=O_≺(Φ_u)`.

The minor is independent of row i, so `P_i v_k−v_k=O_≺(Φ_u)`. The formula is not applied with k=i. That exceptional term is `−Z_i=O_≺(√Φ_u)`, multiplied by `S_ii≤C/W`. Since `Φ_u≥W⁻¹` and Φ_u≤1 eventually, `W⁻¹√Φ_u≤Φ_u`. Hence

`a_i = u m² Σ_k S_ik v_k + O_≺(Φ_u)`.

Apply (DFA) to the row coefficients S_ik and use v=a+Z:

`(I−u m² S)a = O_≺(Φ_u)` in the maximum norm.

`RBM.stable_Sblk_short_edge` in `Green/EntryBound.lean` proves the deterministic bound `||(I−u m²S)⁻¹||_(∞→∞)≤Kstab(κ)`, uniformly in u<1, W,L. This is the bounded same-sign inverse, with no `(1−u)⁻¹` loss. Thus `max_i|a_i|≺Φ_u`. Applying (DFA) once more to each block gives the announced bound on the normalized block trace. The primitive length-one term is m; no K term or normalization has changed.

### E. One event for all times and indices

First the above proof is uniform in fixed u: p, envelope exponent K, weak/entry-law constants, and the upper-control exponent are independent of u. Fix the desired ε,D. On an event of arbitrary polynomial probability, `||X||op≤N^B` for a fixed B. For the repository scaling flow, the resolvent identity gives

`||G_u−G_v||op ≤ N^{2K} (N^B |u−v|^{1/2}+|u−v|)`.

Conditional expectations satisfy the same type of bound with a polynomial prefactor: integrate the resampled row's norm, whose expectation is polynomial, and retain the untouched coordinates' norm bound. Thus the centered weighted averages and the block traces have polynomial Hölder-1/2 moduli. The weights needed here are time independent. Furthermore Φ_u is deterministic, increasing, satisfies Φ_u≥N⁻¹, and its explicit formula has polynomial Lipschitz constant on this eta range.

Choose a mesh spacing `h=N⁻ᴸ` so large L makes the additive modulus error, and the change of Φ across a mesh interval, at most `N⁻³`. Choose moment order p sufficiently large for failure exponent `D+L+3` at mesh points, using tolerance ε/2 there. Then choose the minor-entry tolerances and exceptional-event exponents needed for that fixed p. A union bound over the polynomial mesh, rows, and blocks followed by interpolation gives

`P(∃u∈[s,t], a: |W⁻¹Σ_(i∈I_a)v_i(u)|>N^ε Φ_u)≤N⁻ᴰ`

for all sufficiently large N. The same holds for all required row/block centered averages. Intersect this event with the existing weak-law, loop, and norm events; the intersection still has arbitrary polynomial probability after adjusting D. Its nonemptiness eventually follows from its probability, not from unrelated sample witnesses. For the original Brownian coupling, fixed-time estimates are identical; a Brownian Hölder exponent below 1/2 and its Gaussian moment bound replace the scaling-flow modulus. No identification of the two path laws is made.

## 1. Exact theorem

Let M=WL≤N, with W≥N^w for some fixed w>0. Use the paper's actual complex Hermitian Gaussian band matrix H_u=√u X, with independent real Gaussian coordinates and variance profile S, and z_u=E+(1-u)m(E), |E|≤2−κ, κ>0. Write m=m(E), G_u=(H_u−z_u)⁻¹. Suppose 0≤s_N≤t_N<1 and eta_(t_N)≥N⁻ᴷ eventually for a fixed K. Assume the **pre-A′** weak local law

`max_ij |G_u,ij−m delta_ij| ≺ N⁻ᵃ`, a>0,

uniformly in u∈[s_N,t_N]. Step1.weakLaw and Cond272Reg supply such an a. In addition use the already proved Gaussian entry estimates (4.2)/(4.3), which under this weak law remove their good-event indicator. These give, uniformly in u and indices,

`max_(i≠j)|G_ij| + max_i|G_ii−m| ≺ sqrt(Γ_u)`,

where

`Γ_u=max_(blocks a,b) W⁻² Σ_(i∈I_a,j∈I_b)|G_u,ij|²`.

Indeed (4.2)'s neighboring-block sum is at most 9Γ, its W⁻¹ term is bounded by CΓ on the weak-law event, and (4.3) handles the diagonal. No averaged law is used here.

For each deterministic coefficient vector t=(t_i), possibly complex, with

`max_i |t_i|≤W⁻¹`, `Σ_i |t_i|≤1`,

let P_i denote integration over all independent Gaussian coordinates incident to row/column i, Q_i=1−P_i, and Z_i=Q_i(G_ii−m). Then

`Σ_i t_i Z_i ≺ Γ`.                                                    (FA)

The probability bound is uniform over any fixed polynomial-size deterministic family of coefficients, including all block averages and the rows of S. A supremum over all *sample-dependent* coefficient vectors is not asserted. Time-independent coefficients, as needed here, admit the uniform-time net upgrade below. For arbitrary time-dependent coefficient families that upgrade additionally needs a polynomial Hölder modulus of the coefficients.

The consequence is

`max_a |W⁻¹ Σ_(i∈I_a)(G_ii−m)| ≺ Γ`,                                (AVG)

uniformly in the same time window. Hence the general `StepGlue.Eq45Flow` transfer follows by Γ≺Φ from a uniform two-loop bound Φ.

All stochastic assertions mean: each requested ε,D>0 is fixed first, then a sufficiently large fixed moment order p, then auxiliary event exponents/tolerances, and finally N≥N0. There is no requirement uniform in all p at fixed N.

## 2. Good event and global envelopes

Fix a finite integer n (eventually n=2p). Choose a small tolerance b>0 and an arbitrarily large exceptional exponent B. From the weak law and entry estimates, take a common event E_N(u) with failure ≤N⁻ᴮ on which

- |G_ii−m|≤Nᵇ sqrt(Γ), |G_ij|≤Nᵇ sqrt(Γ) for i≠j;
- |G_ii| and |G_ii|⁻¹ are bounded by a fixed bulk constant;
- c/W≤Γ≤N⁻ᵃ⁰ for some a0>0.

The last upper bound follows directly from `Γ≤C/W+max_(i≠j)|G_ij|²` and the weak law. The lower bound follows from the W diagonal entries in any diagonal block: |G_ii|≥|m|/2=1/2 eventually. Choose b small enough, depending on n, that every N^(C_n b)sqrt(Γ) below tends to zero. All fixed finite minor estimates below are then deterministic consequences on this event.

Globally, for every sample and every principal minor, `||G^S||op≤eta⁻¹≤Nᴷ`. Therefore every matrix entry and every two-loop norm has a deterministic polynomial envelope. The positive floor introduced below gives a polynomial inverse envelope as well. These global bounds pay all exceptional events; no indicator is inserted into a conditional-cancellation identity.

The uniform-time version of E_N is available from the uniform pre-A′ estimates. In the fixed-time proof it is enough that its failure bound and all constants are uniform in u; the resampled samples have the same fixed-time Gaussian law.

## 3. Embedded minors and the random proxy

For a finite set S of particle indices, G^S is the resolvent of the principal minor with rows S removed, embedded back into the original M×M matrix with zero rows and columns on S. Block sets I_a and normalization W⁻² are **unchanged**. No K term is involved.

Define

`Γ_ab^S=W⁻² ||1_(I_a) G^S 1_(I_b)||HS²`.

Choose an even integer q≥4n with q≥log(L²+1); increasing q by a fixed multiple is harmless. Put

`λ^S=[ W⁻q + Σ_(a,b)(Γ_ab^S)^q ]^(1/q)`.

The floor is one extra coordinate of the norm. In particular λ^S≥W⁻¹ for every sample. For fixed n, q=O_n(log N), and

`max(W⁻¹,max_ab Γ_ab^S)≤λ^S≤e max(W⁻¹,max_ab Γ_ab^S)`.

All these are measurable functions. λ^S reads only matrix entries with both indices outside S, so it is independent of every Gaussian row-coordinate set for an index in S. In particular λ^{S∪{i}} is row-i measurable-free even though it is random.

Write Δ_k F^S=F^S−F^(S∪{k}) and Δ_T for the commuting iterated differences in distinct k∈T disjoint from S. This is an exact inclusion–exclusion difference, with no differentiation in a random coordinate.

### 3.1 Exact rank-one identity and normalization

For k∉S, the embedded identity is

`G^S−G^(S∪{k})=G^S_:k G^S_k:/G^S_kk`.                                (R1)

It holds also in row/column k: the right-hand side there equals G^S itself, while the embedded minor is zero. Thus no exceptional boundary term from deleting a block entry is omitted.

On E_N, all minors of at most n rows satisfy the same diagonal boundedness and off-diagonal N^(C_n b)sqrt(Γ) bounds. Induction uses the usual entry form of (R1): a deletion changes a surviving diagonal by O(N^(C_n b)Γ), and a surviving off-diagonal by the same amount. Since n is fixed and Γ→0, denominators stay bounded away from zero. The original Γ here is the full-matrix control at that sample.

For each block and surviving k,

`W⁻¹ ||1_(I_a) G^S_:k||₂² ≤ C_n N^(C_n b) Γ`.

The single diagonal contribution is O(W⁻¹)≤CΓ; every other entry uses the off-diagonal bound. The analogous row estimate holds. Applying (R1),

`W⁻¹ ||1_a Δ_k G^S 1_b||HS ≤ C_n N^(C_n b) Γ`.

This is a rank-one product of two normalized vector norms, and has no factor W.

### 3.2 Higher differences: complete degree induction

For distinct deletion indices T, |T|=r≥1, all disjoint from S,

`W⁻¹ ||1_a Δ_T G^S 1_b||HS ≤ C_n N^(C_n b) Γ^((r+1)/2)`.             (Rr)

For a diagonal entry i∉S∪T,

`|Δ_T G^S_ii| ≤ C_n N^(C_n b) Γ^((r+1)/2)`.                           (Dr)

Here and below C_n may increase a finite number of times. Proof: start from (R1). A block term consists of a column endpoint, a row endpoint, internal off-diagonal entries between distinct deletion vertices, and bounded inverse diagonal entries. Its normalized HS degree is the product of endpoint degrees sqrt(Γ), sqrt(Γ), and one sqrt(Γ) for each internal edge. Apply one new difference using the exact discrete Leibniz rule `Δ_k(fg)=(Δ_k f)g+f^(k)Δ_k g` repeatedly. Differencing an endpoint inserts a new endpoint and one off-diagonal entry via (R1), gaining sqrt(Γ). Differencing an internal off-diagonal replaces one edge by two, also gaining sqrt(Γ). Differencing an inverse diagonal uses

`Δ_k (1/G_jj^S)=−(G_jk^S G_kj^S)/(G_jj^S G_jj^(S∪{k}) G_kk^S)`,

and gains Γ, which is at least as strong. New k is distinct from every old graph vertex, so the inserted edges are off-diagonal. Each differentiation has finitely many branches depending only on n. This proves (Rr). For (Dr), the two endpoints at i are off-diagonal scalar entries; the same counting starts with two such entries and proves the assertion. Minor superscripts change but never invalidate the bounds.

For r=0 the block norm is at most Csqrt(Γ), while the *centered diagonal* |G_ii^S−m|≤C_n N^(C_n b)sqrt(Γ); this follows from the full centered entry bound and the r=1 changes.

### 3.3 Loop and smooth-proxy differences

Discrete Leibniz applied to the HS inner product gives, for 1≤r≤n,

`|Δ_T Γ_ab^S|≤C_n N^(C_n b) Γ^(1+r/2)`.                              (Lr)

Every term splits T between two matrix factors. If one part is empty use its sqrt(Γ) norm; otherwise use (Rr). The product always has exponent 1+r/2. The same proof works with conjugates because deletion commutes with conjugation.

In particular all finite-minor maxima differ from the full maximum by O(N^(C_n b)Γ^(3/2)). Thus λ^S is comparable to Γ, with constants depending only on n, uniformly for |S|≤n. This also verifies the row-deleted proxy's stability rather than assuming it.

For the smooth norm F(v)=(Σ v_j^q)^(1/q), including its positive floor coordinate, its rth derivative satisfies

`|D^r F(v)[h_1,...,h_r]|≤C_r q^r ||v||_q^(1-r) Π ||h_j||_q`, r≤n.

To verify the bound, differentiate Σv_j^q and its 1/q power. Each resulting sum of products is bounded by generalized Hölder; q≥4n ensures all exponents are nonnegative. Each term has homogeneous degree 1-r, and the number of terms depends only on r. The corresponding bound for F⁻¹ has degree −1-r. This uses no lower bound on an individual block component.

Apply these derivative bounds to the multi-affine interpolation of the finite cube `{Γ_ab^(S∪U):U⊆T}`. Its derivatives are convex combinations of the exact lower-order differences in (Lr). Its entries are nonnegative; at every interpolation point some vertex has coefficient at least 2⁻ʳ, so its q-norm is at least 2⁻ʳ times that vertex's norm and hence comparable to Γ. The fundamental theorem of calculus in each cube variable, followed by the finite chain rule, proves

`|Δ_T λ^S|≤C_n q^(C_n) N^(C_n b) Γ^(1+r/2)`,
`|Δ_T (λ^S)⁻¹|≤C_n q^(C_n) N^(C_n b) Γ^(−1+r/2)`.                    (Pr)

For fixed n, powers of q are subpolynomial. They can be made part of an arbitrarily small N tolerance by choosing b afterwards. This proves, rather than posits, the needed random-proxy difference gain.

## 4. Row-centered normalized variables

For i∉S define

`Y_i^S=(G_ii^S−m)/λ^(S∪{i})`, `X_i=Q_i Y_i^empty=Z_i/λ^{i}`.

The equality is exact because λ^{i} is independent of row i. Moreover Y_i^S is independent of every row in S. Combining (Dr), the centered r=0 bound and (Pr) by discrete Leibniz gives

`|Δ_T Y_i^S|≤C_n q^(C_n) N^(C_n b) Γ^((r−1)/2)`, r=|T|≤n.            (Yr)

The denominator's deleted set always includes i, but T excludes i; all estimates in §3 apply to this shifted family. The r=0 bound is Γ⁻¹/²; r=1 is O(1); r=2 gains sqrt(Γ). This is precisely the gain required by singleton cancellation. Globally each Y and its finitely many differences are polynomially bounded because λ≥W⁻¹ and eta⁻¹ is polynomial.

### 4.1 Resampling and shared control

A word of P_j and Q_j is represented by expanding Q_j=1−P_j and integrating independent copies of the Gaussian row coordinates. Products of several words use independent auxiliary copies per factor. Each transformed full sample has the original Gaussian law, even when two rows share one matrix entry: the independent objects are the underlying real Gaussian coordinates, and conditional expectations over subsets of those coordinates commute.

Fix a moment tuple, and let R be **all distinct particle indices in that tuple**. Every transformation used by the pivot words resamples only coordinates incident to R. Hence the embedded minor G^R and λ^R are identical for the original sample and every transformed sample. On the event that every transformed sample lies in E_N, §3 yields Γ(transformed)≈λ^R. This is the common control for products. Independence between the X_i is never assumed.

There are only C_n transformed samples in any one term. Their union-bad probability is ≤C_n N⁻ᴮ by marginal equality, whether or not they are mutually independent. On that bad set use the polynomial global envelope. B may be chosen after n, K and the desired error power. No conditional expectation of an indicator is set to zero, and no favorable event is inserted into the cancellation identity.

## 5. Fourth moment, with all collision powers

Let S_t=Σ_i t_i X_i. Expand

`E|S_t|⁴=Σ_(i1,i2,i3,i4) t_i1 t_i2 conjugate(t_i3 t_i4)
 E[X_i1 X_i2 conjugate(X_i3 X_i4)]`.

Conjugation commutes with P,Q and the minor operations. For each tuple, call an index singleton if it occurs in exactly one slot; let a be the number of singleton slots.

Pivot each singleton row k. Its factor retains a Q_k, since all other row conditional operators commute. In each other factor insert `1=P_k+Q_k`. The all-P term integrates to zero: its other factors are row-k independent and P_kQ_k=0. Every surviving term has at least one new Q_k on another factor. After all a pivots, each surviving term has d≥a extra Q letters across its four factors.

For each factor, replace the rows of its extra Q letters by the corresponding iterated minor difference of Y. This is exact: a deleted-row term is row-independent and annihilated by Q. P letters merely average. After expanding Q and representing all P by resampling, (Yr) and the shared λ^R give the product bound

`C N^ε (λ^R)^((d−4)/2) ≤ C N^ε W^((4−a)/2)`

on the joint good event (λ^R≤1 and λ^R≥W⁻¹). The bad contribution is ≤N⁻ᴸ with L freely large. The finitely many pivot branches cost a constant depending on 4 only.

For a fixed equality partition with d0 distinct indices, the sum of absolute coefficient products is at most W^(−(4−d0)). Proof: for each group of multiplicity r, bound |t_i|^r≤W^(−r+1)|t_i|, then sum the remaining products using Σ|t_i|≤1; dropping distinctness only increases this nonnegative sum.

| Multiplicities | a | d0 | Coefficient mass | Product bound without N^ε | Product of the last two |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 4 | 0 | 1 | W⁻³ | W² | W⁻¹ |
| 3+1 | 1 | 2 | W⁻² | W^(3/2) | W⁻¹/² |
| 2+2 | 0 | 2 | W⁻² | W² | 1 |
| 2+1+1 | 2 | 3 | W⁻¹ | W | 1 |
| 1+1+1+1 | 4 | 4 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

Thus `E|S_t|⁴≤C N^ε+N⁻ᴸ`. No W-dependent factor remains. In the all-distinct case all four cancellations are essential; a single minor replacement would not suffice.

## 6. General fixed moment and recovery of the original fluctuation

Repeat the same argument with n=2p slots. If a singleton slots occur, the number d0 of distinct labels satisfies `d0≤(n+a)/2`. The coefficient mass of each equality partition is therefore ≤W^(−(n−a)/2). Pivoting all singletons gives at least a extra differences, so the joint product bound is ≤C_p N^ε W^((n−a)/2). They cancel. There are finitely many equality partitions/pivot branches depending only on p. Exceptional errors are polynomial envelopes times N⁻ᴮ, and Σ_tuple Π|t_i|≤1. Therefore, for every fixed p and every ε>0,

`E|Σ_i t_i X_i|^(2p) ≤ C_(p,ε) N^ε` eventually, uniformly in u,t.       (M)

Markov gives S_t≺1. This is a direct moment proof with a finite budget; it does not instantiate the unsatisfiable unbudgeted FlucGain interface discussed in T324.

It remains to compare the denominators. From (P1), on the good event,

`|1/λ^empty−1/λ^{i}|≤N^ε Γ⁻¹/²`.

Also `Z_i≺sqrt(Γ)` uniformly in i. To prove this without assuming conditional stability, use the row-free λ^{i}: conditional Jensen bounds the L^r norm of `Q_i[(G_ii−m)/sqrt(λ^{i})]` by twice the norm of its argument. On the good event this argument is N^ε-bounded by §3; on its complement use the global polynomial envelope. Markov, row-proxy comparability, and a union over i give the assertion. The same argument works for any fixed moment order required here.

Consequently

`|Σ_i t_i Z_i/λ^empty − Σ_i t_i X_i|
 ≤ Σ_i |t_i| |Z_i| |1/λ^empty−1/λ^{i}| ≺1`.

Together with (M) and λ^empty≈Γ this proves (FA). The error bound uses Σ|t_i|≤1, so it does not reintroduce a factor M or W. The proxy is not a deterministic threshold δ² and no assumption Wδ²≺1 occurs.

## 7. Conditional IBP and the averaged law

The same shared-minor argument proves the following preservation fact: if F has a polynomial global envelope and |F|≺Γ^r for a fixed nonnegative r, then

`|P_i F|≺Γ^r`, uniformly in i.

Indeed normalize F by (λ^{i})^r, which is row-independent, use conditional Jensen and the good-event comparison, and pay the polynomial bad-event envelope. This also applies to a finite sum with coefficient l1 norm ≤1. It is a genuine random-control conditional stability statement, obtained through λ^{i}, not through an eta-dependent sandwich Γ≈W⁻¹.

Let a_i=P_i(G_ii−m). The exact complex Gaussian IBP identity in the paper gives

`a_i=u m Σ_k S_ik P_i[(G_kk−m)G_ii]`
`   =u m² Σ_k S_ik P_i(G_kk−m)
     +u m Σ_k S_ik P_i[(G_kk−m)(G_ii−m)]`.

The second line's remainder is ≺Γ: each centered diagonal is ≺sqrt(Γ) by (4.3), their product is ≺Γ, and the conditional preservation just proved applies. This uses Σ_k S_ik=1; no eta loss enters the stochastic bound.

For k≠i, the minor identity writes `G_kk−G_kk^{i}=G_ki G_ik/G_ii≺Γ`. Since G_kk^{i} is row-i independent, conditional preservation gives `P_i(G_kk−m)−(G_kk−m)≺Γ`. For k=i the error is Z_i≺sqrt(Γ), but its coefficient S_ii≤C/W makes it ≺Γ because W⁻¹≤CΓ and Γ≤1. Uniformity over k and the row l1 bound permit summation.

It follows that

`a_i=u m² Σ_k S_ik (G_kk−m)+O_≺(Γ)`.

Write G_kk−m=a_k+Z_k. Apply (FA) with t_k=S_ik (the actual profile has max S_ik≤C/W; a fixed constant rescaling is harmless) to obtain

`a=u m² S a+O_≺(Γ)`.

The short-charge deterministic stability estimate used in the paper after (4.9), and proved by the repository's propagator machinery, is

`||(I−u m² S)⁻¹||_(∞→∞)≤C_κ`, uniformly for 0≤u<1.

This is the non-singular same-sign kernel, not the long-edge `(I−uS)⁻¹` with norm (1−u)⁻¹. It yields max_i|a_i|≺Γ. Finally apply (FA) to t_i=W⁻¹1_(i∈I_a), sum a_i with the same weights, and obtain (AVG), exactly (4.5).

No K term has been changed: for length one the primitive is m, so the last expression is precisely the block trace of G−m used in Eq45Flow.

## 8. Time, events, nondegeneracy, and dependency order

The preceding moment constants are uniform in u∈[s_N,t_N] because only bulk κ, the common polynomial eta envelope, fixed p, and uniform weak/entry event probabilities enter. Thus they first give a fixed-time estimate with one N0 for every u.

For the final event with time inside the probability, take a polynomial mesh. On the Gaussian norm event, resolvent identity and `|sqrt(u)−sqrt(v)|≤sqrt(|u−v|)` give polynomial Hölder-1/2 bounds for G, Γ, and the original weighted averages. For conditional expectations, integrate the same bound over the resampled row; its Gaussian norm has finite polynomial moments, while the untouched coordinates are controlled on the norm event. These are the existing `CondExpMod`/`CondStableFlow` moduli. The lower bound Γ≥c/W≥c/N on the weak-law event lets a sufficiently fine mesh absorb the additive continuity error into a smaller stochastic threshold. Choose mesh fineness first, choose fixed p larger than its cardinality/tail requirements, then choose the small entry tolerances and large exceptional exponent. The polynomial union over times, rows and blocks is harmless. This proves the asserted uniform-time (FA)/(AVG).

Every exceptional event used in a resampling calculation is under a full Gaussian product measure with original marginals. On the finite mesh, intersect the original weak/entry event, the norm event, and the final moment consequences. Its probability tends to one with arbitrary polynomial accuracy. The result therefore supplies an eventual nonempty common event; it does not combine unrelated sample witnesses.

## 9. Alternative route and falsification checks

**Route I (row/minor moment expansion):** §§3–6 execute it at p=2 and arbitrary fixed p. The new ingredient is smooth random minor normalization; it directly addresses the hΨW obstruction in T324.

**Route II (direct resolvent/IBP):** §7 executes conditional Gaussian IBP. IBP alone reduces a to a stable linear system plus a row-centered average. Applying absolute bounds to that average gives only sqrt(Γ), so IBP without cancellation does not close (4.5). With Route I supplying that precise missing term, it closes the chain without a second probabilistic input.

**Falsification attempts:**

1. Replace λ^{i} by λ in X_i. Then Q_i[(G_ii−m)/λ]≠Z_i/λ, and singleton cancellation no longer applies to the proposed variable. Row-free denominators are essential.
2. Use the hard maximum Γ as the differentiable proxy. Higher minor differences of a maximum need not gain one sqrt(Γ) per deletion at a switching point. The positive-floor ℓ^q proxy and the explicit higher derivative argument avoid this false step.
3. Bound products by individual global L∞ bounds, λ⁻¹/²≤sqrt(W), before singleton cancellation. This loses the positive powers of the *same* random λ and gives false W losses. Shared λ^R is retained until the whole product is formed.
4. Condition an indicator good event before pivot cancellation. This destroys the zero expectation. All pivot identities above are untruncated; truncation is only in the final joint-resampling estimate.
5. Treat WΨ²≺1 as a consequence of weak law. T324's later-window counterexample disproves that numerical implication. No such condition is present here.
6. Extend to coefficients chosen from the sample signs of Z_i. Then weighted averaging can select magnitudes and lose cancellation. The theorem is restricted to deterministic coefficient families, as the paper requires.
7. Let p grow with N. The finite-minor constants and q choice then have no stated control. Only fixed p is asserted, with N0 depending on p.
