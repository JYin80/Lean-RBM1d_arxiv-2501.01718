## 2. Exact covariance contraction

Let ξ index the independent Gaussian coordinates used at N, with variances g_ξ≥0 and Hermitian matrix directions B_ξ. For a real function b of X write

`||∇_g b||² = sum_ξ g_ξ |∂_(X_ξ)b|²`.

For a complex matrix observable F, use exactly the repository QV

`Q_H(F)=sum_ξ g_ξ |D F[B_ξ]|²`.

No independence between different prefix times is assumed: they are functions of the same X.

For z=sqrt(|F|²+ε²)/T,

`∂_ξ z = Re(conj(F) ∂_ξ F)/(T sqrt(|F|²+ε²))`.

Since |F|/sqrt(|F|²+ε²)≤1,

`||∇_g z_(j,a)|| ≤ sqrt(u_j Q_H(F^matrix_(j,a)))/(T_(j,a))`.

The sqrt(u_j) factor is exact because F_(j,a)(X)=F^matrix_(j,a)(sqrt(u_j)X). The deterministic K term has zero matrix derivative. There is **no ε^(−1)**. At F=0 the derivative is zero; no division by |F| is introduced.

If P=(sum z_a^q)^(1/q), then dP=sum_a α_a dz_a, with α_a=(z_a/P)^(q−1)≥0. Hölder gives sum_a α_a≤M^(1/q). Likewise dS=sum_j β_j dh_j, β_j=(h_j/S)^(q−1), sum_j β_j≤k^(1/q). All denominators are positive for k≥1 because ε>0 and h_j>0. The triangle inequality in the covariance seminorm therefore proves, on **every Gaussian sample**,

`||∇_g S_k|| ≤ (kM)^(1/q) max_(j<k,a) sqrt(u_j Q_H(F^matrix_(j,a)))/(T_(j,a) R_j⁴)`

`                 ≤ e max_(j<k,a) sqrt(u_j Q_H(F^matrix_(j,a)))/(T_(j,a) R_j⁴).`  (C)

This is an inequality, not an identity. The exact derivative is the positive αβ combination above; cross-time covariance terms need not vanish. There is no extra q factor: it cancels between the power and root derivatives. For k=0 both sides are defined as zero. For a prefix consisting only of u_0=0 the derivative is also zero.

The factor sqrt(u_j) belongs to the fixed prefix. The running-time Stein coefficient remains 1/(2sqrt(r)). One cannot replace sqrt(u_j) by sqrt(r) throughout an integral r∈[s,u_k]: many prefix points can lie after the running r. The safe bound sqrt(u_j)≤1 preserves the integrable r^(−1/2) factor.

## 10. Self-contained conditional moment lemma and zero-moment check

Here is the precise analytic theorem used above. On the actual Gaussian probability space, fix p≥1, an endpoint v, prefix k, and the finite-dimensional smooth cutoff c of §1. Assume Y_r is the actual T292 coordinate, Q_r its actual covariance QV, and G_r its actual drift norm. Assume the genuine weighted Gaussian differentiation identity on r>0, the T298 pointwise generator inequality, and the finite-size regularity/integrability just proved. Define A,V,Z,C exactly as in §§4–5. Then (M) holds. The only favorable stochastic assumptions needed to estimate its right side are the explicit event/profile bounds in (J) and the analogous displayed A,V splits; no global sharp gradient constant is an assumption of this theorem.

To verify the powers, the drift integrand factors exactly as

`c^(2p)|Y|^(2p−1) G = |cY|^(2p−1)(cG)`.

Hölder with exponents `2p/(2p−1)` and `2p` gives `φ^((2p−1)/(2p)) A`. The QV integrand factors as

`c^(2p)|Y|^(2p−2) Q = |cY|^(2p−2)(c²Q)`.

For p>1, Hölder with exponents `p/(p−1)` and p gives `φ^((p−1)/p) V`, with **V=||c²Q||_p**, not `||Q||_(2p)` and not a Good-indicated replacement of Q. At p=1 the first factor is identically one, including Y=0, and the integral is exactly V=E[c²Q]. There is no application of Hölder with an undefined finite exponent at p=1.

For the cross term, the weight derivative has `c^(2p−1)` and the observable derivative has `|Y|^(2p−1)`. Their product is exactly `|cY|^(2p−1)`. The remaining covariance contraction is B sqrt(Q), multiplied by χ′ and supported on T_k. This proves (S5) using the same `2p/(2p−1),2p` Hölder pair, even for p=1. No factor c is silently dropped from a weighted QV norm; the cross norm genuinely contains the unweighted product on T_k.

At any r with φ(r)=0, cY=0 almost surely. Thus drift and cross integrals vanish since 2p−1≥1. If p>1 the QV term vanishes as well; if p=1 it is V and may be positive. This agrees exactly with the differential inequality, interpreting its QV factor as 1 at p=1. In the regularization proof, multiplying by `(φ+ε)^((1−2p)/(2p))` is always legal. The inequalities

`φ^((2p−1)/(2p))/(φ+ε)^((2p−1)/(2p)) ≤1`,

`φ^((p−1)/p)/(φ+ε)^((2p−1)/(2p)) ≤(φ+ε)^(−1/(2p))`

hold because the numerator exponents are nonnegative; this includes p=1. Therefore the F_ε differential inequality in §5 is valid at zero moments. The scalar comparison then proves (M) without a strict positivity premise.

The profile requirements are local to Good∩support: T_k⊂{c>0}, and §1's modulus lemma implies the cap at every running r up to the fixed endpoint. The same Good supplies the loop/entry estimates used to build both the early QV envelope at all u_j and the evolved current QV envelope at r. This is exactly the situation of T330's full near/J²/J³ rates. Their deterministic rate is used on Good∩support only; the actual uncut Q_r stays inside the generator and all norms.
