## Exact claim and dependency audit

For `W : ℕ`, `k : Fin W`, `π : PermΩ W`, and `v : Fin W`, put

`C = {ρ | ρ ∈ prefixCell W k.val π}` and `E = {ρ | ρ ∈ prefixCell W k.val π ∧ ρ k = v}`.

The T766 candidate is

```lean
theorem uniformPerm_nextImage_given_actualPrefix
    (W : ℕ) (k : Fin W) (π : PermΩ W) (v : Fin W)
    (hv : ∀ i : Fin W, i < k → π i ≠ v) :
    uniformPerm W {ρ | ρ ∈ prefixCell W k.val π ∧ ρ k = v} /
      uniformPerm W {ρ | ρ ∈ prefixCell W k.val π} =
        (1 : ENNReal) / ((W - k.val : ℕ) : ENNReal)
```

This is the exact proposed statement, not an implemented theorem. T711 defines `PermΩ W = Equiv.Perm (Fin W)`, `uniformPerm W = uniformOn univ`, and `prefixCell` by agreement at all `i.val < k` (`PermutationPrefixFiltrationCore.lean`, lines 14–20 and 47–50). Thus `π ∈ C`, and there is no arbitrary inconsistent prescribed prefix. T749's `prefixCell_mem_iff_prefix` verifies that this `< k.val` condition is exactly the `i < k` condition in `hv` (`PermutationPrefixCellFiberBridge.lean`, lines 36–68). The unused-image hypothesis is exact: injectivity makes a used `v` impossible as `ρ k` for every `ρ ∈ C`, so its numerator is zero and the displayed positive right side would be false.

## Boundary and nonvacuity checks

- `W=0`: `Fin 0` has no `k`, so there is no next-image assertion. T711's natural-indexed empty-permutation cell remains nonempty; this is not a zero-mass conditional event.
- `W=1`: necessarily `k=0`, `v=0`, and `π` is unique. The empty-prefix event and next-image event both have mass one; `1/(1-0)=1`.
- `W=2`, `k=0`, `π=id`: T711 makes `C` the two-permutation space; identity and swap are distinct. T749 supplies nonempty fibers at `v=0` and `v=1`; direct enumeration makes each a singleton. Thus each quotient is `1/2`, with a genuinely nonconstant next image.
- `k=W-1` for any `W>0`: exactly one image remains unused, so the proposed quotient is one. `k=W` and later natural-indexed full-information cells have no next coordinate `Fin W` and cannot instantiate the signature.

## Exact scope and corrections to T766

The quotient is the probability of `ρ k=v` **conditioned on the positive event** `C` by elementary division. It is not, by its statement alone, the random variable `μ[1_{ρ k=v} | prefixSigma W k]` or a regular conditional distribution `condDistrib`. To obtain either filtration-level assertion, one must connect the finite cell values to the entire generated sigma algebra and verify the relevant measurability/integral characterization. Identifying the cells as atoms or constructing a prefix observation is one sufficient route; proving the characterization directly over the finite partition is another. T766's categorical wording that the atom/observation theorem is required should be read as a proposed implementation route.

The paper's (2.1) is `ℓ(z)=min(η⁻¹ᐟ²,L)+1`. Its (2.3) bounds Green-function entries by `W^τ/(Wℓη)^{1/2}` with probability `>1-N^{-D}`, and (2.4) bounds partial block traces by `W^τ/(Wℓη)` under Theorem 2.3's spectral and asymptotic conditions. The finite quotient contains none of the paper's `N`, `W`-scale analytic estimates, `ℓ`, `η`, resolvent, or time powers. It supplies no first-cell carrier, shared event, P2 concentration, or local-law/chain closure.
