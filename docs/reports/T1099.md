## Claim and paper-order check

For `L ≥ 3`, let a finite list of edges have `0 ≤ sⱼ ≤ tⱼ < 1` and `‖ξⱼ‖ = 1`. The product uses chronological list order: the later kernel multiplies on the left, so `[e₁,e₂]` denotes `K₂ K₁`. Then every matrix entry of the phase product is bounded in norm by the real entry of the same product with all phases set to `1`; that real entry is nonnegative. The empty product is the identity.

I checked the rendered `paper/250520-YinJun-v2.pdf`, printed p. 53, equations (5.17)–(5.18). Equation (5.17) defines the evolution operator by summing over intermediate/output indices with the product of its edge entries. Each edge is

```text
(1 - s ξ Sᴮ) (1 - t ξ Sᴮ)⁻¹,
```

and (5.18) rewrites it as `I - (s - t) ξ Θᴮ_{tξ} Sᴮ`. The repository's `edgeKer` and `edgeKer_eq` use this same factor and the equivalent `Sᴮ Θᴮ` order; `Theta_commute_SB` verifies the order change. For temporal composition, later operators act on the left, as also recorded by `edgeKer_mul`: `K(u,t) K(s,u) = K(s,t)`. Thus the list convention above matches the evolution-operator composition order. The finite matrix-chain statement is an auxiliary consequence of (5.17)–(5.18), not a theorem asserted verbatim by those equations.

## Mathematical preflight

The exact claim follows from the accepted one-edge results T1081/T1082. For every edge and entries `a,b`, T1081 gives

```text
‖Kξ(a,b)‖ ≤ K₁(a,b).re,       0 ≤ K₁(a,b).re.
```

For one multiplication, expand `(AB)(a,b) = ∑ c, A(a,c) B(c,b)`. The triangle inequality followed by the two entrywise bounds gives

```text
‖(AB)(a,b)‖ ≤ ∑ c, A₁(a,c) B₁(c,b) = (A₁ B₁)(a,b),
```

where the all-one entries `A₁,B₁` are real and nonnegative. Their product entries remain nonnegative because they are sums of products of nonnegative reals. Induction gives the claim for any finite ordered list, including distinct phases and intervals; it imposes no adjacency condition between intervals. The argument is uniform in `a,b`, so diagonal and off-diagonal entries are both covered. For the empty list, both products are the identity, with diagonal entry `1` and off-diagonal entry `0`.

## Nonzero `L = 3`, two-edge witness

Take the chronological edges `(s₁,t₁,ξ₁) = (0,1/4,i)` and `(s₂,t₂,ξ₂) = (1/4,1/2,i)`. On `ZMod 3`, `SB = J/3` and `P := J/3` satisfies `P² = P`. By the edge composition identity, the two-edge product is

```text
Kᵢ(1/4,1/2) Kᵢ(0,1/4) = Kᵢ(0,1/2).
```

The latter is `I + ((-1+2i)/5)P`; its `(0,1)` entry is `(-1+2i)/15`, of norm `√5/15 > 0`. With both phases set to `1`, the product is `K₁(0,1/2) = I + P`, whose `(0,1)` entry is `1/3`. All edge hypotheses hold, and the complex product is nonzero. This is a substantive two-edge check, not the empty-chain case.
