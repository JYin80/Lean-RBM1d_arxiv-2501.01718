## Statement and proof audit

I compared the ticket, the two-arrow preflight, the T1456 reconciliation, accepted T1397,
and manuscript (2.10), (2.29)–(2.33). The PDF's useful bound derived from (2.10) concerns
bulk eigenvectors. T1397 keeps the genuine weighted beta factor, rather than the unweighted
pole sum in the paper's stochastic (2.31). The new theorem controls precisely that factor.

Write `N=L W>0`, `J=[-2+κ/2,2-κ/2]`, `δ=κ/4`,
`P₁=∑β |λβ-z|⁻¹`, and
`Q_y=∑β |λβ-z|⁻¹|uβ(y)|²`. Under `κ>0`, `|E|≤2-κ`,
`|Re z-E|≤δ`, `Im z>0`, and the **bulk-only** bound
`|uβ(y)|²≤D/N` for `λβ∈J`, the source proves
`Q_y≤(D/N)P₁+δ⁻¹`. Its split is sound: outside `J`,
`|λβ-Re z|≥δ`; eigenbasis completeness gives total site mass one.
No estimate on individual tail eigenvectors is used. The pole norms for both Boolean
resolvent signs agree because conjugation preserves `|λβ-z|` for real eigenvalues.

The relative clause defines `R_z²=N⁻¹∑β|λβ-z|²` from the *same* matrix.
Positive imaginary part makes every distance positive and hence `R_z>0`.
Titu's inequality gives `N²≤P₁∑β|λβ-z|`; Cauchy–Schwarz gives
`∑β|λβ-z|≤N R_z`. Thus `P₁/N≥1/R_z`, and the stated
`Q_y≤(D+R_z/δ)P₁/N` follows without a zero-denominator case.
The source's window helper correctly passes from the paper's `C₀/N` window to
`|Re z-E|≤δ` once `C₀/N≤δ`; eventual dimension and fixed-parameter order
remain obligations of a later lift.

Both composition theorems invoke T1397's
`norm_green_spectral_identity_blockM_le` directly. They keep its full nonnegative
alpha factor and apply the additive or relative beta estimate to it. The signs
`σ₁,σ₂` are independent, covering all four `G/G*` combinations. The resulting
prefactor is `N⁻¹ A_y Q_y`, with the relative form giving the deterministic
`N⁻² A_y P₁` scale and explicit multiplier `D+R_z/δ`. This agrees with
the algebraic precursor to (2.31); it does not assert stochastic domination.

## Nondegeneracy and scope

The T1456 reconciliation's band-supported path plus isolated site is a coherent
same-matrix witness. For example, with `W=1` and sufficiently large `L=N`,
put unit off-diagonal entries along the path on sites `0,…,N-2`, zero all edges
to site `N-1`, and put a real diagonal value `3` there. The path eigenvectors
have masses at most `2/N`; its spectrum contains bulk eigenvalues, while the
isolated eigenvalue lies outside `J` for, for example, `κ=1`, `E=0`.
At `y=N-1`, the tail eigenmass is one and its pole term is positive for any
`z=iη`, `η>0`; the same finite matrix has a finite positive `R_z`.
The active centered-profile tail cited in T1456 may coexist with these facts.
This checks deterministic satisfiability, including zero allowed coordinates,
without claiming a positive-probability event at the exact matrix. The
positive-variance Gaussian support argument in the reconciliation supplies
neighborhoods after allowing slack in the mass constant.

The source assumes bulk mass on the matrix at hand and does not construct an
actual Gaussian or OU event carrying this bound and a controlled `R_z` together.
It does not prove the paper's loss-free (2.31), (2.29), (2.30), or the alpha-tail
arrow needed for (2.32)–(2.33). Any later stochastic lift must reconcile the
weak OU `N^{a τ_U}` loss with the final `C τ_U` budget on the same event.
