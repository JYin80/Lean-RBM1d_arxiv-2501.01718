## Scalar identity: PASS

Put `g_u(x)=(√u x−i(1−u))^(-1)`. For real `x` and `0≤u≤1/2`, `|√u x−i(1−u)|²=u x²+(1−u)²≥1/4`; hence there is no pole, including `u=1/2` and `x=±2`. At `u=0`, `g_0(x)=i` pointwise, so the normalized semicircle integral is `i` without dividing by `√u`.

For `u>0`, let `y=(1−u)/√u>0`. The convention `(x−z)^(-1)` gives `∫g_u dρ_sc=(1/√u)m_sc(iy)`. The required **analytic** Stieltjes formula is `m_sc(iy)=i(√(y²+4)−y)/2`. One direct check, avoiding any identification with the repository's algebraic `msc`, uses symmetry and `x=2 sin θ`:

`m_sc(iy)= i y/(2π) ∫_{−2}^{2} √(4−x²)/(x²+y²) dx`,

`∫_{−2}^{2} √(4−x²)/(x²+y²) dx=π(√(y²+4)/y−1)`.

The second formula follows from `∫_0^(π/2)(y²+4 sin²θ)^(-1)dθ=π/(2y√(y²+4))`. Now `√(y²+4)=(1+u)/√u`, so `m_sc(iy)=i√u` and the desired integral is `i`. This verifies the sign and both closed-time endpoints. The paper defines its transform by the density integral; the algebraic equation and positive-imaginary root alone do **not** prove that the repository's `RBM.msc` equals this integral.

## Equal-mass midpoint quadrature: PASS

For `W≥1`, use the semicircle CDF restricted to `[-2,2]`, with `γ_j=F^(-1)(j/W)` and `λ_j=F^(-1)((j+1/2)/W)`. Its positive interior density makes these points well-defined, `γ_0=−2`, `γ_W=2`, and each half-open cell has mass `1/W`; endpoint atoms vanish. The representative is inside its cell. Symmetry gives `λ_(W−1−j)=−λ_j`; odd `W` has central zero, even `W` has opposite pairs. In particular, `W=1` has `λ_0=0`, while `W=2` has two distinct points of opposite sign and two positive-mass cells.

For any real `a,b`, the resolvent identity and the denominator gap give

`|g_u(a)−g_u(b)| ≤ [√u/(1−u)²]|a−b| ≤ 2√2|a−b|`.

The last factor is maximal at `u=1/2`; at `u=0` the difference vanishes. Integrating over every cell, including the two endpoint cells, costs at most `2√2(γ_(j+1)−γ_j)/W`. Their widths telescope to `4`, so

`|(1/W)∑_(j<W) g_u(λ_j)−i| ≤ 8√2/W`.

This is a uniform bound for all positive `W`, independent of the largest cell width. It is nonvacuous: at `W=1,u=1/2`, the single quantile is zero, the quadrature is `2i`, and its error against `i` is `1`. At `u=0` the error is exactly zero for every `W`. The constant need not be sharp.
